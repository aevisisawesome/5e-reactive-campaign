# The Broken March — WP07: Hardcore Encounter Library

**Status:** Canonical campaign content document
**Consumes:** `Campaign design doc.md` (§6, §14, §15, §2.1, §2.2, §23, §29); WP01 §3.3, §9, §12, §14, §15, §16, §17; WP02 §4, §7, §11, §12, §13, §15; WP03 §5, §6, §14; WP04 §5, §6, §7, §8, §11, §13; WP05 §4, §6, §7, §8, §9; WP06 §4, §5, §7–§11, §13–§15
**Feeds:** WP08, WP09, WP10, WP11, WP12 — and, for creature illustration, WP15
**Scope:** Every stat block, hazard, morale rule and encounter entry the campaign needs, plus the eight-field entry format later packages must use for any encounter they add.

This document turns the world WP02–WP06 built into things that happen at a table. It supplies the stat blocks four earlier packages deferred here — the militia at six Defense levels, twenty-two Red-Tusk fighters, nine Laughing Hollow wardens, Threll's cargo guards, the Listener and the Sentinel — and it answers the question WP06 deliberately left open: what is coming through the Broken Quarter. It does **not** decide when any of this appears. Placement against world state is WP08–WP12's work. WP07 supplies the pieces, their weaknesses, and what they do after the party walks away.

|---:|---|---|---|

# 3. How to Read an Encounter

## 3.1 The eight fields

Every entry in §12, §13 and §14 carries these, in this order, without exception. Later packages adding an encounter must use the same eight.

| # | Field | What it must contain |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | **Conventional difficulty** | What a straight fight costs five PCs at the stated level. An observation, never a budget |
| **2** | **Why brute force is costly** | The specific price — a resource, a body, a clock, a relationship, a day |
| **3** | **Telegraphing** | Mapped to design doc §6's levels 1–4, each with a **place** and a **source** |
| **4** | **Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered** | At least one route that is **scout it**, **ask an NPC**, or **watch it**. Never a single check |
| **5** | **Alternative solutions** | **At least two that are not combat.** One non-combat solution is a defect |
| **6** | **Failure consequences** | What changes if the party loses, flees, or never comes. Failure changes the game; it never stops it |
| **7** | **Reward** | What is gained. Often information, access, or a relationship. WP13 owns prices |
| **8** | **World-state impact** | Concrete state changes in WP01 vocabulary — *"Food −1; Red-Tusk Strategic → 3; Z4 hazard → Stage 3"* |

## 3.2 The six types as used here

| Type | Name | WP07's operating rule |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Fair tactical combat | Winnable at the stated level with normal play. **Still has a non-combat route.** Type A means fair, not mandatory |
| **B** | Expensive combat | Winnable, and the cost is written out in field 2 as a named loss, not as "resources" |
| **C** | Puzzle predator | Has **one weakness on a stated axis**, discoverable by at least three routes, one of which is asking someone |
| **D** | Environmental threat | The terrain is the encounter. **May contain no creature at all** — four of ours do not |
| **E** | Social or tactical threat | Combat is possible and strategically disastrous. The entry states exactly what winning costs |
| **F** | Apex threat | Cannot be defeated when first met. The encounter is survival, information, escape, diversion, or sealing |

> **No entry in this library has combat as its only solution, and no entry has exactly one non-combat solution.** Those are the brief's two prohibitions, and §15.2 checks every entry against them.

## 3.3 The signalling contract

Design doc §6 is a promise, not flavour. WP07 discharges it by giving every lethal encounter **all four levels, each with a named place and a named source.**

| Level | Means | Obligation on WP07 |
|---:|---|---|
| **1 — Suspicion** | Environmental cue | Must exist **in a zone the party passes anyway** |
| **2 — Warning** | Evidence of danger | Must be **physical and readable without magic** |
| **3 — Explicit warning** | Direct evidence that conventional combat is extremely dangerous | Must come from **a named NPC or an unmissable object** |
| **4 — Immediate threat** | The party has continued anyway | **The DM is not required to protect them** |

> **Design doc §29's obligation is telegraphing, not mitigation.** Where an entry below is unrecoverable, it is signalled three times and then allowed to happen.

## 3.4 On "conventional difficulty" when the world does not scale

Field 1 states what a fight costs a five-PC party at a stated level. **It describes the encounter. It is not a licence to move it.**

- The encounter exists at that place whether or not the party is ready (design doc §2.1).
- A level 3 party that walks four watches to the Old Delving meets Stage III content. That is the design working, not a mistake.
- **Never adjust an encounter's contents because the party arrived early or late.** The telegraphing is the fairness. Adjust nothing.
- Where an entry says *"level 5–6"*, that is where a fight becomes winnable — not where the encounter starts existing.
- **No CR budgeting.** There is no encounter-building arithmetic anywhere in this document, and later packages must not add any.

## 3.5 Stat block conventions

- Full blocks, printed. No page references, nothing to look up mid-fight.
- **Passive Perception is always printed**, because half these entries turn on who notices whom first.
- **Morale** is a line on every block that has one. A block with no Morale line never checks (§4.1).
- **Attitude** lines use WP01 §4.6 and WP03 §15's four values, and state the creature's *starting* attitude only.
- Where a block belongs to a named NPC, WP03's format governs the person and WP07 adds only the numbers. **There is one NPC standard in this campaign.**
- Damage is written out in full. Nothing here requires a spell the party may not have.
- **Group sizes are fixed by the world, not by the party.** Where a number appears — eleven militia, three scouts, four guards — it comes from WP02, WP04 or WP05 and must not be adjusted.

# 4. Morale, Retreat and Memory

Design doc §15: enemies behave according to goals, not suicidal combat logic. Survivors remember.

## 4.1 Who checks morale, and who never does

| Checks | Never checks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Red-Tusk scouts, fighters, spear-line | **The Sentinel** | A machine executing an instruction (WP06 §11.2) |
| Greybank militia, quarry crew | **The Moorgaunt while feeding** | It will die over a cache (§9.3) |
| Threll's cargo guards | **Laughing Hollow wardens** | They do not break. They **withdraw on schedule** (§7.3) |
| Village dogs | The reed-leeches | No mind to break |
| **Ordga Brune**, from round 4 | **Berrick Hone** — see §5.3 | His is a specific, deliberately uncertain case |
| The Moorgaunt when *not* feeding | The Listener below half HP — it submerges instead | Submerging is not a morale result; it is what it does |

## 4.2 The procedure

**Morale uses WP01 §17.1's d6. No new die, no new track.**

When a trigger fires, roll **1d6 for the group**:

| d6 | Result |
|---:|---|
| **1** | **Rout.** They drop what they are carrying and run. Pursuit is a chase, not a fight |
| **2** | **Break.** They withdraw in order, covering each other, taking wounded if they can |
| **3–4** | **Waver.** They fall back to their nearest advantage — cover, a doorway, the wagon — and fight from it. **They break on the next trigger without a roll** |
| **5** | **Hold.** They keep fighting, and change tactics |
| **6** | **Harden.** They keep fighting and do something the party will not like — fire, hostages, or the objective instead of the people |

**Roll once per group per trigger.** Never per creature. Never twice for the same trigger.

## 4.3 Triggers

| Trigger | Applies to |
|---|---|
| **Leader killed or down** | Red-Tusk (Ghesh), militia (Berrick), guards (the captain) |
| **Half the group incapacitated** | Every group that checks |
| **Objective lost** — cargo secured, flock driven off, wall held | Guards, raiders, militia |
| **Overwhelming magic displayed** | Militia and guards **waver automatically, no roll.** Red-Tusk roll. Wardens do not care |
| **Escape route opened** | All. A group given a way out takes it |
| **Something the group has never seen before** | Militia only. Greybank has fought nothing since the wolf winter, eleven years ago (WP02 §12.1) |

> **The most useful trigger in the campaign is the fifth.** A party that leaves a door open ends most fights in this library in two rounds. A party that surrounds its enemy has chosen a longer fight, and the first thing that fights to the death should tell them so.

## 4.4 Retreat behaviour by group

| Group | How they leave | What they take | Where they go |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Red-Tusk scouts** | Separately, uphill and north, using Nimble Escape | Nothing. They drop the sheep | Z7, then Z11, then Z16. Two watches |
| **Red-Tusk raiders (W4)** | In order, covering, carrying wounded | **The food.** Always the food | Z16. Back inside three nights |
| **Greybank militia** | Badly, individually, toward their own houses | Nothing | Home, and barred doors. **Defense −1 if three or more are killed** |
| **Quarry crew** | Together, up the haul road | Their tools | The quarry. They do not come back down |
| **Cargo guards** | Around the wagon, then with it | **The wagon, or nothing** | South, down the road toward Daggerford |
| **Wardens** | On schedule, into cover, in silence | Whatever they came for | Z9, then Z15. Untrackable |
| **The Moorgaunt** | At speed, dragging if it can | **A body, living or dead** | Its nearest cache (§9.3) |
| **The Listener** | Into the water | Whatever it has grappled | Deeper. It does not leave S8 |
| **The Sentinel** | It does not retreat | — | It resumes its circuit where it left it |

## 4.5 What escaped enemies do next session

**Explicitly owed by the brief.** Survivors remember, and the memory is specific.

| Who escaped | What happens next session |
|---|---|
| **One Red-Tusk scout** | Ghesh now knows the party's number, their reach, and whether they have magic. **He counted.** The next probe avoids whatever hurt him — fire means the next approach is upwind and at distance |
| **All three scouts** | The band treats the party as the village's teeth. The clock advances on schedule regardless (WP04 §6.6), and the party is now a named factor in WP04 §8.3's pressure count: **War +1** |
| **Raiders driven off the fold** | They raid **somewhere the party is not** — the outlying farmsteads or the charcoal camp (WP04 §6.4). The bell reaches neither (WP02 §12.4) |
| **Ghesh, wounded and away** | He never challenges the party in the open again. He waits until they are inside the ring and hits the fold. **He does not repeat a mistake** |
| **Militia the party fought and beat** | **Greybank Reputation −1** minimum, and **Berrick Hone drops one attitude step permanently.** The next meeting with the village is §5.5, not a fight |
| **A warden the party attacked** | No retaliation in kind. **Laughing Hollow Intervention +1**, and within two days something the party relies on is quietly gone — a boat, a cache, a rope, a route (WP04 §5.3, stage 4) |
| **Cargo guards who lost the wagon** | Threll sends no more guards. He sends a **claim, in writing, two days later** (WP04 §7.2) |
| **The Moorgaunt, driven off alive** | It abandons that cache and makes a new one **closer to easier food** — which means closer to Greybank. **Its next kill is one zone further in** |
| **The Listener, hurt and withdrawn** | It stays in S8 and becomes *more* careful, not less: it stops taking the stair and waits at the waterline. **The dry route becomes safe and the wet route becomes the killing ground — inverted from last time** |
| **The Sentinel, evaded** | Nothing. It does not learn, hunt or remember. **It resumes the circuit.** This is the only row where escaping costs nothing, and it is exactly why players mistake it for beatable |

> **Write the survivor's name down.** WP01 §18.3's end-of-session update has a line for it. A goblin the party let run in session 1 who leads the fold raid in session 3 is the cheapest continuity this campaign has.

## 4.6 Prisoners and surrender

- **Anything that checks morale can be captured.** A group that rolls 2 and is cut off surrenders instead of withdrawing.
- **Red-Tusk prisoners are worth more than they look.** A live scout is the fastest route to Orbal, and returning one unharmed is **Red-Tusk Rep +1** and **Compact +1** in WP04 §8.3's count.
- **Killing a prisoner taken after surrender** is a serious crime under WP01 §7.1 if a villager sees it, and **War +2** if a goblin does.
- **Wardens neither surrender nor take prisoners.** They leave.
- **Greybank takes prisoners badly.** There is no gaol. The Moot Hall store room has a bar on the outside of the door and that is the whole arrangement. Anyone held there past three days becomes a village argument: **Unity −1**.

# 5. Greybank's Militia — the Six Defense Levels

**Owed to WP01 §3.3 and WP02 §12.1, which both defer stat blocks to WP07.** WP02 supplied the manpower, the muster times, the equipment list and the positions. WP07 supplies the numbers on the page and nothing else.

> **WP02 §12.3's turnout figures and muster times are fixed and are not restated here as decisions.** They are quoted so the DM does not need two documents open.

## 5.1 What the militia actually is

Eighteen names on a list in the Moot Hall, drawn from eighty-five able adults. Farmers, quarrymen and carters with billhooks. They drill four times a year. **Three of them have killed anything, all of them in the wolf winter, eleven years ago** (WP02 §12.1).

The equipment in the militia shed (G12) is the hard constraint on everything below:

| In G12 | Qty |
|---|---:|
| Billhooks | 14 |
| Spears | 9 |
| Poor shortbows | 6 |
| Shield | 1 |
| Large ammunition bundles | 2 |
| Horn | 1 |
| Pitch barrel | 1 |

**Only two people in Greybank can shoot** — Berrick Hone and Nissa Crake (WP03 §14.8). The other four bows are ballast. **Nobody has armour except Berrick's leather jack.**

## 5.2 Which block to use

**Defense level sets numbers, positions and preparation. It does not set competence.**

| Condition | Block used |
|---|---|
| **Militia training project not completed** (WP02 §15) | **Greybank Militiaman** — the default, at every Defense level |
| **Militia training project completed** | **Drilled Militiaman** — replaces the above everywhere |
| Armed civilians turning out at Defense 5–6 | **Armed Villager** |
| Quarry crew fighting for the quarry | **Quarry Hand** |

> A village at Defense 5 that never trained anybody is still eighteen frightened farmers behind a good wall. **Defense buys walls, warning and turnout. Only the training project buys competence.**

# 9. The Moorgaunt

**The answer to WP06 §11.3 — what is currently coming through the Broken Quarter (S16).**

## 9.1 What it is

**[CAMPAIGN]** · **[ADAPTED note]** — the Moorgaunt occupies the ecological place a large upland ambush predator would occupy in Realms highland country. It is **not** a published monster and a DM should import no assumptions about one.

A long-limbed quadruped the colour of wet limestone, built for scree and cold, and **visibly starving**. Its ribs are the memorable thing; a party that sees it in daylight describes it afterward as *thin*, not as *huge*. It is neither ancient nor magical nor connected to the Illefarn. **It is an animal that ran out of country.**

**Names for it:**

| Who | Calls it |
|---|---|
| The Red-Tusk | **the Thin Dog.** They have watched it on the moor for two seasons and they avoid it |
| Bessa Ruddock, after the first kill | *"the lean thing"* — she has seen the drag marks, not the animal |
| **The S6 records** | **Nothing. It is not in them, and that absence is information:** this is not part of the site |

## 9.2 Why it is here — and why it is the Red-Tusk's fault

WP05 §12 fixed that **Kettlerock's ground is stripped**, and made that the physical evidence for why the Red-Tusk clock advances on its own. Sixty-one goblins have eaten the moor's game to nothing across two seasons.

**The Moorgaunt's range collapsed with it.** It came south and west looking for food, found the failed spans at the top of the Broken Quarter, and came down the hole because cold air and the smell of old bone came up it. It has been working inward and outward ever since.

> **One fact causes two problems.** The stripped moor moves the Red-Tusk clock *and* puts a predator on Greybank's doorstep, and neither is anyone's plan. There is no mastermind here (design doc §7) and no coincidence — just a shortage with two consequences.

**This makes the encounter reactive in a way the party can feel:**

| Party action | Effect on the Moorgaunt |
|---|---|
| **Compact branch — Greybank feeds the Red-Tusk (C4)** | The goblins stop stripping the moor. **The game does not recover within four sessions.** The Moorgaunt keeps hunting south. *Harsh, and honest: the fix is real and slow* |
| **Flight branch — the Red-Tusk leave** | The moor begins to recover, and the Moorgaunt's pressure eases from session 4. **But S12 breaches and the Sentinel walks out** (WP06 §15) — and §9.5 is what happens to the Moorgaunt then |
| **War branch — the band is destroyed** | Same as Flight, one session later, and with the fold already burned |
| **The party kills the Moorgaunt** | The Z4 and Z11 hazards drop. **The hole in the fence is still open** (§9.6) |
| **The party closes the fence** — System 1 or System 7 | Nothing more comes through. The Moorgaunt, if alive, is trapped on whichever side it was on |

## 9.3 The block

### THE MOORGAUNT

*Large beast · unaligned · [CAMPAIGN]*

**Armour Class** 14 (natural armour)
**Hit Points** 76 (9d10+27)
**Speed** 40 ft., climb 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18 (+4) | 15 (+2) | 16 (+3) | 3 (−4) | 13 (+1) | 6 (−2) |

**Skills** Perception +5, Stealth +6, Survival +5
**Senses** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 15
**Languages** —

**Keen Smell.** Advantage on Perception checks relying on smell. **It finds the fold from a mile upwind.**

**Stone-Footed.** Ignores difficult terrain from scree, rubble, spoil or broken masonry, and has advantage on Stealth checks on any of them. **It is silent on exactly the ground a party is loudest on.**

> **Caches.** The Moorgaunt kills, drags the body to a cache, and returns to it across **three nights** until it is gone. **It will not hunt while a cache holds meat.** This is why the fold loses one animal, then nothing, then another three nights later — and it is the single most useful thing about it.

> **Starving.** It breaks off any fight to take a body — living, dying or dead — to a cache. **If any creature within 30 ft. is at 0 hit points or is a fresh corpse, the Moorgaunt's next action is to grab it and leave.** This is not mercy and it will not feel like mercy.

> **Vulnerable While Feeding.** While at a cache with its head down, its passive Perception is **10**, it cannot Hide, and **the first attack made against it has advantage.** It also will not flee: it fights over the cache and **does not check morale** (§4.1).

If the target is Medium or smaller, it must succeed on a DC 15 Strength saving throw or be **grappled** (escape DC 15). **While grappling, the Moorgaunt can move at its full speed, dragging the grappled creature with it.**

**Claws.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 11 (2d6+4) slashing damage.

***Bonus Actions***

**Take It and Go.** If grappling a creature, the Moorgaunt may Disengage.

**Morale.** Checks when **not** feeding, on §4.3's triggers, and leaves at speed with whatever it is holding.

## 9.4 The weakness, and the five ways to find it

> **It is predictable in time and in place, and it will not leave a cache.** Everything the party can do to it follows from those two facts.

| # | Route | What they learn | Requires |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Ask Bessa Ruddock** | She keeps a tally of the flock by habit. **One gone, then three quiet nights, then one gone.** The rhythm is on a slate in the byre | A conversation. WP03 §6.7's person, free |
| **2** | **Watch the fold for a night** | It does not come. Two nights later it does. **Three watches of nothing is the discovery** | Patience, and a watch of time |
| **3** | **Find a cache** | A rock cleft on Z11, or the rubble slope in S16 — bones stacked dry, out of the weather, part-eaten and returned to | Following the drag trail. It is not subtle |
| **4** | **Ask Ghesh or Orbal** | *"The thin dog. It takes and it goes away. Then it comes back to the same place."* The Red-Tusk have lived beside it for two seasons and simply avoid it | Red-Tusk Rep ≥ 0, or a captured scout (§4.6) |
| **5** | **Watch it at S10's mouth** | It comes to the gallery's mouth, stops, and turns. Every time. **A party that has seen the groove knows why** | Being in Stage III, and looking |

**None of these is a check.** Four of them are asking or watching. The fifth is following a drag trail through scree, which anyone can do.

## 9.5 What changes it

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| **A cache is emptied or burned** | It hunts **that night**, and it hunts closer in than last time |
| **It is driven off but lives** | It abandons that cache and makes a new one nearer easier food. **Its next kill is one zone further in** (§4.5) |
| **It is fed deliberately** | It caches what it is given and does not hunt for three nights. **This buys the party three nights and nothing else** |
| **It is led** | It follows meat. A party willing to drag a carcass can move it — including onto Hagsfoot, into the Cut, or down into S16 |
| **The Sentinel is removed or corrected** | **The groove stops meaning anything.** It goes inward. **Stage III and, if S5 is breached, Stage I are open to it** |
| **The Red-Tusk leave (F6)** | The moor recovers slowly. Its pressure eases from session 4 — **and S12 breaches, and it now has a second door** |
| **The fence is closed (System 1 or 7)** | It is trapped on whichever side of the line it was on when the span lit |

## 9.6 Killing it does not fix it

**The hole is the problem. The animal is a symptom.**

If the Moorgaunt dies, S16 is still breached, three spans are still dead, and Hagsfoot Moor is still stripped. **Something else comes down the hole within a season** — which is longer than this campaign, and so it is not an encounter. It is what the DM tells the players when they ask whether they have finished.

> **Say it out loud at the table.** *"The hole is still there."* That sentence is what makes System 1 and System 7 mean something in session 4.

## 9.7 For WP15

**The one memorable physical fact: you can count its ribs from thirty feet.** Not huge, not monstrous — *thin*, and moving well anyway.

# 11. The Sentinel

**Owed to WP06 §11.2, under one constraint: there is no attack sequence available at levels 3–6 that produces a dead Sentinel.**

## 11.1 How to stat a thing that cannot be killed

**By statting what it ignores.** The block below has no printed hit points, and that is deliberate (§1.3, decision 8): a printed total invites the table to do arithmetic and conclude that four sessions of damage would do it. **They would not.**

The Sentinel is a machine executing an intact instruction — *turn back what crosses the line* — with a corrupted map. It is **not intelligent, not evil, not negotiable, and not killable.** Everything below follows from that.

## 11.2 The block

### THE SENTINEL

*Large construct · unaligned · [CAMPAIGN, on ADAPTED Illefarn foundations]*

**Armour Class** 20 (jointless stone)
**Hit Points** **not tracked — see Threshold of Consequence**
**Speed** 30 ft. **It never moves faster and it never moves slower.**

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 22 (+6) | 9 (−1) | 22 (+6) | 1 (−5) | 11 (+0) | 1 (−5) |

**Damage Immunities** poison, psychic, necrotic, fire, cold, lightning, thunder, acid; bludgeoning, piercing and slashing from nonmagical attacks
**Condition Immunities** blinded, charmed, deafened, exhaustion, frightened, grappled, incapacitated, paralysed, petrified, poisoned, prone, restrained, stunned, unconscious
**Senses** tremorsense 120 ft., **presence-sense 120 ft.** (it perceives that a creature is within the line; sight, invisibility, illusion, darkness and disguise are all irrelevant), passive Perception 10
**Languages** understands the cut names of the Marrowward. **It does not converse and cannot be reasoned with.**

> **Threshold of Consequence.** Damage from a single source of **less than 25** leaves no mark and has no effect of any kind. Damage of 25 or more chips it, visibly and permanently, and **does nothing else.** There is no total. There is no bloodied. **A party cannot damage it into stopping.**

**Immutable.** Cannot be polymorphed, banished, teleported, put to sleep, turned, commanded, dominated, or reduced in speed. Effects that would move it against its will do not.

**Walks the Line.** It moves at 30 ft. and takes the Dash action never. It does not open doors it was not built to open; it walks the circuit, and where the circuit is blocked, it walks the other way round. **It is never in a hurry, and it never stops.**

**Authorisation Register.** A creature carrying valid authorisation (WP06 System 6, *claimed* or *corrected*) is ignored entirely — not attacked, not approached, not acknowledged. **A creature without it, inside the line, is an incursion.**

**Does Not Pursue Past the Perimeter.** It will not step outside the ward line. Up the ramp to Z2, or out through S16 onto Z11, and it stops and turns. **The Watch-Glass shows exactly where that boundary is, at level 3, for free** (WP06 §7).

***The three postures***

The Sentinel is always in exactly one, and it escalates in order. **It never de-escalates within one encounter.**

| Posture | Trigger | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Warning** | First contact with an unauthorised creature in a sector | Approaches at walking pace. **Stops at 30 ft.** and stands. One full round of grace. **This is design doc §6's Level 4, arriving in person** |
| **2 — Removal** | The creature is still inside the line at the end of that round | Advances and strikes to push. Not to kill — but it does not measure |
| **3 — Suppression** | Any damage is dealt to it, **or** a creature has been struck twice and is still inside the line | Multiattack, and it does not stop. **This kills level 3–6 characters in two rounds** |

***Actions***

**Turn Back** *(posture 2)*. *Melee Weapon Attack:* +9 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 17 (2d10+6) bludgeoning damage, and the target is pushed 15 ft. **toward the nearest point outside the line.** A creature pushed against a wall takes an extra 5 (1d10) bludgeoning damage.

**Suppress** *(posture 3)*. Multiattack: two attacks, *+9 to hit*, reach 10 ft. *Hit:* 22 (3d10+6) bludgeoning damage each.

**Morale.** None. It does not check, cannot be intimidated, and has no concept of losing.

## 11.8 For WP15

**The one memorable physical fact: it has no face and no front.** The circuit runs both ways and it is built to walk either. On the DM copy only, its circuit is drawn through S10 and S14, and at Instability 3+ up and out to Z7.

## 12.1 E01 · The Fold — Z4

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** §9.4's five routes. At the fold specifically: **the slate**. Bessa's tally is the rhythm, and the rhythm is the animal. *Ask an NPC* and *watch it* both work; neither is a check.

**5 · Alternative solutions.** At least four, three of them without a fight:
- **Move the flock.** Bessa will not do it alone — the western grazing is where the grass is. A party that helps her move it inside the ring for a tenday costs the village nothing and ends the predation. **Bessa's attitude +1.**

If they do not go, the character is dead.

## 12.5 E05 · The Cut — Z8

**Type E · level 4–5 · WP05 §6, §7.3, §8 · the region's best defensive terrain, one watch from Greybank**

A road defile with banked sides, on the Daggerford road. **Ambush ground at Stage 1 from day one**, and **Stage 4 — Response** the moment the Red-Tusk reach W5, when interdiction closes it (WP05 §7.3).

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** Six Red-Tusk holding the banks against a party in the road: **the party loses the exchange and wins the fight, expensively.** Two Red-Tusk Bow above and four Spear at the neck is genuinely dangerous at level 4 and survivable at level 5.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** **The Cut is not the objective. The road is.** Clearing the defile once does nothing: it is a mile of banked road one watch from the village, and the party cannot hold it and be in Greybank at the same time. WP05 says so plainly — *holding it means not being in the village.* **Trade −1 stands while the road is unsafe**, no matter how many goblins die in it.

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | Z8, any passage | The banks are cut and old, and a carter naturally goes quiet through it. **Gedd Plank does** |
| **2** | Greybank | **Messengers to Daggerford stop arriving** (WP04 §6.4, W5) |
| **3** | Greybank | **Berrick Hone says the village is being isolated before it is attacked, and he is right** |
| **4** | Z8 | The cart ahead has stopped and the driver is not on it |

**4 · Discoverable weakness — the encounter's.** **The Red-Tusk are interdicting, not ambushing.** They want the road closed, not the party dead. Discovered by *scouting the defile from above* (the banks are walkable), by *asking Gedd Plank* what a stopped cart means, or by *watching* — they let the first cart through and stop the second. A party that understands this can pass by being obviously not-cargo.

**5 · Alternative solutions.**
- **Escort a cart and be seen to be escorting it.** They withdraw. They are not looking for a fight with five armed people over one wagon.
- **Use Bythe Ford** — the detour, +4 watches, and it is also closed at W5 (WP05 §7.3). **Both closures are the point: the party must break the interdiction politically, not tactically.**
- **Reach Orbal.** At Red-Tusk Rep ≥ +1, the interdiction lifts for the party specifically, and only for them.
- **Take the far bank.** W5 does not close Z9 or Z15 — the wardens' country stays open, and **that gap is deliberate** (WP05 §7.3). It is the seam a clever party finds.

**6 · Failure consequences.** **Trade −1 while the Cut is closed.** Threll prices protection rather than providing it (WP05 §7.3). If the party fights and loses, the road stays closed and Greybank now knows the party cannot open it.

**7 · Reward.** The road. And the discovery that the Red-Tusk **will not enter elven country**, which is the largest single piece of strategic information in WP05.

**8 · World-state impact.**

| Outcome | State change |
|---|---|
| Interdiction broken by force | **Red-Tusk fighters dead — and they cannot be replaced.** War +1. The road reopens for as long as the party stands in it |
| Interdiction lifted politically | **Trade returns. Red-Tusk Rep +1** |
| Ignored | **Trade −1** stands. Daggerford Commercial advances as shipments fail |
| The party uses the far bank instead | Nothing changes for Greybank, and **the party has a route the village does not** |

## 12.6 E06 · The Rim of Kettlerock — Z16

**Type E · any level · WP05 §9 discovery 7, §15.2 · the campaign's most important piece of scouting**

**This entry contains no combat and no stat block, and it is in this library on purpose.**

A party that walks three watches north and looks down into Kettlerock before deciding anything sees **sixty-one goblins, of whom thirty-nine cannot fight and eleven are wounded.** It sees stripped ground. It sees a camp, not a warband.

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** Twenty-two fighters and thirty-nine non-combatants. **There is no version of this that is a fight the party should have**, and the entry states so rather than pricing it.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** Attacking Kettlerock is attacking a refugee camp, in front of the party's own eyes. **Red-Tusk Rep → −3, War locks, and if a villager ever finds out, Greybank Reputation −1 and Unity −1** — Greybank has children of its own and the parallel will not be lost on anybody.

**3 · Telegraphing.** The rim itself is levels 1–3 in one look: cold fires, stripped ground, and people carrying other people.

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **The band's weakness is the thirty-nine, and it is visible from the rim.** *Scout it.* That is the whole mechanism, and WP05 §15.2 already priced it: a day and a half, in exchange for the Compact argument for free.

**5 · Alternative solutions.**

- **Bring Bessa Ruddock.** She has lost four sheep to these people and she will still come, and what she says when she sees the eleven wounded is the campaign's best free scene.

**6 · Failure consequences.** A party that never scouts meets the envoy blind and decides a branch of the campaign on no information (WP04 §8.2).

**7 · Reward.** The Compact argument. **What the Red-Tusk saw at the ruins' outer reaches** (WP04 §6.5, at Rep +2) — which is the party's second-best source on the Marrowward after the site itself. And Orbal, who holds a name the Approach of Names accepts (WP06 §5.3).

## 12.10 E10 · The Assault on the Ring — Z1, W6 only

**Type B and Type E together · level 5–6 · WP04 §6.4, WP02 §12 · only if the War branch reached stage 6**

Twenty-two fighters against WP02 §12.3's manpower, at the gap they have watched longest. **§6.4 gives the outcome by Defense level; this entry gives the shape of the night.**

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** The party is not the deciding factor and should be told so beforehand. **Five PCs cannot hold four ring gaps.** Their real decision is which one they stand in, and what they give up by standing there.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** Every militiaman who dies is **Defense −1 per three**, permanently, and every one of them has a name in WP03. **The party cannot be everywhere**, and the gap they leave is the gap that opens.

**3 · Telegraphing.** Two full sessions of it: W4's raids, W5's watched roads and stopped messengers, and Berrick Hone saying out loud that the village is being isolated before it is attacked.

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **Ghesh calls the withdrawal at six down** (§6.2, Counts the Cost) — and he counts his own losses, not the village's. Discovered by *watching him* in any earlier engagement, or by *asking a captured scout*, or by *scouting Kettlerock* and understanding that a band with thirty-nine dependents cannot spend fighters. **Concentrating the defence so that six goblins fall in one place ends the assault**, and that is a tactical problem with a knowable answer.

**5 · Alternative solutions.**
- **Close the ring first.** WP02 §15.1's palisade project, six days and a lot of consent, and it moves this entry from catastrophe to costly repulse.
- **Evacuate.** South over the bridge, two days on foot with children (WP02 §12.5). **Greybank Abandoned is a real ending** (design doc §24) and it is not a loss condition.
- **Give them the western grazing anyway**, even at W6. Ghesh will not take it — but **Orbal will make him consider it in front of everyone**, and that is a scene, not a die roll.
- **Take the fight to Kettlerock while the band is out.** Vile, effective, and it puts thirty-nine non-combatants in front of the party.

**6 · Failure consequences.** §6.4's table. At Defense 2 it is a catastrophe with named dead. **Use WP03's grief map**: the seven permanent-Hostile deaths are all available here, and two of them are children.

**7 · Reward.** Survival, and design doc §24's Greybank Fortified if Defense is 4+ and the ring held.

**8 · World-state impact.** Per §6.4. Plus: **Unity +1 for a public success**, **Unity −1 if three or more cotts burn** (WP02 §13), and **Defense −1 per three militia killed.**

## 13.1 E11 · Stage I — The Generous Stage — S1–S4

**Type D, and informational · level 3 · WP06 §7**

> **WP07's ruling, stated plainly: there is no creature in Stage I, and WP07 declines to add one.**

WP06 §7 promises that nothing in Stage I can kill a careful level 3 party. The literal reading is the strong one. Stage I's dangers are **a forty-foot ramp in the dark, a shutter that breaks permanently, and an anchor that answers if it is forced** — and every one of them is a decision rather than an ambush.

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** None. **A careful party takes no damage in Stage I.** A careless one takes some, and every source of it is announced first.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** The only things here that can be broken are the S4 shutter and the S3 anchor, and **both breakages are permanent** (WP06 §13).

| The temptation | The price |
|---|---|
| **Prise the warped S4 shutter** rather than find the key in S2 | **S4 → Breached, permanently.** The Watch Chamber can never be secured, and anything in the site can reach the Watch-Glass |
| **Force the S3 anchor** | **Instability +1**, and **2d6 force damage to every creature in the room** (§1.3, decision 6). The recess warms under the hand first — the warning is tactile and unmissable |
| **Take a hammer to a waystone** — three of which are in the village | **That span cannot be re-anchored, ever.** A second one puts the good ending permanently out of reach (WP06 §13). **WP08 must have somebody say the stones are old and should be left alone, before anyone is holding a hammer** |

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | S1 | **The air moves.** It has been moving for two thousand years |
| **2** | S2 | The roster's last third is cut in a different, hurried hand |
| **3** | S4 | **The Watch-Glass: six anchors, three of them dark, and the dark ones face north-west.** The whole problem, in one image, at level 3, for free |
| **4** | — | **There is no Level 4 in Stage I. That is the point** |

**4 · Discoverable weakness — of the site, not a creature.** The Watch-Glass **shows where the ward line runs**, which is Sentinel solution 1 (§11.5) handed to a level 3 party in session 1. A party that looks into the basin and asks what the rings mean has been given the answer to the campaign's apex threat before it has met it.

**5 · Alternative solutions.** The stage has no problem to solve, so the alternatives are routes: the S2 key opens S4 without breaking it; the overflow shaft from the quarry's lowest bench bypasses S5 entirely; and **the ramp cannot be closed from inside** — this stage is not a trap (WP06 §7).

**6 · Failure consequences.** A party that breaks both S4 and the anchor has spent Instability +1 and a permanent breach on forty minutes underground, and **the elves notice the second one** (WP04 §5.3 advancement trigger).

**7 · Reward.** **The Watch-Glass** (WP13's item), a resonance key, the S2 roster (WP14's handout), and the fact that the tower Greybank has stood under for ninety years is made of the same stone as this room.

**8 · World-state impact.**

| Outcome | State change |
|---|---|
| Entered and explored, nothing forced | **S1–S3 → Discovered. Instability unchanged. Laughing Hollow Intervention → 2** — the aperture has been opened |
| S4 opened with the key | **S4 → Discovered.** System 2 → Identified |
| S4 shutter prised | **S4 → Breached, permanently** |

> **The fold gets raided while they are down here.** WP06 §17.1 and the WP08 handoff both say so, and it is the campaign's first lesson that a watch spent is a watch spent.

## 13.2 E12 · The Lock Gallery — S5

**Type D · level 4 · WP06 §5.1, §8, §13 · the campaign's cleanest irreversible choice**

A hundred feet of corridor with a counterweighted stone leaf across the middle. **Three routes through it, and one of them is permanent.**

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** No creature, no fight, no check. **The encounter is entirely a decision.**

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** Breaking the counterweight housing works, immediately, and **makes S5 Breached forever.** Stage I and Stage II become one space permanently, **the Sentinel's circuit can eventually extend into Stage I** (WP06 §13), and Stage I stops being the safe place the campaign promised it was. The elves notice.

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | S5 | The housing is exposed, and obviously exposed, because it was built to be maintained |
| **2** | S2, S6 | Two other barriers in this site work the same way, and neither has been broken |
| **3** | S6 records, or Vaerelin | **The records describe the leaves as part of the line.** Vaerelin, if asked, says one sentence about doors |
| **4** | — | There is no immediate threat here. **The threat is three sessions away and the party will not connect it** |

**4 · Discoverable weakness — of the barrier.** **A key opens it, and there is one in S2**, twenty minutes' walk back. Discovered by *reading the S2 roster wall*, by *searching the warden's kit under the bench*, or by *asking* — the S6 records say which spans the keys are tuned to (WP06 §5.2).

**5 · Alternative solutions.** The key. **The overflow shaft** from the quarry's lowest bench, which bypasses S5 entirely, is wet and awkward and free (WP06 §5.1). Both are non-combat and neither is a check.

**6 · Failure consequences.** There is no failure state here — only a choice with a cost. **That is the entry's purpose.** A party in a hurry breaks it, and pays in session 4.

**7 · Reward.** Stage II.

**8 · World-state impact.**

## 13.3 E13 · The Drowned Stair — S8

**Type C · level 4–5 · WP06 §8, §11.1 · the Listener**

Eleven bodies on the dry steps. **Not one in the water.**

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** As a fight, **Type B and expensive** at level 4–5: 97 hit points, reach 15 ft, a grapple that drowns people, and it can be neither blinded nor deafened because it is already both. As a crossing, **free** — once the party has understood the room.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** It grapples and drags to deep water. **The party will lose the character it cannot reach.** And killing it costs **Instability +1** and opens the channels (§10.5) — the water system was the last thing keeping anything else out of the galleries.

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | S7 | The race is loud, and the noise is a fact about this whole stage |
| **2** | S8 | **Eleven bodies. Count them** |
| **3** | S6, or Ghesh, or Orbal | Described by function in a maintenance register; goblins died on the stairs and never in the channels |
| **4** | S8 | Something moves in the water at the bottom of the stair |

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **Running water hides you.** §10.3's five routes, of which *count the bodies*, *ask a goblin* and *throw a stone* are all free and none is a check. **The water is the safe place and the dry stair is the killing ground — precisely inverted from what any party will assume**, and the room hands the inversion to anyone who looks down.

**5 · Alternative solutions.** §10.4: stand in the channel and wait; cross in the channel; drain it with System 3 and stop Greybank's mill; drive it with System 5; feed it. **Five, four of them non-combat.**

**6 · Failure consequences.** A drowning. And a party that retreats without understanding the room comes back to a Listener that has changed its habits — **it waits at the waterline now, and the dry route is the safe one** (§4.5).

**7 · Reward.** The wet route bypasses the S9 barrier entirely (WP06 §5.1) — Stage III without a key.

**8 · World-state impact.**

| Outcome | State change |
|---|---|
| Crossed in the water | **S8 → Accessible.** Nothing moves |
| Drained with System 3 | **S8 → Breached, permanently. Greybank's mill stops. The shoal opens permanently. Bythe Ford goes ankle-deep** (WP06 §14). **Instability +1 forced** |
| Driven off with System 5 | **Instability −1** run correctly, +1 partial. **Red-Tusk Rep +1, Compact +1.** Wilderness threat near Greybank −1 stage while active |
| **Killed** | **Instability +1.** Lower galleries safe. **Z6 → Stage 2 within a tenday** (§10.5) |
| Ignored | It stays in S8 and never roams upward. **The party loses the wet bypass** |

## 13.4 E14 · The Long Gallery — S10

**Type F · level 4–6 · WP06 §9 · the longest sightline in the campaign, and nothing in it to hide behind**

Dead straight, level, twelve feet wide, running the whole spine of the line. **At its midpoint the floor is worn into a groove an inch deep by footsteps.**

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** **There is no difficulty rating for the Sentinel and this document will not print one.** A party that commits to a stand-up fight in this gallery should die in it, and the campaign will have told them four times before they do (WP06 §11.2).

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** §11.3 is the whole answer. Nothing the party can do at these levels affects it. **Damage below 25 leaves no mark; damage above 25 chips a wall.**

**3 · Telegraphing.** §11.4, all four levels. The groove is Level 3 and it is one of two objects in the campaign that discharge the fairness contract on their own — the other is the pile at Z17.

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.**

**Not a weakness — a schedule and a boundary.**

| Route | What it gives |
|---|---|
| **Watch it** | It walks. Always at 30 ft. Always the same circuit. **A party that spends one watch observing from S11's mouth knows where it will be** |
| **The Watch-Glass (S4)** | **Where the line runs** — and therefore where it stops. Free, at level 3 |
| **The S6 records** | Its function, its instruction, and that the register can be amended |
| **Ask Ghesh** | *"It did not hurry."* Two words that are worth a session of caution |
| **Watch the Moorgaunt** | It comes to the gallery mouth, stops, and turns. **Every time** (§9.4, route 5) |

**5 · Alternative solutions.** §11.5's four, plus the gallery-specific one: **S11's mouth is off the Long Gallery and the Sentinel passes it without entering.** A party can stand in the Deterrent Chain and let it go by. That is not a trick; it is the circuit.

**6 · Failure consequences.** Death, and it is a legitimate death. **Design doc §6 Level 4 is explicit: at this point the DM is not required to protect them.** A PC killed here is replaced from WP03's roster and WP02's empty cott H22.

**7 · Reward.** Reaching S11, S12 and S13. And the understanding — usually arrived at while running — that **the thing cannot be beaten and must be answered.**

**8 · World-state impact.**

| Outcome | State change |
|---|---|
| Crossed by timing the circuit | **S10 → Accessible.** Nothing moves |
| Fought and fled | Nothing moves. **It does not learn, hunt or remember** (§4.5) |
| Fought and someone died | Nothing moves. **That is the horror of it** |
| System 6 claimed | **Instability 0 change.** The bearer is ignored. **Nobody else is** |
| System 6 corrected | **Instability −2. Hollow Rep +2**, clock toward cooperative containment, **Nethra's position wins.** Region-wide threat −1 stage (WP06 §14) |

## 13.5 E15 · Running the Deterrent Chain — S11

**Type D · level 4–5 · WP06 §6, §9, §14 · a full watch of work and no creature in the room**

A side gallery of resonating plates, each cut with a depth-mark. **The sequence exists specifically to move the Listener**, and the S6 records hold it.

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** No fight. **The cost is a full watch in a sector off the Long Gallery**, and the Sentinel passes the mouth during it.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** Striking the plates out of sequence is **a partial activation: Instability +1 instead of −1**, and it moves the Listener somewhere the party did not choose. WP06 §11.1 is explicit: *"possibly somewhere worse."*

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | S11 | The plates are cut with marks and the marks are in an order |
| **2** | S6 | The records give the sequence, plainly, if read |
| **3** | S11 | **The mouth of this gallery opens onto S10.** The party can hear the circuit from inside |
| **4** | S11 | It is passing now |

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** The sequence is **written down in S6** and takes a watch to read, without magic (WP06 §8). *Ask the records.* That is the route, and it is deliberately the same route that gives the Sentinel's description and the forms of authorisation — **one patient hour in a records room is the best-value action in the dungeon.**

**5 · Alternative solutions.** Don't run it — the Listener is avoidable (§10.6). Run it correctly for **Instability −1**. Or use it to move the Listener **into** a gallery the party wants blocked, which works and which the DM should allow.

**6 · Failure consequences.** Partial activation: **Instability +1**, and the Listener is now in a channel the party has not mapped.

**7 · Reward.** A resonance key, hung on the last plate where a warden left it (WP06 §9). **Wilderness threat near Greybank −1 stage while active** — Z4, Z5 and Z6 all drop (WP06 §14). This is the only thing in the site that makes the *surface* safer without costing the village anything.

**8 · World-state impact.**

| Outcome | State change |
|---|---|
| Run correctly | **Instability −1. Red-Tusk Rep +1, Compact +1. Hollow Intervention +1, Hollow Rep +1.** Z4, Z5, Z6 hazards −1 stage while active |
| Run partially | **Instability +1.** The Listener relocates, undetermined |
| Not run | Nothing |

## 13.6 E16 · The Broken Quarter — S16

**Type B · level 5–6 · WP06 §10, §11.3 · the hole in the fence, and the Moorgaunt's cache**

Collapsed stone, a slope of rubble, daylight at the top of it, cold air coming down. **Three dead anchors, unfixable in place.** Bones of things that came in and did not get further, and a goblin's bundle, dropped, forty years old.

**And, dry and out of the weather at the back of the rubble slope: a cache** (§9.3).

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** **The Moorgaunt at its cache is a Type B fight the party wins expensively** — and it is the only place in the campaign where it will not run, because it does not check morale while feeding. 76 hit points, a grapple that drags, and rubble underfoot that it ignores and the party does not.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** It fights to the end here, and it has the ground. **And killing it does not close the hole** (§9.6). A party that comes to S16 to solve the predation problem has come to the right place for the wrong reason.

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | Z11 | Drag furrows in the scree, all converging on one place |
| **2** | S14, S16 | Bones, stacked dry, part-eaten and returned to. **Old bones underneath and fresh ones on top** |
| **3** | S16 | **Daylight, and cold air, and a slope a sheep could be dragged up** — the fence has a hole in it and the party is standing in it |
| **4** | S16 | It is between them and the rubble slope, and it is not leaving |

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **Vulnerable While Feeding** (§9.3). Discovered by §9.4's five routes, and here specifically by *arriving when the cache is fresh* — which the party can time, because the rhythm is three nights and Bessa Ruddock has it written on a slate.

**5 · Alternative solutions.**
- **Come when it is not here.** Two nights in three, it is not.
- **Empty or burn the cache and leave.** It hunts that night, closer in — a real cost, honestly stated.
- **Use S16 as an exit and do not engage.** It is the Stage IV escape route to Z11 (WP06 §4.5).
- **Close the fence.** System 1 or System 7. **This is the only solution that fixes anything**, and the party will not have it before session 4.

**6 · Failure consequences.** A body dragged up the rubble slope and out onto Hagsfoot Moor, which is two watches from anywhere. And **the party has now been in Stage IV** — S16 is an entrance as well as an exit (WP06 §4.5), and a party that climbs down here at level 3 or 4 has skipped Stages I–III and is standing next to the Sentinel's Walk.

**7 · Reward.** **The three dead spans, seen close.** This is where a party physically understands why the Heart must be re-anchored on living spans elsewhere (WP06 §6). The goblin's bundle is forty years old and is WP13's business.

**8 · World-state impact.**

| Outcome | State change |
|---|---|
| S16 found from the moor | **S16 → Discovered.** Stage IV reachable, and the party has skipped three stages |
| The Moorgaunt killed here | **Z4 and Z11 hazards → Stage 1.** The hole is still open |
| The cache emptied | It hunts that night, one zone further in |
| **S16 widened** — deliberately, or at Instability 5 | **The Broken Quarter becomes a route, not a hole. Things come through in numbers** (WP06 §13). **Permanent** |
| The fence closed — System 1 or 7 | Nothing more comes through. **This is the answer, and it costs everything WP06 §6.8 says it costs** |

# 14. Hazards That Need No Creature

**WP05 §8 names two; WP06 hands over a third; WP02 §13 already built the fourth.** Design doc §14D is the type, and a campaign that only threatens with creatures has left half its tools unused.

**All four use WP01 §12's ladder at the appropriate tempo** — which is WP02's standing precedent (§33.2): *reuse the ladder at the appropriate tempo rather than inventing a subsystem.*

## 14.1 H01 · Anvil Gorge — Z10

**Type D · any level · WP05 §5, §8 — Stage 2 at campaign start · foot crossing only, single file, no cargo**

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** There is nothing to fight. The span is the encounter: single file, no rail, wet stone, and a drop.

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** There is no force to apply. **A party that hurries is the hazard.**

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | The approach | The path narrows and the noise of water rises from below |
| **2** | Z10 | **It is visibly a single-file crossing over a long drop, and it is visibly old** |
| **3** | Greybank | **Corvin Ash**, who reads the river: *"You can cross at the Anvil. You cannot bring anything with you."* |
| **4** | Z10 | Somebody is halfway across with a pack on |

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **It is safe unhurried, roped, and unladen.** Discovered by *asking Corvin Ash* (WP05 §9, discovery 2), by *asking Ib Sallow*, or by *looking at it*. There is no check to fail and no roll to make until somebody decides to hurry.

**5 · Alternative solutions.** The bridge. The shoal, in late summer. Bythe Ford, with a cart, +4 watches. The skiff, four at a time. **Four alternatives, all of them named in WP05 §5, none of them a fight.**

**6 · Failure consequences.** A fall. **This is one of two places on the map that kill by being what they are** (WP05 §17), and it should be allowed to.

**7 · Reward.** A crossing the Red-Tusk do not watch and no cart can use, one and a half watches west of Greybank.

**8 · World-state impact.** None by itself. **In spate it is impassable**, which matters when the bridge is down.

## 14.2 H02 · Bythe Ford in Spate — Z12

**Type D · any level · WP05 §5, §8, §17 · Stage 1, rising to Stage 3 in spate · the cart detour, +4 watches round trip*

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** In normal water: none. It is a ford. **In spate it is impassable, and a cart in it is lost.**

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** Nothing to fight, and everything to lose: the cart, the horses — **which never replenish** (WP02 §11.3) — and whoever was driving.

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | Z12 | **The marker post, cut with the water heights of bad years.** WP14's handout, and an explicit warning standing in the water |
| **2** | Greybank | The Marrow is brown and carrying branches |
| **3** | Greybank | **Corvin Ash says there will be spate tomorrow, and he is right, and he is never rolled for** (WP05 §17) |
| **4** | Z12 | The party is in the water and the water is rising |

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **Spate is telegraphed a full day ahead by a named NPC and never rolled.** That is the mechanism. *Ask Corvin Ash*, or *look at the post*, or *look at the river*.

**5 · Alternative solutions.** Wait — spate lasts 1–2 days. Anvil Gorge on foot. The skiff. The bridge, if it stands. **And if the bridge is down and it is spate, Greybank has no cart route at all**, which is not a solution but is the situation, and WP05 §17 fixed it.

**6 · Failure consequences.** The cart, the horses, the cargo, and possibly Gedd Plank — one of the five load-bearing providers (WP02 §19). **His death takes the Daggerford run with it.**

**7 · Reward.** None. It is a road.

**8 · World-state impact.** **Trade −1 while no cart route exists.** Every timber project stalls. Ib Sallow is stranded on the far bank. The smithy runs at half (WP05 §17).

## 14.3 H03 · The Sluice Hall — S7

**Type D · level 4 · WP06 §8, §14 · owed by WP06's handoff: an environmental threat needing no creature at all**

Channels and counterweighted gates over a black race of moving water. **Wet walkways, a real drop, and gates labelled in cut names.**

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** No creature. The hall is the encounter: narrow wet stone above moving water, and **it is loud enough to mask a conversation and everything else.**

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** **Nothing in here hears anything, including the party.** A party that splits up in the Sluice Hall cannot call to each other, cannot hear a fall, and cannot be warned. The noise is the hazard and it cuts both ways.

**3 · Telegraphing.**

| Level | Where | Source |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | S6 | The floor is damp forty feet before the door |
| **2** | S7 | **Nobody can hear anybody.** Establish this in the first sentence of the room description |
| **3** | S6 records | The gates are named, and the names say what each one does — including the one that drains S8 |
| **4** | S7 | Somebody is on the walkway and cannot hear the shout |

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **The gates are labelled.** A watch in the S6 records tells the party which gate does what, before they touch one. *Ask the records.* Alternatively, *watch the water* — the race's direction says which way each channel runs.

**5 · Alternative solutions.** **The overflow shaft** climbs out to the quarry's lowest bench and bypasses S5 entirely (WP06 §4.5). Rope the walkway. Cross one at a time in daylight brought from above. Do not use the hall at all — S6 connects to S9 without it.

**6 · Failure consequences.** A fall into a race that runs into the flooded galleries — **which is where the Listener is.** This is the only way in the campaign to arrive at the Listener involuntarily, and it is entirely fair: the hall is loud, wet and obviously dangerous, and it is described as such.

**7 · Reward.** **System 3, the Sluice Works.**

**8 · World-state impact.** WP06 §14's ledger row, unchanged:

| Use | State change |
|---|---|
| **Raised** | Shoal closed. **Bythe Ford impassable.** Instability +1 forced |
| **Lowered** | Shoal open permanently, ford ankle-deep, **and Greybank's mill stops.** Corvin Ash knows within a day. **Hollow Intervention +1** |
| Worked slowly | **Instability 0.** Reversible, slowly |

> **Draining the flooded gallery is the single most useful thing the party can do underground and the single most expensive thing they can do to the village, and they are the same action.**

## 14.4 H04 · Fire in Greybank

**Type D · any level · WP02 §13 · a procedure, not a subsystem**

**WP02 §13 owns what burns, how it spreads, and what it costs. WP07 adds only how to run it at the table and what an enemy does with it.**

**1 · Conventional difficulty.** Fire is not fought with attack rolls. **Run WP01 §12's ladder at combat tempo**, one stage per round, ending when the fire is out — WP02's standing precedent (§33.2).

**2 · Why brute force is costly.** There is nothing to hit. Every round spent fighting is a round the fire advances, and **the Cotts, the wagonyard and the mill/granary pair spread automatically** (WP02 §13).

**3 · Telegraphing.** WP02 §13.5's four accelerants are all on the map and **the players will find all of them**: the pitch barrel in the militia shed, the hay loft at the wagonyard, the charcoal stacks at G20, and six charges of blasting powder at the quarry.

**4 · Discoverable weakness, and how it is discovered.** **The store's slate roof is the Street's firebreak** (WP02 §19). Discovered by *looking at the roofs* — WP02 §5 requires roof material be visible on the DM map for exactly this reason — or by *asking anybody in the village*, because everyone here knows which way a fire runs.

**5 · Alternative solutions.** Water butts at every third building. Pull down the burning cott before the eaves catch the next one — **which needs Enna Fisk or people who will listen to her.** Drive the livestock east. **Get the children out of the Cotts**, which is the correct first action and almost never the one taken.

**6 · Failure consequences.** WP02 §13's two catastrophes: **Millside** (Mill destroyed, **Food −2**) and **the Cotts** (three or more lost in a night, **Unity −1**).

**7 · Reward.** None. Nobody wins a fire.

**8 · World-state impact.** Per WP02 §13 and §14. Plus WP07's addition:

> **An enemy will eventually use this.** The Red-Tusk at W4 burn **outside the ring** — the fold, the byres, the bunkhouse, the charcoal camp — because they are not stupid and the core is stone. A morale roll of **6 — Harden** (§4.2) is the DM's licence to have any group reach for fire, and the accelerants are already on the map.

## 16.1 Example A — The party solves the wrong half of the fold

**Session 1. Level 3. Starting state: Food 4 / Trade 3 / Defense 2 / Unity 4. Red-Tusk Rep −1, all others 0. Red-Tusk Strategic 1. Illefarn Instability 1.**

**What happens.** Bessa Ruddock reports losses on the Green in the Evening watch. The party goes to Z4 in daylight, sees a broken top course and a drag furrow, does not talk to Maud, and decides to sit up at the fold that night.

**Night one.** Nothing comes. The party is cold and bored.

**Night two.** Three Red-Tusk Scouts come through the wall — stones moved and put back badly — and start on a ewe. The party attacks. Two scouts drop; one uses Nimble Escape and is gone north before anyone reaches the wall.

**Resolution at the table.** A fair Type A fight (§6.5), over in four rounds, one PC at half hit points. The party walks back and tells Bessa it is dealt with.

### The state changes, immediately

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Red-Tusk Rep −1 → −2** | Two fighters dead. WP01 §4.4 |
| **War +1** in WP04 §8.3's pressure count | §4.5 — all three scouts, or their fate, reported by the survivor |
| **Bessa Ruddock: Neutral → Friendly** | She believes them. WP03 §15 — it happened to her |
| **Greybank Rep 0 → +1** | A visible public success against a threat |
| Z4 hazard | **Unchanged. Still Incident** |

### Three nights later

**The Moorgaunt takes a ewe.** Broken top course, drag furrow west and uphill, and no goblins anywhere.

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Food 4 → 3** | Major livestock attack. WP02 §7, G18 |
| **Bessa Ruddock: Friendly → Neutral** | It happened to her, and they told her it was over. WP03 §15's one-step rule |
| **Greybank Rep +1 → 0** | The public success was not one |
| **Z4 hazard: Incident → Escalation** | WP05 §8's stated advancement — ignored predation, by session 3 |

### Session 2

The escaped scout has reported. **Ghesh now knows the party's number and reach, and that they attack on sight** (§4.5).

- **The envoy still comes** — Orbal sends it over Ghesh's objection (WP04 §13.4). It comes to the bridge, not the fold, because the fold is where two of them died.
- **Red-Tusk Rep is −2.** WP01 §4.4 at −2 is *ambush, sabotage, prisoners taken for leverage* — so the envoy arrives into a village that has already been told the party killed goblins at the fold, and the five blocs are not agreed about what that means.
- The party has **one chance left** to avoid the War branch and it is a conversation they now have to have from −2.

> **Nothing here punished the party for fighting.** The fight was fair, winnable and won. What it cost them was the *diagnosis* — and Maud Elleth was a four-minute walk away the whole time (WP03 §15: reading an injury is Moderate evidence).

### The cascade check

Scouts killed → Red-Tusk Rep −1 and War +1. **Two links. Stop** (WP01 §16). The Moorgaunt taking a ewe three nights later is not a consequence of the fight — it is the animal's own three-night rhythm (§9.3), which was running before the party arrived and would have run if they had never come.

## 16.2 Example B — Four warnings and one round of grace

**Session 3. Level 4. The party has a resonance key from S2, has read the S6 records for a full watch, and has come down the Long Gallery to reach S11 and the Deterrent Chain.**

**What they have already been told:**

1. **Level 1**, session 2: Ghesh, at the rim of Kettlerock — *"Something came at night. It did not hurry."*
2. **Level 2**, this session: the S6 records describe it by function, in the register of a maintenance manual, without alarm.
3. **Level 3**, ten minutes ago: **the groove in S10, worn an inch deep into stone by footsteps.** The wizard measured it with a knuckle.
4. **Level 4**, now: it is at the far end of the gallery, walking toward them at thirty feet a round.

**Posture 1 — Warning.** It closes and **stops at 30 ft.** It stands. **One full round of grace** (§11.2).

### What the party does with the round

**They attack.** Two spells, an arrow and a javelin. Total damage from the largest single source: 19.

**Nothing happens.** No mark. No reaction. **Threshold of Consequence** (§11.2). The DM says: *"It does not appear to have noticed."*

**Posture 3 — Suppression**, immediately, because damage was dealt.

### Rounds 2 and 3

- **Round 2.** Multiattack. Two attacks at +9, 3d10+6 each. The fighter takes 22 and is pushed 15 ft toward the S9 end. The cleric takes 24 and drops.
- **Round 3.** The party runs. It walks after them at 30 ft, and **it does not stop.**

### The escape

They reach S9, get through the leaf, and shut it. **Solution 2** (§11.5): the leaf holds it.

**It is temporary.** It walks another way, and the party has bought hours, not sessions. They go up the overflow shaft to the quarry's lowest bench and out.

### The state changes

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **A PC at 0 hit points, stabilised, and one failed death save** | The fight was legitimate. §11.4 — four warnings, one round of grace |
| **Illefarn Instability: unchanged** | **Nothing here advances on a timer** (WP06 §12). They touched no system |
| **S10 → Discovered. S9 → Accessible** | WP01 §15.2 |
| **The Sentinel: nothing** | It does not learn, hunt or remember. **It resumes the circuit** (§4.5) |
| **Laughing Hollow: unchanged** | Nobody saw. Nobody notices a fight in a corridor |

### What the party now knows, which is the reward

- Damage does nothing.
- It walks and does not stop.
- **It stopped at thirty feet before it did anything**, which means it was not hunting them.
- The Watch-Glass in S4 — which they looked into in session 1 and did not think about — **shows exactly where the line runs**, and therefore where it will stop following.

> **The encounter cost them a PC's blood and gave them solution 1 for free.** That is the Type F contract: survival, information, escape.

### If they had spent the round differently

**Walking backward toward S9 during the grace round ends the encounter with no damage at all.** Posture 2 only triggers if a creature is still inside the line at the end of the round, and "inside the line" is a place, not a room. The party did not know that. **The Watch-Glass would have told them.**

## 16.3 Example C — The party drains the gallery and stops the mill

**Session 3. Level 4. The party wants past the Listener and does not want to swim.**

They have read the S6 records (a full watch), so they know the S7 gate names. They lower the sluice. **S8 drains.**

### Immediate

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **S8 → Breached, permanently** | WP06 §13. Does not reset with Instability |
| **The Listener's water is gone. It goes somewhere, and where it goes is now a fact** | WP06 §13 |
| **System 3 → Active. Illefarn Instability 1 → 2** | Forced rather than worked slowly. WP06 §14 |
| **Waystones: two warm that were not** — W4 on Stonecrop Rise and W5 at Wend's Barrow, warm to the touch and audible at night | WP06 §12, Instability 2 |
| **Laughing Hollow Intervention +1** | WP06 §14's ledger row for System 3 |

### Within a day — the surface

**The Marrow drops.** This is not subtle and it is not deniable.

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Greybank's mill stops** | WP06 §14. The Mill is not damaged — there is not enough water to turn it |
| **Mill → Disabled** | WP01 §14.2's infrastructure state, by cause of water rather than of fire |
| **The shoal opens permanently** | WP06 §14 — an eighth way into Greybank, at all seasons, for anyone (WP02 §4.7 route 6) |
| **Bythe Ford goes ankle-deep** | The cart detour is now trivially passable. **The Red-Tusk W5 interdiction loses half its bite** |
| **Corvin Ash knows first, and comes to find the party** | WP06 §14 names him. WP03 §5's person |

### Within three days

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Food 4 → 3** | No mill, no flour. The granary is full of grain nobody can grind |
| **Corvin Ash: Neutral → Wary** | It happened to him and his. WP03 §15 |
| **Lisbet Ash: Friendly, and stays Friendly** | WP03 §15 — she is the designated demonstration of attitude overriding reputation, and this is the moment it demonstrates |
| **Unity 4 → 3** | A visibly catastrophic PC decision (WP01 §3.4) |

**And that is where the cascade stops.** System activated → mill disabled → Food −1. **Two links** (WP01 §16). Unity −1 is a *direct* effect of WP01 §3.4's "visibly catastrophic PC decision", not a third link, and the DM should say so out loud when updating.

### What the party got

- **A dry route to Stage III**, permanently.
- **The shoal open**, which is a route they now control and the village did not choose to have.
- **The ford passable in spate**, which will matter enormously in session 4 if the bridge goes down.

### What it is reversible into

**Yes, slowly** (WP06 §6). Raising the sluice again refloods S8, restarts the mill, and closes the shoal — and it puts the Listener's water back with the Listener somewhere else in it. **The party can undo the mill and cannot undo the breach.**

> **This is the campaign's clearest statement that the dungeon is not a separate place.** The party solved a monster problem underground and turned off the village's bread.

## 16.4 Example D — The Flight branch, night three

**Session 4. Level 5. The party fought the Red-Tusk refugees at Kettlerock in session 2 and never spoke to Orbal. The branch resolved to Flight. F6 landed two days ago: the band is gone.**

**Entering state:** Food 3 / Trade 2 / Defense 3 / Unity 3. Greybank Rep +1, Hollow 0, Red-Tusk −3, Daggerford +1. Illefarn Instability 3. **S12 breached** — nobody has re-stacked the pile for six days and the slope did the rest (WP06 §15).

### Nights one and two

**Night 1 — Z7 Stonecrop Rise.** Nobody in Greybank goes there at night. The party is not there. **Nothing is observed, and this is correct** — the campaign does not narrate what nobody sees.

**Night 2 — Z14 Wend's Barrow.** **Gedd Plank is on the Daggerford road** with a stone wagon and he sees it cross the road ahead of him at fifty yards, walking, not hurrying. He turns the cart around and comes back at speed, and he tells the whole taproom.

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Gedd Plank: Neutral → Wary** | It happened to him |
| **Unity 3 → 2** | The village is now arguing about whether to leave. **The isolationists become the loudest voice at Unity ≤2** (WP04 §16) |
| The stone shipment does not go | **Trade 2 → 1** |

### Night three — Greybank

It arrives from the north footpath, past the burial ground, at the Deep Night watch when **two or three people are awake** (WP02 §16).

**It walks to the tower base (G13), then the shrine foundation (G9), then the burial-ground wall (G19)** — the three anchors the village built onto, ninety years ago, without asking why the stone was jointless.

**Everything unauthorised standing between them is an incursion.**

### What does not work

| The village tries | Result |
|---|---|
| **The bell** | Rings. **Muster at Defense 3 is ~12 minutes** (§5.4). It is at the shrine by then |
| **Eleven billhooks at chokepoint 2** | It is not going down the Street. **It is going between three stones** |
| **Berrick and Nissa on the Moot Hall roof** | Two arrows. **Damage below 25 leaves no mark** (§11.2) |
| **Barring doors** | It does not open doors it was not built to open. **It walks past them** |
| **Ordga Brune in the forge doorway with a hammer** | **Dangerous Once** does 1d10+3+1d8. It is not enough and she is in the way |

### What does work

| The party does | Result |
|---|---|
| **Clears the line between the three anchors** | Nobody is standing in it. It walks the circuit and goes. **This costs nothing but nerve and about ninety seconds of shouting** |
| **Carries authorisation** (System 6, claimed) | The bearer is ignored. **Nobody else is** — and the party has to decide who stands where |
| **Has corrected its map** (System 6) | It is not here. It is walking the line as built, and this scene does not happen |
| **Re-stacks the Kettlerock pile** — four watches out, hours of work | It holds a while. **This is available and the party has to leave the village to do it** |

### The state changes if the party clears the line

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Nobody dies** | And the village saw the party do it |
| **Greybank Rep +1 → +2** | A public success against a threat nobody understood |
| **Unity 2 → 3** | Public success against threat (WP01 §3.4) |
| **Illefarn Instability: unchanged at 3** | **Nothing here advances on a timer.** The Sentinel is not a clock |
| **It comes back tomorrow night** | The circuit is a circuit. **This is the problem the finale has to solve** |

### The state changes if Ordga stands in the doorway

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Ordga Brune dies** | WP02 §19 — a load-bearing provider. **Metal service stops immediately, stock remains, no replacement within four sessions** (WP03 §15) |
| **Ammunition stops replenishing** | WP02 §11.3 — it needed Ordga alive |
| **Ondra and Ordga's grief map** | WP03 §8.3. Whoever the village blames goes to permanent Hostile |
| **Unity 2 → 1** | Trusted NPC killed (WP01 §3.4). **At Unity 1 Defense cannot rise above 3 through collective action** |

> **Nothing about this night is retroactive** (WP06 §15). The party chose Flight in session 2 by fighting refugees instead of asking what they were running from. Ghesh and Orbal both described it. The pile at Z17 was visibly maintained by hand. **The Watch-Glass shows the line, and the line ends at Greybank.**

# 17. Edge Cases and Adjudication Guidance

## 17.1 The party splits

WP01 §21.6 governs. **WP07's addition is that most of this library is fine with it and two entries are not.**

| Entry | Split party |
|---|---|
| **Most surface entries** | Run each group at its own tempo. Watches are long enough to interleave |
| **E14 the Long Gallery** | **It walks toward whichever group is inside the line.** If both are, it goes to the nearer. **The other group's round of grace is spent watching**, which is the best information delivery in the campaign |
| **H03 the Sluice Hall** | **They cannot hear each other. At all.** A split party in S7 is genuinely two encounters, and this is the hall's whole point |
| **E16 the Broken Quarter** | The Moorgaunt takes the isolated one and leaves. §9.3, *Starving* |
| **§5.5 Greybank as terrain** | The village is 500 yards end to end and **anywhere in the ring is under 30 rounds from anywhere else** (WP02 §4.5). A split party inside the ring is not really split |

## 17.2 The party wipes

WP01 §21.7 and design doc §33 govern: **new characters, a setback, and some skipped time.**

**WP07's addition — where the bodies are, because it changes the replacement.**

| Wiped by | What the world does |
|---|---|
| **The Sentinel, in S10** | Nobody finds them. Nobody in Greybank enters the site (WP03 §15). **The new party's hook is that five people went into the quarry and did not come out** — which is the same Level 3 warning, delivered harder |
| **The Moorgaunt** | **The bodies go to a cache**, and they are findable. New characters can recover gear |
| **The militia at Rep −3** | They are alive and in the Moot Hall store room (§4.6). **A party wipe against Greybank is usually a capture, not a death** — WP01 §9.3 |
| **The Red-Tusk at W6** | Prisoners, for leverage. WP01 §4.4 at −2 |
| **Anvil Gorge or the ford** | Nobody finds them either, and **there is no enemy to blame** |

**Time skipped:** at least a tenday. Every clock advances one stage on its own trigger; **Illefarn does not**, because nobody touched it (WP04 §16).

## 17.3 The party ignores this package entirely

**Perfectly possible, and the campaign still runs.** WP04 §12.2's four-session default is the baseline, and it does not require a single encounter from this document.

| If the party never | Then |
|---|---|
| Goes to the fold | **Food −1, then Z4 → Escalation by session 3.** Bessa loses the flock and the village loses the wool |
| Goes underground | **Illefarn stays at 1** (WP04 §16). The Watch-Glass is never seen; the Sentinel never appears **unless the Flight branch fires** |
| Meets a warden | The Hollow **never moves at all** (WP04 §16). This is a campaign with three factions in it and that is legitimate |
| Fights anything | **Also legitimate.** Every entry above has non-combat solutions, and a party that takes all of them has played the campaign correctly, not avoided it |
| Leaves Greybank | The Red-Tusk arrive on their own schedule. §6.4's assault table is the ending |

> **Unused material stays unused.** Design doc §22. Do not relocate an encounter to wherever the party went.

## 17.4 The party commits to killing the Sentinel anyway

**Let them, and do not soften it.**

- Say, out loud and out of character, **once**: *"You have been told four times. Damage does nothing. Are you sure?"* That is the fairness contract's last instalment.
- Then run §11.2's posture 3 exactly as written. **It kills level 3–6 characters in two rounds.**
- **Do not invent a hidden weak point.** There is not one. WP06 §19 is binding: *not killable at these levels.*
- If they retreat mid-fight, it does not pursue past the perimeter, and **nothing has changed in the world** (§4.5).

## 17.5 The party tries to talk to it

A bard will try. **They should try, and it should be a real scene.**

It has **INT 1** and no language beyond the cut names of the Marrowward. It does not converse, cannot be persuaded, deceived or intimidated, and **stopping at thirty feet is not it listening** — it is posture 1.

**But:** presenting a **name the register accepts** is not talking, and it works (WP06 §5.3, §11.5 solution 3). A player who says the name from Orbal's cords out loud in front of it, in S14, has solved the campaign's apex threat with a sentence. **That is the intended outcome and it should feel like one.**

## 17.6 The party brings hirelings or villagers into the site

**No villager will enter the Illefarn site, ever, for any inducement** (WP02, WP03 §15, WP04 §11 — binding). Not for money, not for friendship, not for the Reeve.

- **Imported hirelings** are a Daggerford problem and cost coin the party does not have locally (WP02 §19: ≈260 gp in the whole village).
- A hireling in the site is **a torch and a rope and nothing else.** Do not stat them as combatants; use the Armed Villager block (§5.3) and its *Not a Soldier* trait.
- **A hireling who dies underground is a Daggerford claim**, in writing, two days later.

## 17.7 The party uses the blasting powder

**Six charges, at the quarry, and WP02 §11.2 says to assume the players will find them.**

| Used on | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **A bridge pier** | It works. Bridge → Destroyed. WP01 §14.1 and WP05 §5 own everything after |
| **The aperture (S1)** | Collapses the ramp. **The site is not closed** — S12 and S16 are both still open. The party has closed their own front door |
| **A quarry face onto something** | It works, once, for that charge. **Kael Durrow will know exactly how many are gone** |
| **A waystone** | **It works.** That span cannot be re-anchored, ever (WP06 §13) |
| **The Sentinel** | Damage from one charge is well over 25. **It chips it. It keeps walking** |
| Inside the Marrowward at all | Stone is jointless and two thousand years old. **A DM may rule any single charge collapses one sector's ceiling** — and every collapse is a permanent change to the sector, per WP01 §15.5 |

**Replenishment: 2 charges per cart, at Trade ≥3, with Durrow alive and the quarry working** (WP02 §11.3). Spend them and they are gone.

## 17.8 A PC is dragged away

The Moorgaunt's *Starving* trait will do this and it will feel unfair the first time. **It is not.**

- **The character is not dead.** They are being carried to a known destination on a route that leaves a furrow in wet ground.
- **The party has a real chase with a real deadline**, and the destination is discoverable (§9.4).
- **If they do not go, the character dies**, and that is the party's decision rather than a die roll.
- **Do not use this to remove a character from a session.** If the party gives chase, they can catch it — the cache is minutes away, not zones away.

## 17.9 Enemies the party keeps letting escape

**Legitimate, and it accumulates.** §4.5 is not a punishment; it is a memory.

- A scout who escapes twice is the one Ghesh sends to **look at the party specifically.**
- The Moorgaunt driven off three times is hunting **inside the ring** by session 4 — it has run out of easier food, exactly as §9.5 says.
- **Nothing in this library escapes and forgets.** Except the Sentinel, which never remembered.

## 17.10 The party is at Greybank Reputation −3 and wants to fight

Run **§5.5**, and read WP01 §9.3 first. **A level 3–5 party openly fighting Greybank should very probably die** (design doc §33.2) — but:

- **Escape and surrender remain possible**, and six routes are telegraphed (WP02 §12.6).
- **The militia never crosses the boundary stones** (WP03 §15, WP04 §11), so the region is still open.
- **The consequence is that Greybank content becomes inaccessible**, not that the campaign ends. There are three other factions.
- **Do not roll initiative for a hundred and forty-three people.** WP01 §9.2 forbids it and §5.5 above gives the alternative.

## 17.11 The party captures Ghesh, or Orbal

| Prisoner | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Ghesh alive, held** | **Compact +2** — Orbal is speaking unopposed. The band will trade for him: food, a place, or a hostage back |
| **Ghesh killed after surrender** | **War +3 and Red-Tusk Rep → −3.** A goblin saw it (§4.6) |
| **Orbal alive, held** | The band will give almost anything. **Ghesh will also stop arguing for war, because she is in the village** — this is the single most effective and most repellent lever in the campaign, and it should be available |
| **Orbal killed** | **The Compact branch becomes unreachable and the War branch locks** (WP04 §13.4). And the party has closed a door into Stage IV they do not know exists |

## 17.12 The party fights something and the village finds out

WP01 §7 and §8 own crime. **WP07's addition: which of this library's encounters are crimes.**

| Act | Severity |
|---|---|
| Killing goblins outside the ring | **Not a crime in Greybank.** It is a favour, until it is not |
| Killing an envoy under parley | **Not a crime in law.** WP04 §16 — it is worse than a crime; it locks a branch |
| Killing a warden | Not a crime. Nobody in Greybank will ever know |
| Killing a caravan guard | **A crime, and Daggerford investigates by contract rather than by law.** A written claim, two days later |

# 18. Final Canonical WP07 Rules

The compressed rule set. Later packages cite this section, and it is readable alone.

## The eight fields

**Every encounter in this campaign carries eight fields:** conventional difficulty · why brute force is costly · telegraphing mapped to design doc §6's four levels · discoverable weakness *and how it is discovered* · alternative solutions · failure consequences · reward · world-state impact as concrete state changes. **Any encounter a later package adds must carry all eight.**

## The two prohibitions

**No encounter may have combat as its only solution. No encounter may have exactly one non-combat solution.** §15.2 checks every entry in this document against both.

## The weakness rule

**Every Type C, D and F encounter has at least one weakness discoverable by scouting, asking an NPC, or watching** — never by a single check, never by a check the party can fail permanently.

## Difficulty is a description

**There is no CR budgeting anywhere in this campaign.** Field 1 describes what a fight costs; it is not a licence to move, scale or soften an encounter. **Never adjust an encounter's contents because the party arrived early.** The telegraphing is the fairness (design doc §2.1, §29).

## Morale

**Morale is WP01 §17.1's d6, rolled once per group per trigger.** 1 rout · 2 break · 3–4 waver, and break on the next trigger without a roll · 5 hold · 6 harden. **The Sentinel, the wardens, the swarms, and the Moorgaunt while feeding never check.**

## Survivors remember

**§4.5 is binding.** Every group that escapes does something specific next session, and the DM writes the survivor down at WP01 §18.3's end-of-session update. **The Sentinel is the only thing in the campaign that escapes being escaped and does nothing about it.**

## The militia

**WP02 §12.3's turnout and muster figures are fixed.** Defense sets numbers, positions and preparation; **only WP02 §15's militia training project buys competence**, and it swaps the Greybank Militiaman block for the Drilled Militiaman. **The militia never crosses the boundary stones and no villager ever enters the site.**

## Greybank as terrain

**At Rep −2 nothing attacks — services close, quietly. At −3, run §5.5**: the village as terrain, on a round-band schedule, with six escape routes telegraphed and no initiative order for a hundred and forty-three people.

## The Red-Tusk

**Twenty-two fighters: 1 Ghesh, 3 scouts, 14 spear, 4 bow. There will never be more.** The band never commits all of them; **the most ever seen in one place before W6 is eight.** **Probes are survivable by both sides.** Ghesh calls the withdrawal at six down, by decision, not by morale roll. **The thirty-nine are never statted.**

## The Laughing Hollow

**Nine wardens, and the party never fights nine.** **Every warden breaks contact after two full rounds, regardless of how the fight is going.** They do not check morale, do not surrender, and do not take prisoners. **Every warden encounter is an interdiction, a recovery, or a warning — never a battle.**

## Daggerford

**Five guards per wagon, and they are paid for the cargo.** They do not move more than 60 ft from it, do not pursue, and stop when it is safe. **They are never sent to Greybank as help** (WP04 §11).

## The Moorgaunt

**One starving upland predator, come down through S16 because sixty-one goblins ate Hagsfoot Moor bare.** It **caches**, and will not hunt while a cache holds meat — one kill feeds it three nights. It is **vulnerable while feeding** and **will not flee a cache**. **It will not cross the Sentinel's groove in S10**, which is why the Marrowward is not already full of moor animals. **Killing it does not close the hole.**

## The Listener

**Blind, hunts vibration through dry stone, cannot detect a creature standing in running water.** Tremorsense 60 ft through stone; blind beyond. **97 hit points, and it is the only resident of the site that can be killed.** Killing it is **Instability +1**, and within a tenday **Z6 Reedwater advances one stage** and the leeches reach the S8 channels.

## The Sentinel

**No hit points. Damage below 25 from a single source leaves no mark; damage above it chips a wall and does nothing else.** Three postures — Warning, Removal, Suppression — escalating in order and never de-escalating within an encounter. **One round of grace at thirty feet, and then the DM is not required to protect anyone.** It does not pursue past the perimeter, does not learn, does not remember, and does not check morale. **Four solutions, none of them a fight**, and the Watch-Glass hands the first one to a level 3 party for free.

## Stage I

**There is no creature in Stage I and WP07 declines to add one.** Its dangers are a ramp, a shutter that breaks permanently, and an anchor that answers if forced.

## Hazards without creatures

**Four: Anvil Gorge, Bythe Ford in spate, the Sluice Hall, and fire in Greybank.** All four run WP01 §12's ladder at the appropriate tempo, per WP02's standing precedent. **Spate is telegraphed a day ahead through Corvin Ash and is never rolled.**

## Cascades

**Every consequence in this document is written as a WP01 state change, and every chain stops at two links** unless a major event directly touches several systems. **Draining S8 → mill disabled → Food −1 is the canonical two-link example**, and Unity −1 alongside it is a *direct* effect of WP01 §3.4, not a third link.

# WP07 Completion Criterion

**The judging test, from the brief:** *at least one discoverable weakness per Type C/D/F encounter that a player could plausibly find by scouting, asking an NPC, or watching.* **§15.2 is that table**, and every C, D and F entry names its route and its source.

WP07 is complete when later packages can answer questions like these without inventing a new framework:

- What does a level 3 party actually face at the fold, and what happens if they solve the wrong half of it?
- How many militia turn out at Defense 3, how long do they take, and what are they holding?
- What changes about the militia when the village completes the training project?
- What does a three-scout probe look like, and what does the band learn from one that gets away?
- How many Red-Tusk fighters are there, how many will ever be in one place, and when does Ghesh call the withdrawal?
- What happens if the party shoots at a warden?
- Why can the party never fight all nine wardens?
- Will Threll's guards defend a person? Will they follow anyone?
- What is coming through the Broken Quarter, why is it there, and whose fault is it?
- What is the Moorgaunt's rhythm, how does a party discover it, and where is the animal on any given night?
- Why is the Marrowward not already full of moor animals?
- What actually happens when a level 4 party attacks the Sentinel in the Long Gallery?
- What does the Sentinel ignore, and what is the shortest list of things that work on it?
- What does a party get for free from the Watch-Glass, at level 3, in session 1?
- Which encounters need no creature at all, and what warns the party about each?
- What notices when the Listener dies?
- What does the Listener do differently if the party hurts it and leaves?
- What happens on the third night after the Red-Tusk are gone, and what does Greybank try that does not work?
- Which of this library's encounters are crimes in Greybank, and which are favours?
- How does a DM run a fight the party has to lose without it being unfair?
- What happens if the party solves every encounter here without fighting anything?
