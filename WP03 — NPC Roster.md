# The Broken March — WP03: NPC Roster

**Status:** Canonical campaign character document
**Consumes:** `Campaign design doc.md`, `WP01 — Campaign State Model.md`, `WP02 — Greybank Village.md`
**Feeds:** WP04, WP07, WP08–WP12, WP13, WP14, WP15, WP16
**Scope:** The 28 people of Greybank — portrait, voice, personality, want, fear, resource, attitude, knowledge, secrets, relationships, reactions, what each does when the party is not looking, and what is lost when each one dies

WP02 built a village and told you who holds the keys. WP03 makes those people run without the party. Every entry answers the one question the campaign actually depends on — *what does this person do tonight if nobody speaks to them?* — because a reactive world is only reactive if its inhabitants were already going somewhere. This document owns personality, voice, want, fear, secrets, relationships and reactions. It does not move a single building, service, key or item that WP02 placed, and it does not decide what any faction does; that is WP04.

---

# 1. Purpose and Boundaries

## 1.1 What WP03 owns

- 28 named recurring NPCs, at three levels of detail.
- Per NPC: portrait, voice handle, personality, **want / fear / resource / attitude**, current objective, important knowledge, secret where relevant.
- **Starting PC attitude** for every recurring NPC (WP01 §4.6).
- The relationship map, and the grief map that goes with it.
- Reaction guidance: how each responds to help, indifference, pressure, crime, and the death of someone they love.
- **The "tonight" line** — what each NPC does if the party never engages them.
- Printable NPC card content (WP01 §13.4 format).
- The load-bearing table's human half: who *tries* to fill a gap, how badly, and what it costs them.
- Secrets, and how each surfaces.

## 1.2 What WP03 defers

| Deferred | To | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Buildings, services, stock, keys, distances, upgrade costs | **WP02** | WP03 relocates nothing. Every location reference points at a WP02 key. |
| Faction leadership, goals, methods, red lines, clocks | **WP04** | Including the elves, the Red-Tusk, and Daggerford's agents — **no faction NPC appears here.** |
| The politics of Greybank's internal blocs | **WP04** | WP03 gives what each person *wants*; WP04 decides what the blocs *do*. |
| Stat blocks | **WP07** | WP03 notes how each would fight, not what they roll. |
| Anything outside the boundary stones | **WP05** | |
| What is behind the quarry boards | **WP06** | Two NPCs have opinions about it. Neither knows. |

> **One NPC standard.** WP04 will need NPCs of its own — an elven envoy, a Red-Tusk speaker, a Daggerford factor. **They use this document's format** (§4, §10) rather than inventing a second one. WP03 owns the template; WP04 owns those characters.

## 1.3 Design decisions taken

Flagged for confirmation, per the working method.

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Not every starting attitude is Neutral.** Six NPCs begin Friendly or Wary. | WP01 §4.6 makes attitude independent of reputation. If all 28 start Neutral, that independence is invisible until session 3. Starting spread gives the players something to notice on day one. |
| 2 | **Seven secrets, no conspiracy.** No secret connects to another. | Design doc §7: *"No hidden mastermind controls all factions."* Secrets that interlock become a plot; secrets that don't become texture and leverage. |
| 3 | **Nobody in Greybank knows what is behind the quarry boards.** Durrow and Fell each have a theory. Both are wrong in different directions. | Preserves WP06's ownership, and makes the players' investigation genuinely the first. |
| 4 | **Sixteen villagers stay deliberately unnamed** (§7.4). | Design doc §21 caps the roster at 20–30. Naming all 34 households makes the list unmemorable and steals WP04's and WP08's room to introduce someone. |
| 5 | **Halvard Vell, the previous Reeve, is alive and diminished.** | Gives the office a history, gives Ondra a private cost, and puts one person in the village who remembers the wolf winter accurately. |
| 6 | **Lisbet Ash is WP01's own example NPC.** WP01 §4.6 illustrates attitude with *"the miller's daughter … Friendly because the PCs saved her family."* | Free continuity. WP03 makes her the roster's designated demonstration of attitude overriding reputation. |
| 7 | Portraits describe **one memorable physical fact** plus supporting detail, not a paragraph. | Design doc §28: no lore dumps. A DM recalls "the smith with the burned forearm," not four sentences. |

**None.**

**Inherited unchanged from WP02 §19 and its Handoffs:**

- All 18 names, with household, occupation and mechanical function **fixed**.
- The five load-bearing providers and their replacement paths (WP02 §9.2).
- **Yorrin Fell casts nothing.**
- **Lisbet Ash** is the mill's only true redundancy.

WP03 exercises the one permission WP02 granted — *"may relocate but not redefine: who holds which key, and which household occupies which cott among H6–H21"* — by naming four of those households (§7) and leaving the rest open. **No key has been moved.**

# 4. How to Run These People

## 4.1 The voice handle

Every NPC has a one-line **voice handle**: a speech habit specific enough to perform and cheap enough to remember. It is not an accent and it is not a catchphrase.

> Use the handle, the **want**, and the **fear**. That is a playable character. Everything else on the sheet is reference.

## 4.2 The four card fields

WP01 §13.4 fixes them. What they mean here:

| Field | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **WANT** | What are they trying to get, this tenday? Drives the "tonight" line. |
| **FEAR** | What will they trade away to avoid? Drives every negotiation. |
| **RESOURCE** | What can they give the party that nobody else can? |
| **ATTITUDE** | WP01 §4.6: Friendly / Neutral / Wary / Hostile. **Personal, not factional.** |

## 4.3 The "tonight" rule

**Every NPC has a line describing what they do if the party never engages them.** This is the roster's core requirement and its completion test.

Three rules:

1. **Tonight lines are not hooks.** They are what the person is already doing. If the party walks past, it happens anyway.
2. **They advance on their own.** A tonight line that is identical in session 3 belongs to a background NPC, not a Tier 1.
3. **They collide.** Ondra needs bodies for the levy; Kael needs the same bodies at the face; Berrick needs them on the roll. Three tonight lines, eighteen men, one village — and no faction machinery required. WP04 will build on this; it does not have to invent it.

## 4.4 Attitude versus reputation

WP01 §4.6 is explicit: faction reputation supplies the default; **personal experience overrides it.**

WP01's own example is this roster's demonstration case:

> *"Greybank Rep −2, but the miller's daughter remains Friendly because the PCs saved her family."*

That is **Lisbet Ash** (§6.2). She is the designated proof that the village is people rather than a number, and WP08–WP12 should use her that way.

**Adjudication:**

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Rep changes, NPC has no personal experience of the party | Attitude follows the new default |
| Rep changes, NPC has personal experience | **Attitude does not move.** It moves when *they* experience something |
| An NPC's attitude and the village's reputation conflict publicly | The NPC pays for it socially. That is a scene, and a good one |
| Attitude shifts | One step at a time, and only on something that happened **to them or to theirs** |

## 4.5 Suspicion

WP01 §13.3: **None / Uneasy / Suspicious / Convinced.** Only crime-relevant NPCs carry it. It is personal belief, not reputation, and it is not attitude either — a Friendly NPC can be Convinced and heartbroken.

Adjudicate against WP02 §16.2's witness table, not against impressions. In Greybank, four people are disproportionately likely to acquire suspicion:

- **Nissa Crake** — awake at Deep Night, by function.
- **Pell** — twelve years old and everywhere.
- **Old Tarn** — sits watching the Green all day.
- **Maud Elleth** — sees every wound in the village and knows what made it.

> **Maud is the campaign's forensic problem.** She cannot cast, but she has treated injuries for thirty years and knows the difference between a fall and a blow. A party that lies to her about an injury is rolling against someone with real expertise, and WP01 §7.2 should treat what she concludes as **Moderate** evidence.

## 5.1 Ondra Vell — Reeve of Greybank

**Moot Hall (G11) · Leadership · Attitude: Neutral · [CAMPAIGN]**

**Portrait.** Late forties, tall, ink on her fingers at all hours — she writes everything down, which in Greybank is nearly a superstition. Wears her father's coat, which is too big and which she has never had taken in.

**Voice.** Finishes other people's sentences, then apologises for it. Asks a question back before answering one.

**Personality.** Competent, tired, and constitutionally unable to promise something she is not sure she can deliver — which villagers read as coldness and which is actually the reason they keep electing her. She holds the office because nobody else wants the blame.

| | |
|---|---|
| **WANT** | To get Greybank to harvest without asking Daggerford for anything |
| **FEAR** | Being the Reeve who lost the village. Not death — *record* |
| **RESOURCE** | The **labour levy** — 20 volunteers at Unity 4. Every WP02 §15 project needs her. Also the moot chest and the granary key |
| **ATTITUDE** | Neutral. Moves on **results**, not on courtesy |

**Objective this tenday.** Get the ring gaps at least talked about at moot without the quarry households walking out.

**Knows.** Who owes what to whom. The terms of Greybank's stone contract with Daggerford. That the village cannot survive a bad harvest and a bad winter in the same year.

> **Secret S1.** The moot chest is **9 gp short.** She covered a shortfall on last year's Daggerford accounting out of it and has not replaced it. It is small, it is stupid, and it would end her. *Surfaces if:* anyone audits the chest, Halvard asks the right question, or she has to open it publicly in a crisis.

**Relationships.** Daughter of **Halvard Vell** (§6.9), whose decline she manages privately. Fosters **Pell** (§6.10). Needs **Kael Durrow's** cooperation and does not have it. Trusts **Enna Fisk** more than she trusts her own judgement about buildings.

| If the party… | She… |
|---|---|
| Helps visibly and collectively | Moves to **Friendly**. Opens the levy without a fight. This is the fastest route to WP02 §15 |
| Ignores the village | Stays Neutral. Treats them as weather |
| Pressures or threatens her | Does not fold. **Wary.** She has already decided she would rather be removed than bullied |
| Commits a crime she learns of | Starts the WP01 §8 investigation herself, properly, and tells them she is doing it |
| Kills Halvard or Pell | **Hostile, permanently.** She will spend the village's last coin on it |

**Tonight.** Writing to Daggerford about the stone shipment, drafting it three times, and not sending it.

**If she dies.** The moot must choose a Reeve — **Unity −1** from the fight over it, and **no §15 project can be organized** until they do. Tam Orrick and Berrick Hone are the candidates and they want different villages.

**In a fight.** She isn't one. She will stand in front of a mob to stop it and she is not remotely capable of stopping it.

## 5.2 Maud Elleth — Herbwife

**Herbwife's cottage (G22) · no bloc · Attitude: Neutral · [CAMPAIGN]**

**Portrait.** A half-elf who looks fifty and is not. Forearms scarred from thirty years of the still. Her hands are always faintly green at the nails and she does not scrub it off.

**Voice.** Asks clinical questions in a flat order and does not react to the answers. Says *"mm"* instead of yes.

**Personality.** Not warm and not unkind — a professional in a village that has no other professionals. She will treat anyone, including someone she has decided she despises, and she will charge them for it afterwards.

| | |
|---|---|
| **WANT** | A dry stone-floored room and a second still — i.e. **the Apothecary** (WP02 §15.5) |
| **FEAR** | Being needed by two people at once. It has happened twice and she lost one both times |
| **RESOURCE** | **Every healing item in Greybank that isn't a potion.** Kits at 1 per 3 days, Trade-independent. Antitoxin. Treatment |
| **ATTITUDE** | Neutral, and slower to move than anyone on the roster |

**Objective this tenday.** Get 110 gp and Ondra's agreement for the apothecary. She has asked twice. She will not ask a third time.

**Knows.** Every scar in Greybank and how it was got. **Which injuries are lied about.** Which households are hungry before they admit it. Herbs of the far bank and the moor edge.

> **Secret S3.** She is **ninety-six**, and the story about coming from Secomber is false. She left somewhere forty years ago and has never once written a letter. *Surfaces if:* a long-lived NPC recognises her, someone checks Secomber, or she is asked directly by someone she has come to trust. **This connects to nothing else in the campaign, by design.**

**Relationships.** No family in Greybank. Closest thing to a friend is **Rosal Tunn**, who sends her food she did not ask for. Has delivered a third of the village. **Old Tarn** dislikes her on principle and comes to her anyway.

| If the party… | She… |
|---|---|
| Funds or builds the apothecary | **Friendly**, permanently, and Greybank's medicine survives a Trade collapse |
| Ignores her | Stays Neutral. Sells at price. Runs out eventually |
| Pressures her for the antitoxin | Sells it and argues the whole time — she wants it *in the village*, not in a pack |
| Lies to her about an injury | She knows. WP01 §7.2 **Moderate evidence** (§4.5) |
| Kills a patient in her care | **Hostile.** She will refuse the whole party treatment and mean it |

**Tonight.** Working the still past midnight with the shutters open, which is why she is one of the two or three people awake at Deep Night (WP02 §16.1).

**If she dies.** **The worst single death in the campaign.** All local medicine production stops. Kits and antitoxin become import-only at Trade ≥2. No replacement inside four sessions without deliberate player effort and a Daggerford Clock ≥4 (WP02 §9.2).

**In a fight.** Will not. Will walk into one afterwards to reach the wounded, including the enemy's.

## 5.3 Ordga Brune — Smith

**Smithy (G5) · Craft · Attitude: Wary · [ADAPTED — shield dwarf]**

**Portrait.** Shield dwarf, forty-ish by dwarven reckoning, with a burn scar the width of a hand up her right forearm that she got badly and treated worse. Leather apron, and a habit of testing a weld with her thumb even when it is cold.

**Voice.** Two registers: monosyllables at the forge, and long precise sentences about metal if you ask her about metal. Never says "probably."

**Personality.** Exacting to the point of obstruction. Will refuse work she does not think will hold, and will not be talked round. Greybank finds her difficult and would be lost without her, and both facts are obvious to her.

| | |
|---|---|
| **WANT** | Enough bar stock ahead of her that she never has to say *"not until the cart comes"* again |
| **FEAR** | Producing something that fails and kills the person carrying it |
| **RESOURCE** | **All metalwork within two days' travel.** Repairs, weapons, ammunition. The chain shirt and two martial weapons (WP02 §11.1) |
| **ATTITUDE** | **Wary.** Armed strangers are people who will want work done fast |

**Objective this tenday.** Finish the militia's billhook repairs before Berrick asks again, and get Vadd to bring charcoal on time for once.

**Knows.** Metal, thoroughly. **That the quarry's old stone is cut in a way no chisel she knows produces** — she has said this out loud twice and been ignored. Who in the village owns what weapon.

> **Secret S6.** She left a Daggerford smithy after a weld failed and a man died. It was not her weld. She has never said so, because saying so requires naming whose it was. *Surfaces if:* a Daggerford NPC recognises her, or the party earns enough trust to ask why a dwarf smith is in a village of 143.

**Relationships.** Apprentice **Wat Dray**, whom she is harder on than she needs to be and intends to make good. Cousin **Vadd**, who hauls and disappoints her. Professional respect for **Enna Fisk** — the only other person in Greybank who builds things that must not fail.

| If the party… | She… |
|---|---|
| Brings her interesting metal or a real problem | Warms fast. **Friendly** — she has nobody to talk shop with |
| Ignores her | Stays Wary and sells at +25% (WP02 §10.6) |
| Demands rush work | **Refuses.** Not negotiable, at any reputation |
| Asks about the quarry stone | **Tells them everything she has noticed.** The best free WP06 lead in the village |
| Hurts Wat | **Hostile.** She has no children |

**Tonight.** At the forge two hours past dark, redoing a repair that was fine.

**If she dies.** **No smithing** — WP01 §14.3, an intact building without a smith provides nothing. Wat manages simple repairs at doubled time, no new production, **no ammunition**. Not replaceable within the campaign.

**In a fight.** Genuinely dangerous with a hammer at close quarters and has no idea how to fight more than one person. Will defend the forge and nothing else.

## 5.4 Sember Dray — Storekeeper

**Store (G4) · Trade · Attitude: Neutral · [CAMPAIGN]**

**Portrait.** Fifties, narrow, spectacles held together at one hinge with wire — an imported object repaired rather than replaced, which tells you everything. Keeps a pencil behind the ear and a second one in the ledger.

**Voice.** Prices things aloud while talking about something else. Ends statements with *"…which is what it is."*

**Personality.** Transactional and not cold. Carries nine households on credit because the alternative is watching them go hungry, and would be embarrassed to have that described as kindness.

| | |
|---|---|
| **WANT** | The Daggerford cart to come on six days instead of eight |
| **FEAR** | Calling in the debts. They know they could not pay, and so does Sember |
| **RESOURCE** | **Imports and Class C goods.** One potion, one kit, two component packages, two ammunition bundles (WP02 §11.1). Credit |
| **ATTITUDE** | Neutral. Strictly commercial until given a reason |

**Objective this tenday.** Get a straight answer from Gedd about whether the cart is coming, and decide whether to order the glass Maud keeps asking about.

**Knows.** **Exactly who in Greybank is desperate**, from the ledger. What things cost in Daggerford. That the two component packages came out of a dead adventurer's kit and that nobody has ever asked about them.

> **Secret S4.** The credit ledger is a **map of the village's private desperation** — nine households, amounts, and how long each has been carrying. Sember has never shown it to anyone, including the Reeve. *Surfaces if:* the store is searched, Sember dies and it is found, or the party earns enough trust to be told about one specific household.

**Relationships.** Raising **Wat** and **Nen**, their late sister's children. Sends Wat to Ordga because the boy has better hands than a shopkeeper needs. Depends on **Gedd Plank** entirely. Quietly carries **Hessa Grail** (§7.1).

| If the party… | They… |
|---|---|
| Spend real money | Neutral, but the potion and packages get *offered* rather than withheld (WP02 §9.4) |
| Ignore the store | Nothing. Sember does not chase custom |
| Steal from the store | **Wary → Hostile**, and Sember tells the Reeve the same hour |
| Ask about the component packages | Sells them cheap and is glad to be rid of them |
| Kill Wat or Nen | **Hostile.** Closes the store to the party permanently, at Rep 0 or +3 |

**Tonight.** Reconciling the ledger by one candle, moving one household's line back another tenday.

**If they die.** WP02 §9.3 in full: store **CLOSED**, stock sealed inside, imports stop, ordinary goods stop being freely obtainable **even at Trade 4+**, Nen can mind a counter but cannot order from Daggerford. Replacement needs Daggerford Clock ≥4.

**In a fight.** No. Will bar the door and get the children upstairs.

## 5.5 Kael Durrow — Quarry Foreman

**The Pale (G14) · Quarry bloc · Attitude: Wary · [CAMPAIGN]**

**Portrait.** Forty, grey with stone dust to the point where his actual colouring is a guess, and missing the top joint of two fingers on his left hand. Sleeps at the face more nights than at H5.

**Voice.** Speaks in instructions even when he is not instructing. Long silences that he does not fill and does not expect you to.

**Personality.** Responsible for fourteen men in a hole in the ground and behaves accordingly. Not hostile — **occupied**. He has decided the party is a variable, and he does not like variables near his face.

| | |
|---|---|
| **WANT** | To keep cutting. Expansion means the crew is paid through winter |
| **FEAR** | **The lowest bench.** He will not say this out loud and will change the subject |
| **RESOURCE** | **Stone Loads. Quarry access. Six charges of blasting powder** (WP02 §11.1) |
| **ATTITUDE** | **Wary** from the first meeting |

**Objective this tenday.** Get Ondra to back the expansion at moot, and get his nine militia-roll men *off* the roll for the duration.

**Knows.** Stone. The quarry's whole face. That the old masonry runs further than the cut has reached. **That the boards on the aperture are loose** — he checks them.

> **Secret S2.** He opened the boards once, alone, at the end of a shift, and went in about ten feet with a lantern. He came out, boarded it tighter, and told nobody. **He does not know what is in there.** He knows the air moved. *Surfaces if:* the party opens the aperture and he learns of it; if expansion is approved and he has to give the order; or if someone asks him a direct question at the face rather than in the village.

**Relationships.** Rivalry with **Berrick Hone** over the same eighteen men (WP02 §8.3) — **not** enmity; they drink together and disagree in public. Employs **Dov Marrek** and the bunkhouse six through **Corm Iddick**. Owes **Hessa Grail** something he cannot pay.

| If the party… | He… |
|---|---|
| Bring him stone-work or expansion backing | **Neutral → Friendly.** Access to the face, and eventually the powder |
| Ignore the quarry | Relieved. Stays Wary |
| Go to the aperture | **Wary → Hostile fast.** He will physically stand in the way once |
| Open the boards and survive | Tells them about the ten feet. **This is WP06's doorway, and it is bought with competence, not charm** |
| Get a quarryman killed | Hostile. Fourteen men agree with him |

**Tonight.** At the face after the crew has gone, with a lantern, not working.

**If he dies.** Quarry output halves; the crew works on without direction. Expansion becomes impossible. **The powder store loses its only responsible keeper** — WP07 and WP08 should treat that as a live hazard.

**In a fight.** Strong, practical, and armed with quarry tools. Fourteen men who do what he says are the real threat.

## 5.6 Berrick Hone — Militia Captain

**Voice.** Cheerful, fast, and interrupts herself to point at a roofline. Answers "can we build X" with a number before a yes.

|---|---|
| **WANT** | To build something that is not a repair. **Any** WP02 §15 project |

**In a fight.** Not a fighter, but she is the person who thinks to pull down the adjacent thatch (WP02 §13.3) and she will be right.

## 7.2 Why these six

Each does one structural job:

- **Corm Iddick** makes Reputation −2 survivable (WP02 §17.4).
- **Sabel Quist** is a Level 1 threat signal about the lowest bench that costs the party nothing to find (design doc §6).
- **Hessa Grail** puts a human face on the quarry's cost, and on Sember's ledger.
- **Dov Marrek** and **Alys Penn** make the Hone/Durrow manpower conflict into two specific men rather than a number.
- **Old Tarn** is a daylight witness who is **unreliable** — the campaign's demonstration that testimony has quality (WP01 §7.2), not just existence.

## 7.3 Suspicion candidates

Restating §4.5 with the roster complete. These five acquire suspicion faster than anyone:

**Nissa Crake** (awake at night) · **Pell** (everywhere) · **Old Tarn** (watches the Green, unreliably) · **Maud Elleth** (reads injuries — Moderate evidence) · **Denk Ottersby** (the crossing book).

## 7.4 The 115 unnamed

Deliberate. Design doc §21 caps the roster at 20–30, and the remaining villagers exist as households, not as characters.

**To improvise one at the table:**

1. Take a household from WP02 §8.2 — the cott, the bloc, and the occupation are already fixed.
2. Give them **one want and one physical detail.** Nothing else.
3. **If they recur twice, name them and add them to §3.2.** If they recur three times, promote them to Tier 2.

> **Do not name a villager to fill a gap in the roster.** Name them because the players asked twice. WP04 and WP08–WP12 each need room to introduce someone, and a roster of 34 named villagers leaves them none.

## 8.4 The three standing tensions

None of these require WP04. They are already running on day one.

1. **Hone vs Durrow — eighteen men, two claims.** Nine of the militia roll are quarry crew. Every drill costs stone; every shift costs readiness. Both men are right. They drink together.
2. **Orrick vs Durrow — twenty bodies, one harvest.** The levy for any WP02 §15 project takes hands off the fields six weeks before harvest.
3. **The village vs the bunkhouse.** Six Daggerford men sleep outside the ring, hear no bell, join no muster, and are counted in nobody's strength. Corm Iddick speaks for them and answers to a wage, not a village.

> **WP04 inherits these.** It does not need to invent a reason for Greybank to be divided; it needs to decide what the division *does* at each Greybank Reaction stage.

Naming whose weld it was is a Daggerford problem |

**Rules for running them:**

1. **A secret is not a quest.** Nobody in Greybank is waiting to be unmasked.
2. **Surfacing is a scene, not a check.** No secret is behind a single Insight roll — design doc §23.
3. **Three of the seven change nothing mechanically** (S3, S6, and S7 unless WP04 claims it). That is correct. A village where every secret is load-bearing is a puzzle box, not a place.
4. If the party never finds any of them, **nothing is lost.** They are texture and leverage, in that order.

## 12.2 Rules

Per WP01 §5.11 and §13:

1. **The service stops the moment they die.** Not at the next dawn.
2. **Physical stock remains** where WP02 §11.1 put it, unless stolen or destroyed.
3. **No replacement appears automatically.** Somebody must do something.
4. **Improvisation requires established skill.** Wat can hammer because he is an apprentice. Yorrin cannot compound a remedy because nothing about him says he could.
5. **Dead NPCs stay dead.** They go to G19 and the players walk past it.

## 13.1 Example A — Maud and the antitoxin

**Situation.** Session 2. Greybank Rep **0**. A PC has been poisoned and the party wants the village's only antitoxin.

### A1. What the rules say

- WP02 §11.1: **1 antitoxin**, at G22, **62 gp** at the standing Tight modifier. Purchasable.
- WP02 §9.4 at Rep 0: *"Class B stock exists but is **not volunteered** — the party must ask for specific items."*
- Maud's attitude: **Neutral**, and §5.2 says she is the slowest on the roster to move.

**So: she sells it.** No check, no scene required. That is the honest answer and the DM should give it.

### A2. What makes it a scene anyway

Her **FEAR** is being needed by two people at once. Her **WANT** is the apothecary. Selling the village's only antitoxin to four people who are leaving town collides with both.

She sells, and she argues the whole time — §5.2's reaction row. What she is arguing for is not the coin. It is that the antitoxin should be **in the village**, and she will say so plainly:

> *"And when the Ruddock boy takes a bite at the fold, what do I tell his mother? That you had somewhere to be?"*

### A3. What the party can do with that

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| Pay and go | Antitoxin **1 → 0**. Replenishes at **1 per 6 days** while she lives and the still works (WP02 §11.3). Attitude unchanged |
| Pay, and fund the apothecary | §15.5: 110 gp, 5 days. **Maud → Friendly permanently.** Medicine survives any future Trade collapse |
| Argue her down on price | Succeeds, and she remembers. Attitude unchanged, but she volunteers nothing later |
| Take it without paying | Theft, at G22, north edge, nearest neighbour 90 yards. Per WP02 §16.2 that is **Weak** evidence at night — **and she is one of the 2–3 people awake at Deep Night.** She knows |

### A4. The point

Nothing was gated. The scarcity did the work. The party got what they asked for and were shown the cost of having it, and WP02 §15.5 was put in front of them without a quest hook.

## 13.2 Example B — Ordga Brune is killed

**Situation.** Session 3. The smith dies — a raid, a fight the party lost, or something they did.

### B1. Immediate state

| System | Change |
|---|---|
| NPC | Ordga Brune → **DEAD** (WP01 §13.2) |
| Infrastructure | Smithy **INTACT** — and per WP01 §14.3, **an intact building without a smith provides nothing** |
| Service | Metal repair, new weapons, **all ammunition production: stopped this moment** |
| Stock | **1 chain shirt, 2 martial weapons** remain physically in the smithy (WP02 §11.1) |

### B2. Who tries

**Wat Dray**, sixteen, her apprentice. Per WP02 §9.1 he manages **simple repairs at doubled time, no new production, no ammunition.**

He also, per §8.3, is the reason **Ordga would have gone Hostile** had it been the other way round — and now he is a boy with a forge he cannot run and a master he has just buried.

### B3. Second order, over six days

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Berrick Hone's billhooks are still on her bench. He collects them himself and says nothing |
| 2 | **Ammunition replenishment stops.** The 1-per-2-days line in WP02 §11.3 is dead. Whatever bundles exist are all there will be |
| 3 | Sember has bar stock on order with nobody to work it. **Trade unaffected; the bar stock is now just weight** |
| 4 | Vadd Brune stops fetching charcoal. Nobody has told him to. Ib Sallow's clamps back up |
| 6 | Wat is working the forge alone at night, badly, and Sember wants him back at the counter |

### B4. State changes, honestly

| System | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Critical stock | **Ammunition: no further replenishment** | WP02 §11.3 |
| Critical stock | Martial weapons: no further production | WP02 §11.3 |
| Equipment | Damaged metal gear **cannot be reliably repaired**; metal armour **freezes at current stock** | WP01 §5.7 |
| Defense | **No immediate change** | The militia's weapons still exist. Defense falls later, when they break |
| Greybank Rep | −1 **only if the party is held responsible** | Attitude tracks belief |
| Unity | **No change** | WP01 §3.4 — a death, not a collective failure |

Note what did not happen: no cascade. WP01 §16's two-link limit holds, and the real cost arrives as **a series of specific situations**, exactly as WP01 Example C prescribes.

### B5. Replacement

WP02 §9.2: a Daggerford smith relocating, at **Daggerford Clock ≥4**. Not within the campaign unless the party spends sessions making it happen. If they do, that is a legitimate and expensive victory.

## 13.3 Example C — Getting the shrine potion

**Situation.** The store's potion is gone. Someone is dying. The party knows there is a second one in the shrine strongbox.

### C1. What stands in the way

Per WP02 §11.1, the shrine potion is **not for sale**. It is the village's emergency reserve, and it needs **the Reeve's consent** — or theft.

This is a **scene**, and the roster tells you exactly how it goes.

### C2. Yorrin Fell

He holds the strongbox and will not open it. Not out of hostility — §6.5 gives his want as *"a priest, an actual one"* and his fear as *"burying a child and having nothing to say that helps."* The potion is the only thing in his keeping that has ever actually saved someone.

He sends them to Ondra. He always sends them to Ondra.

### C3. Ondra Vell

§5.1: **Neutral**, and *"moves on results, not on courtesy."* Her fear is being the Reeve who lost the village.

| Party's position | Her answer |
|---|---|
| Rep **+1** or better, and they have done something for Greybank | **Yes**, and she opens it herself |
| Rep **0**, no history | *"And when it's one of ours?"* — **not a refusal, a negotiation.** She wants something in exchange that stays in the village |
| Rep **0**, and they offer to replace it | **Yes.** A potion ordered through Sember at Trade ≥3 is a real, slow, expensive promise (WP02 §11.3) — and she will hold them to it |
| Rep **−1 or below** | **No.** And she will have Yorrin move it |
| They threaten her | §5.1: **she does not fold.** Attitude → Wary. She would rather be removed than bullied |

### C4. If they take it

Theft from a shrine. Per WP02 §14.5 this is the **Desecrated** state:

> **Unity −1 · Greybank Reputation −1 · Yorrin Fell → Hostile, permanently**

And the village's emergency reserve is gone the next time a child goes under a cart.

### C5. Why this is the right shape

A purchase would have been a transaction. Because WP02 placed the second potion behind consent rather than coin, an item the party could have bought in any other campaign becomes **a conversation with two people who have reasons** — and the party's reputation is finally spending itself on something they wanted.

---

## 13.4 Example D — Nissa Crake saw something

**Situation.** The party does something at Deep Night in the Cotts that they would rather nobody knew about.

### D1. Was there a witness?

WP02 §16.2: the Cotts, any watch — *"someone always hears. Walls are cob; lanes are 6 ft."* At night that is **Moderate** evidence.

And §4.5 names Nissa Crake as **Greybank's only reliable night witness.** She is awake, at the window, per her tonight line.

**Decide once whether she saw, and do not revisit it.** That is WP02 §17.1's ruling and it applies here.

### D2. She saw

Her suspicion goes to **Uneasy** (WP01 §13.3). Not Suspicious — she saw movement and a shape, and she is a woman who says *"did you hear that?"* about things nobody heard, and knows people discount her.

Her want is **to be believed the first time.** Her fear is **sleeping through it.** Both push her to speak.

### D3. What happens next, without the party doing anything

| Watch | Event |
|---|---|
| Dawn | She tells Berrick Hone. He half-listens; he is cleaning bows |
| Morning | She tells Ondra Vell, who **writes it down** — §5.1's portrait detail is now a mechanic |
| Afternoon | Nothing. It is one report from a woman known for hearing things |
| **When the crime is discovered** | Ondra's note exists. Investigation opens at **Stage 2–3** rather than Stage 1 (WP01 §8) |

### D4. If the party tries to manage it

| Approach | Result |
|---|---|
| Befriend her | Her attitude is already **Friendly**. She will tell them what she saw, unprompted — including that she told the Reeve |
| Discredit her | Easy, and it works: Berrick and Old Tarn both dismiss her routinely. **Suspicion → Suspicious**, because she knows when she is being handled |
| Threaten her | **Suspicious → Convinced**, and she goes to Ondra the same watch. WP01 §8 — failed concealment accelerates investigation |
| Kill her | Night witnesses drop to 0–1 (§12.1). A second body, and Berrick Hone loses the only other person in Greybank who can shoot |

### D5. The point

The party's problem is not a roll. It is a specific woman with a specific reason to talk and a specific reason nobody listens to her — and **the ways of handling her are all worse than the ways of not needing to.**

# 14. Edge Cases and Adjudication Guidance

## 14.1 The party adopts someone as a mascot

Usually Pell, sometimes Wat. Let them. Then apply §8.3 honestly: **that person now has someone who goes Hostile if they die**, and the party has handed the world a lever. Do not use it cheaply, and do not refuse to use it ever.

## 14.2 The party wants a follower

Greybank has almost nobody who can leave. The village's people are its services (§3.3). Adjudicate by **what stops if they go**:

| Candidate | If they leave |
|---|---|
| Wat Dray | Wants to. Ordga forbids it. **Taking him anyway → Ordga Wary** |
| Vadd Brune | Would go. **Forge supply drops** |
| Pell | Would go instantly. **Do not.** Ondra's reaction is §8.3 |
| Dov Marrek, Alys Penn | Plausible. On the militia roll — **muster thins by one** |
| Corm Iddick | For money. Answers to a wage, not the village |

> The right answer is usually *"yes, and here is what stops."*

## 14.3 Romance

Fine, and unglamorous. The village is 143 people who will all know by the Evening watch — **Rosal Tunn** in particular. Consequences are social and reputational, never mechanical bonuses. An NPC in a relationship with a PC moves **one step** on the attitude scale and no further; WP01 §4.6 governs, not affection.

## 14.4 An NPC should know something you did not plan

Check §5–§7's **Knows** lines first. If it is plausibly in one, they know it. If not, ask **who would**, and let the party be sent to that person — a referral is better play than a convenient answer. **Halvard Vell** (§6.9) is the village's search function two days in three, and **Rosal Tunn** knows everything roughly and a day late.

Do not invent a new NPC to hold information. Use §7.4's promotion rule if someone new must exist.

## 14.5 The party kills someone with no witnesses

WP02 §16.2 is the authority, not instinct. The genuine blind spots are **the fold at night** and **the river at any time**. Everywhere else, someone heard something.

Then WP01 §8.6 governs: a murder without witnesses still produces a **missing person**, and Greybank is 143 people who all know who did not come home. **Absence is evidence.** The investigation is slower, not absent.

## 14.6 Attitude and reputation conflict in public

This is the system working. Lisbet Ash greeting the party warmly on a Street where nobody else will look at them is a **scene**, and it costs her something. Play the cost: someone says something to her about it that evening.

Do not resolve the tension. WP01 §4.6 is explicit that the two values are independent.

## 14.7 You forgot to introduce someone who now matters

Introduce them **as someone the party has been walking past**. Greybank is 500 yards across and everyone has been in the taproom. Say so plainly — *"you've bought bread from her twice"* — and move on. Never retcon a relationship, only an acquaintance.

## 14.8 The party asks a villager to fight with them

Nearly everyone says no, and the sheets say why. The exceptions:

| Who | Will fight | Worth |
|---|---|---|
| **Berrick Hone** | If the village is threatened | The most competent villager, and still only that. **May break if alone** — keep that uncertain |
| **Nissa Crake** | Yes, and she can actually shoot | One of two real shots |
| **The militia** | Only at Berrick's word, only for Greybank, **never** beyond the boundary stones | WP02 §12.3's numbers |
| **Kael Durrow's crew** | Only for the quarry | Fourteen men with tools |
| **Ordga Brune** | For the forge, nothing else | Dangerous once, then out of ideas |

> **Nobody in Greybank goes into the Illefarn site.** Not for money, not for friendship. If the party needs a torchbearer, they must import one — which is a WP04 or Daggerford problem.

## 14.9 An NPC should die and you do not want to lose them

Lose them. Design doc §29 forbids retroactively changing facts, and WP01 §13 is built on deaths persisting. Every death on §12.1 has been costed precisely so the DM can afford to let it happen.

If it is genuinely uncertain, use WP01 §13.2's **Injured** or **Missing** — both are real states with real consequences, and neither is a rescue.

## 14.10 The party plays villagers against each other

Encourage it. §8.4's three tensions are live from day one and require no faction machinery. Backing Durrow over Hone, or Orrick over Durrow, gets a party what they want and costs them the other side's attitude. **Unity moves only after a major scene** (WP01 §3.4) — not after every argument they stir up.

## 14.11 A player wants to run a Greybank NPC as a replacement PC

Excellent. §12.3 lists the sources. Two rules:

1. **The NPC's service goes with them.** If a player takes Wat Dray adventuring, the forge loses its apprentice.
2. **Their relationships come too.** A player running Lisbet Ash inherits Corvin, the mill, and the sluice — and the village's opinion of the family.

## 14.12 The party never engages with any of this

Then the roster runs on its tonight lines and the village changes without them. Halvard declines. Maud never gets her apothecary and the kits run down. Berrick never gets his drill. Bessa loses the flock.

Per design doc §22, unused material stays unused. **But the tonight lines still resolve**, and in session 4 the party will be standing in a village shaped entirely by twenty-eight people who got on with it.

# 15. Final Canonical WP03 Rules

## Roster

**28 named recurring NPCs**: 9 Tier 1 full sheets, 13 Tier 2 compact, 6 Tier 3 background. **115 villagers stay deliberately unnamed.** Promote an unnamed villager only when the players ask about them twice (§7.4).

## Format

Every recurring NPC carries WP01 §13.1's compact table fields, WP01 §13.4's four card fields (**want / fear / resource / attitude**), a **voice handle**, and a **tonight line**. WP04's faction NPCs use this same format.

## Attitude

WP01 §4.6's four values — **Friendly / Neutral / Wary / Hostile** — tracked per recurring NPC, independent of faction reputation. **Starting spread: 4 Friendly, 19 Neutral, 5 Wary, 0 Hostile.** Attitude moves one step, and only on something that happened to them or theirs. **Lisbet Ash is the designated demonstration case**, per WP01 §4.6's own example.

## The tonight rule

**Every NPC has a line describing what they do if the party never engages them.** Tonight lines are not hooks, they advance across sessions, and they collide with each other — which is where Greybank's internal conflict comes from without any faction machinery.

## Suspicion

WP01 §13.3's **None / Uneasy / Suspicious / Convinced**, blank for everyone at start, adjudicated against WP02 §16.2's witness table. Five NPCs acquire it disproportionately: **Nissa Crake, Pell, Old Tarn, Maud Elleth, Denk Ottersby.** Maud reading an injury is **Moderate evidence** under WP01 §7.2.

## Secrets

**Seven, none connected to another.** Three change nothing mechanically. A secret is never behind a single check, and if the party finds none of them, nothing is lost.

## Grief

**Seven deaths make someone Hostile permanently** (§8.3). Two are unrepairable: **Pell** and **Wat Dray**, both children. Telegraph that they are children before it matters.

## Death and replacement

Per WP01 §5.11: the service stops immediately, stock remains where WP02 §11.1 put it, no replacement appears automatically, and improvisation requires established skill. **Ordga, Maud, Enna and Rosal have no replacement within four sessions.** Corvin has a complete one — **Lisbet.**

**Enna Fisk has no replacement at all** — not within four sessions, not with a Daggerford clock, not ever. Confirmed canonical (§5.7). No later package may introduce a joiner to soften it. The obligation this creates is to **telegraph her centrality**, not to provide a substitute.

## Standing tensions

Three, live from day one, requiring no faction machinery: **Hone vs Durrow** (eighteen men, two claims), **Orrick vs Durrow** (twenty bodies, one harvest), **the village vs the bunkhouse** (six men outside the ring who hear no bell). WP04 inherits these.

## Boundaries

WP03 moved **no** building, service, key, item or price from WP02. It contains **no faction NPC** — the elves, the Red-Tusk and Daggerford's agents belong to WP04, which uses this document's format.

## Nobody enters the site

**No villager will go into the Illefarn site**, for any inducement. Torchbearers must be imported.

---

# WP03 Completion Criterion

WP03 is complete when the DM can answer, for any named NPC, **"what do they do tonight if the party never speaks to them?"** — and when later packages can answer questions like these without inventing a villager:

- Who in Greybank will tell the party about the shoal? About the ways in? About the quarry's strange stone?
- The party wants the shrine potion. Who says no, and who can say yes?
- Whose death stops construction across the whole village?
- Who is awake at Deep Night, and what does that mean for a crime in the Cotts?
- The party is at Rep −2 and needs to buy arrows. Who will buy for them, and why is he willing?
- Who goes Hostile permanently, and over whom?
- Which NPC knows the party lied about how someone got hurt?
- Who wants the quarry expanded, who wants it stopped, and who has been inside the aperture?
- If the smith dies, who picks up the hammer, and what can they actually do with it?
- Who would a replacement character plausibly be, and who already cares about them?
- Which two villagers are counting on the same nine men?
- What is the cheapest thing the party can do to be liked here?

# Handoffs

## To WP04 — Factions

**Inherit and build on:**

- The **three standing tensions** of §8.4. Greybank is already divided; WP04 decides what the division *does* at each Greybank Reaction stage.
- The **bloc spokesmen**: Kael Durrow (quarry), Tam Orrick (farms), Berrick Hone (militia), Sember Dray (trade, informally), Corm Iddick (the Daggerford six). Old Tarn is the isolationists' temperament with no organisation behind him — **WP04 decides whether that becomes a bloc.**
- **Secret S7** (Denk Ottersby's unrecorded wagon) is unclaimed. WP04 may take it as a Daggerford thread or leave it as a loose end.
- **Secret S4** (Sember's credit ledger) is leverage over nine households — a natural Daggerford or Red-Tusk instrument.

**WP04 owns and must supply:** every faction NPC — the Laughing Hollow envoy, the Red-Tusk speaker, the Daggerford factor. **Use §4 and §10's format**: portrait, voice handle, want/fear/resource/attitude, tonight line. Do not create a second NPC standard.

**Do not:** change any Greybank NPC's service, household or key. Attitudes may move through play; functions are fixed.

## To WP05 — Regional Map

**Knowledge holders you will want:** **Gedd Plank** (the Daggerford road and its people), **Ib Sallow** (the far-bank woods — and he is cut off if the bridge falls), **Bessa Ruddock** (the western grazing and what has been taking livestock), **Corvin Ash** (the river, its levels, and the shoal).

## To WP06 — Illefarn Dungeon

- **Kael Durrow has been ten feet inside** (Secret S2) and does not know what is there. He is the best lead in the village and is bought with competence, not charm.
- **Ordga Brune has noticed the old stone is cut in a way no chisel produces**, has said so twice, and been ignored. The best *free* lead.
- **Sabel Quist** will not work the lowest bench and cannot say why.
- **Yorrin Fell** knows the shrine's foundation course is not villager work and is untroubled by it.
- **No villager will enter the site** (§14.8). If WP06 assumes hirelings, WP06 must import them.

## To WP07 — Encounters

**Who fights, and how well** (§14.8): Berrick Hone and Nissa Crake are the only two who can shoot. Berrick **may break if alone** — keep it uncertain. Ordga is dangerous once. The militia never crosses the boundary stones.

**Civilians in a fight** are WP02 §18.7's business; WP03 adds that the named ones **witness specifically** — §4.5's five are the ones who will be able to describe what happened.

## To WP08–WP12 — Sessions

- **WP08** should put **Bessa Ruddock** in front of the party early: the fold is the designated first Level-2 warning (WP02 §7, G18) and she is its face.
- **Rosal Tunn's common room in the Evening watch** is the intended rumour-delivery route. **Enna Fisk** is the intended discovery route for the WP02 §15 catalogue. **Berrick Hone** gives the tactical briefing on the ways in, unprompted and at length.
- **Halvard Vell** is the village's search function two days in three.
- The seven secrets are session material; **none is required** for the campaign to resolve.
- Tonight lines must **advance** between sessions. A tonight line unchanged in session 3 is a bug.

## To WP13 — Items

**Who wants what:** Ordga wants bar stock. Maud wants glass and reagents. Enna wants tools. Berrick wants anything that shoots. **Sember has two spell-component packages nobody has ever asked about** and will sell them cheap.

## To WP14 — Handouts

**Owed:** the card set of §10, physically produced. **Denk Ottersby's crossing book** with its three-month gap. **Sember's credit ledger** — a genuine handout, since Secret S4's whole content is a list. The militia roll of eighteen names, of which nine are quarry crew.

## To WP15 — Visual Assets

**Owed:** 22 portraits, Tier 1 and Tier 2. Each §5–§6 entry opens with **one memorable physical fact** — the burn up Ordga's forearm, Sember's wired spectacles, Kael grey to the eyebrows, Halvard's chair. Build the portrait around that, not around a costume.

## To WP16 — Dashboard

**No new tracked values.** WP01 §19's NPC block already holds status and unavailable services. WP03 adds only that **attitude and suspicion are tracked per recurring NPC** — both already in WP01 §13.1's compact table, which §3.2 supplies pre-filled.
