# Session 1 — Visual Assets

**Status:** Table document. A **partial WP15 slice** — the style guide plus generation-ready prompts for everything session 1 needs. Not a work package; WP15 supersedes it and owns the full asset list.
**Use:** paste a prompt into an image tool as-is. Each is written to stand alone, with the style block already folded in.
**Judged by:** internal consistency — one visual language across every asset.

---

# 1. The Style Guide

**Everything below is built on this. Do not vary it between assets.**

## 1.1 The look

| | |
|---|---|
| **Register** | Grounded, unromantic, lived-in. **Nothing heroic, nothing gleaming, no glow effects.** This is a working village that cuts stone for money |
| **Medium** | Ink line with restrained watercolour wash. Visible linework. **Not painterly, not photoreal, not cel-shaded** |
| **Palette** | Pale limestone grey-white · wet slate · weathered oak brown · dull iron · lichen green · one muted ochre. **Desaturated throughout** |
| **The one accent** | **Illefarn stone is the only clean thing in the campaign** — a pale, jointless, faintly warm off-white with no tool marks. Wherever it appears it should read as *wrong for this place* |
| **Light** | Overcast north light. Low sun for exteriors. **Interiors are lit by one fire and one candle, and the corners are dark** |
| **Weather** | Late summer, six weeks before harvest. Dry, dusty, everything faintly white with limestone dust |
| **Faces** | Ordinary. **Nobody is beautiful and nobody is grotesque.** Tired, weathered, specific |
| **Avoid** | Fantasy armour, capes, glowing runes, magical effects, tavern-fantasy clutter, cobblestone streets, anything gothic |

## 1.2 The negative prompt — append to every portrait and scene

```
no glowing effects, no magic auras, no fantasy armour, no capes, no ornate
weapons, no heroic posing, no cinematic lens flare, no photorealism,
no anime, no cel shading, no bright saturated colours, no clean clothing,
no perfect teeth, no modern faces, no text, no watermark, no signature
```

## 1.3 Consistency anchors

**If your tool supports reference images or a seed, lock these three first and generate everything else against them:**

1. **The limestone.** Pale, dusty, slightly warm grey-white. It is in every exterior.
2. **Illefarn stone.** Jointless, no tool marks, faintly warmer than the limestone, subtly *smoother than it should be*.
3. **One face.** Generate **Rosal Tunn** first and use her as the style reference for every other portrait — she sets the register.

# 2. Portraits

**WP15's rule: each portrait is built on that NPC's one memorable physical fact.** That fact is in bold in every prompt. If the image loses it, regenerate.

**Common frame for all portraits:** *waist-up, three-quarter view, plain neutral background, ink-and-wash illustration, overcast light.*

# 3. Maps

## 3.1 DM Village Map — **the priority asset**

**Spec: WP02 §5, honoured in full.** A3 minimum, portrait. Village core occupies the central 60%.

```
Top-down illustrated map of a small stone-cutting village on a river's north
bank, ink-and-wash cartography style, muted watercolour on cream paper,
hand-drawn cartographer's feel with a fine ink line. Late summer, everything
dusted pale with limestone.

LAYOUT, north at top:
- A river ("the Marrow") running west to east-south-east across the lower
  third. A stone-piered timber bridge crossing it at centre-south, with a
  small stone toll-shed on the SOUTH bank at the bridge foot.
- A single main street running east-west just north of the river, lined both
  sides with buildings: a general store with a SLATE roof, a two-storey
  taproom with THATCH, an open-fronted smithy under a slate lean-to, and a
  wagonyard with a thatched hay loft at the east end.
- North of the street, an open green sixty yards across with a stone-collared
  well and an eight-foot timber bell post. A long stone hall with a slate
  roof on its north side, a small open-fronted stone shrine on its west side,
  and a small timber shed with a slate roof twenty yards off it.
- North of the green, twenty-two small cob-and-thatch cottages in four short
  irregular narrow lanes, eaves nearly meeting overhead.
- West end: a water mill on a stone sluice, thatched, with a raised stone
  granary on staddle stones beside it, and a thirty-foot timber wharf with a
  single flat-bottomed skiff.
- North-west, outside the village and uphill, a stepped white limestone
  quarry with three benches, a crane derrick, spoil heaps, and a long
  thatched bunkhouse beside the haul road.
- North on a low knoll: a squat round stone tower, thirty feet, and a small
  walled burial ground below it.
- West, outside the village: drystone sheepfolds and three timber byres.
- East: open arable fields.
- A BROKEN, incomplete low earth bank encircling the village, with four
  obvious gaps in it.

STYLE: muted greys, browns, lichen greens, cream paper. Fine ink linework,
light watercolour wash. Buildings drawn in slight three-quarter relief.
No text labels, no legend, no compass rose, no grid.
```

**Then annotate by hand or in an editor** — the tool will not do these reliably:

1. Labels **G1–G22** on all 22 keyed locations.
2. The four ring gaps lettered **NW / N / E / SE**.
3. All **seven entry routes** drawn to the map edge, including **the shoal** and **the wharf**.
4. The **six chokepoints** with a distinct symbol and their **width in feet**.
5. **Sightline arcs** from the tower and the quarry lip.
6. A **50-yard grid**.
7. **Roof material per building — thatch hatched, slate plain.** *Fire behaviour depends on this; it is not decoration.*
8. **Water depth at the bridge (9 ft)** and the shoal with its seasonal note.
9. Household numbers **H1–H34**.
10. The quarry face with **the aperture marked**, flagged *"Illefarn Stage I entry."*
11. A margin strip carrying the **§3.3 starting-state block**.

**Must not show** anything beyond the boundary stones — the map ends 400 yards out with route labels only.

## 3.2 Player Village Map — **hand out in the first watch**

**Spec: WP02 §6. It is deliberately worse.** A4, hand-drawn feel, as though a villager sketched it for a stranger.

```
A crude hand-drawn map of a small river village, sketched in brown ink on
cream paper by an unpractised hand, with a single pale wash. Deliberately
rough and slightly out of proportion. Uneven lettering.

SHOWS ONLY: a river across the bottom with a bridge; one main street with a
handful of buildings drawn as simple boxes with pitched roofs; an open green
with a well; a cluster of small cottages; a water mill at the west end; a
road climbing north-west to a quarry drawn as three stepped lines; a small
round tower on a hill to the north; rough field boundaries to the east; a
simple compass rose.

LABELLED, in the same rough hand: store, taproom, smithy, mill, shrine,
Moot Hall, quarry. At the south edge: "to Daggerford - 2 days".

STYLE: amateur, charming, imprecise. Ink line and one wash only. No grid,
no scale bar, no symbols legend, no decorative border.
```

**Must omit** (players earn these): the four ring gaps *as gaps* · the shoal · the south-east riverside path · the skiff · the quarry aperture · household numbers · chokepoints and sightlines. **No travel times printed.**

> Hand it out without comment and let them annotate it. By session 3 their own marks should be the campaign's best record of what they have done to the place.

## 3.3 Illefarn Stage I — S1–S4

**The only part of WP06's four stage maps session 1 needs. DM copy only — there is no player map of the interior.**

```
Top-down dungeon map, four small connected chambers, ink-and-wash
cartography on cream paper, precise fine linework.

- A wide cut ramp descending at a shallow grade from an opening at the top
  of the map, wide enough for two carts abreast, forty feet long.
- The ramp opens into a long hall, eighty feet by forty, with stone benches
  running down both long walls and a row of pegs cut into the wall above
  each bench.
- Off the hall's west side, a small circular chamber, perfectly round, empty
  but for a shallow recess in the wall at chest height.
- Off the hall's east side, a smaller round chamber with a single stone
  table and a shallow round basin on it. A door with a warped shutter.
- Two further exits from the hall lead off the bottom of the map into
  darkness, both closed.

The stonework is JOINTLESS and seamless throughout - no visible blocks, no
mortar lines, no tool marks, pale warm off-white, subtly smoother than stone
should be. This is the defining visual feature.

STYLE: muted, cold, precise. No furnishings, no rubble, no bones, no
decoration. The emptiness is the point. No text, no grid, no legend.
```

**Annotate:** sectors **S1–S4** with empty state boxes · **★2 systems** (System 1 in the round chamber, System 2 at the basin) · **S4 marked "Locked — key in S2, or prise shutter = BREACHED permanently"** · **S5 barrier marked "2 routes"** · exits highlighted with the surface zone each reaches.

# 4. Scenes

**Optional but high-value. Two of these carry most of session 1's atmosphere.**

## 4.1 The quarry face and the aperture — **after the collapse**

```
Illustration, ink and restrained watercolour, overcast daylight. The lowest
bench of a stepped white limestone quarry. Fifteen feet of the working face
has collapsed outward into a heap of broken pale rubble, dust still hanging
in the air. Behind the fallen face, exposed: a wide cut ramp descending into
darkness, its lintel made of a pale, JOINTLESS, seamless stone with no tool
marks at all - visibly not the same material as the quarried limestone, and
visibly much older. Splintered timber boards lie among the rubble where they
were torn away. A quarryman's dropped bar and a lantern. Desaturated palette,
pale limestone dust over everything. No figures.
```

## 4.2 The Watch Chamber

```
Illustration, ink and restrained watercolour. A small perfectly round
underground chamber of pale, jointless, seamless stone with no visible blocks
or tool marks. In the centre, a low stone table bearing a shallow round basin
of dark, absolutely still liquid. Faint soft light rises from the basin -
the only light in the room - suggesting six small rings arranged in an arc
on its surface, three of them dark. Cold, dry, silent, entirely empty
otherwise. Two thousand years of nothing. Desaturated blue-grey palette with
one warm accent from the basin. No figures, no rubble, no decoration.
```

## 4.3 The fold at night

```
Illustration, ink and restrained watercolour, night scene. Low drystone
sheepfolds and three timber byres on rough western grazing, lit only by a
single lantern hanging on a post. A section of the drystone wall has its TOP
COURSE broken outward and scattered. In the wet grass beyond, a wide drag
furrow leads away uphill into the dark. Two sheepdogs stand close to the
lantern, refusing to go further. Deep blue-grey night palette, one warm
lantern accent. Isolated, unlit, and a long way from anywhere.
```

## 4.4 The Sundered Cart, Evening

```
Illustration, ink and restrained watercolour, interior. The common room of a
small stone-and-timber taproom, thirty by twenty-five feet, low beams, a
central open hearth. Full: perhaps twenty ordinary working people, farmers
and quarrymen, dusty, tired, mid-conversation. A broad older woman working
behind a plank bar with a cloth over one shoulder. In a corner, apart, a
well-dressed woman travelling alone with her hands still and visible on the
table, watching the room pleasantly. Warm firelight, dark corners, thatch
visible through the ceiling boards. Desaturated palette with a strong warm
hearth accent.
```

## 4.5 The Watch-Glass handout image

**This is the player handout, not a scene. No text of any kind on it.**

```
A single simple image on plain cream card, no border, no text, no labels.
Six soft rings of pale warm light arranged in an incomplete ARC that opens
toward the upper left - not a circle, a broken curve. THREE of the six rings,
the three at the open upper-left end of the arc, are DARK - visible as rings
but unlit. Two small separate marks sit apart from the ring line: one near
the inner focus of the arc, one at its far end. Nothing else. No compass, no
scale, no writing, no decoration. Quiet, faintly beautiful, and completely
uninformative.
```
