The expectation

The single most likely end state is Food 3, Greybank Reaction 2, Greybank Rep +1, Illefarn Instability 1, sectors S1–S4 Discovered, and the Watch-Glass in somebody's pack. Write that down as the expectation and be unsurprised by anything either side of it. WP08 §3.4

Levelling

Level 3 → 4, unconditionally, at the end of the session. Milestone. Announce it as a fact if the players ask. It is not a reward for engagement and must not be presented as one: a party that spent the session drinking in the taproom and talking to Halvard Vell levels with everyone else. WP08 §3.5

The spine, watch by watch

Nine watches. Each block below says what fires and whether it is a deadline, what you need to know, what the players are likely to do and what it costs them, and what changes on the dashboard. Deep detail lives in the sections after this one, and each block links into it.

Day 1 · Afternoon — the arrival

Fires: the party arrives over the bridge. The quarry crew comes down the haul road about a watch early and does not talk on the way. Contingent on nothing, but it is not a deadline — it is simply the opening.

The road comes out of the defile and drops toward the water. The bridge is ninety feet of timber deck on three stone piers, eleven feet wide, and on the downstream side there is no parapet at all — just the edge, and nine feet of brown water under it. A stone hut stands at the near end, and the man in its doorway has already counted your wheels. Beyond it the village runs five hundred yards along the far bank, stone and thatch, with water butts standing at every third building.

What you need to know

Open with a place that works. Nothing is wrong yet and nobody asks for help. Five armed strangers in a village of 143 is an event, but Greybank Reaction is 1 — routine governance — so the response is curiosity, not suspicion. Nobody is hostile. Nobody is suspicious. The campaign has three sessions in which to change that.

Name three villagers in the first five minutes and let the party ignore all three. Denk Ottersby is unavoidable at the toll-shed, Pell will find them within a watch, and they need a bed from Rosal Tunn. Someone will ask what they are here for, and the answer goes round the village by Evening.

Do not have anyone ask for help yet. Bessa Ruddock arrives at Dawn on Day 2 and she is worth waiting for. The six beats of the arrival are set out in full below.

Day 1 · Evening — the Sundered Cart

Fires: the taproom fills. Rumour cards R1, R2, R3, R5, R11, R12 are available. Contingent — it happens whether or not the party attends, but they only get it if they are in the room.

The common room is thirty feet by twenty-five with a hearth in the middle of it and most of the village standing round the edges. It is hot, and loud, and it smells of wet wool and hearth smoke. A broad woman in her sixties works the room without stopping, talking the entire time, with a cloth over one shoulder that does not come off. In one corner six men from the quarry are sitting together and drinking slowly, and they are the only quiet thing in the building.

What you need to know

This is the session's one set-piece and it contains no danger at all. It is the watch when the crew is down from the Pale and the day's news is exchanged; roughly fifty people are in the core of the village and a good number of them are in this room.

The room has three jobs. It establishes that Greybank has no money — somebody settles a debt in eggs, and everyone knows Sember carries nine households and nobody says it. It establishes that the party is now the village's business. And it puts the quarry in the air without explaining it: six quiet men, Durrow arriving late from the face, and a stranger asking pleasant questions about the lowest bench. Three unconnected observations that the players will connect tomorrow morning.

Who is in the room, and Serina Malk, in full below.

Day 1 · Deep Night — the village is asleep

Fires: nothing. Two or three people in Greybank are awake: Nissa Crake, Maud Elleth at her still, and sometimes Denk Ottersby at the wharf.

The fold and the far bank are unwatched entirely. A party out at night is genuinely alone — which is worth saying out loud once, because it is also true of anything else that is out there. If a crime happens in this watch, evidence quality is Weak, and Nissa Crake is the only witness in the village who might contradict that.

Day 2 · Dawn — Bessa Ruddock and the ewe

Deadline 1 of 3

Bessa Ruddock comes onto the Green with a dead ewe on a hurdle and a slate with a tally on it. This fires regardless of the party — unless they were at the fold and stopped it. WP08 §12.1

There is a hurdle set down on the turf of the Green with a dead ewe on it, and a woman in her fifties standing beside it with two dogs sitting at her heel. She is not making a scene. People are going past on their way to work and some of them are slowing down. The ewe's back is opened from the shoulder, and there is turf and grit worked into the wool along one whole side of her.

She reports. She does not ask.

“Four in eleven days. That's the fourth. Something went over the wall, not through it, and it took her uphill. I'd like somebody to come and look before it's a story instead of a warning.” WP08 §5.1

What you need to know

She talks to people the way she talks to dogs — short, clear, not unkind. She has a count and she has physical evidence, and she is this campaign's Level 2 warning with a person attached.

Bessa Ruddock does not ask twice. If the party brushes her off, she says “right” and goes back to the fold, and she stays there at night, alone, with two dogs, 260 yards outside the ring. Honour the choice and let her be right. Do not have her come back a third time.

Day 2 · Morning — the bench collapses

Deadline 2 of 3

Fifteen feet of the lowest bench at the Pale gives way, into a void nobody knew was there. Dov Marrek is under it, conscious, and the crew can hear him. Contingent on nothing. It has been coming for two years. WP08 §12.1, §7

The noise arrives before anything else does — a long grinding crack from the north, up at the quarry, and then a sound like a cart tipping that goes on for much longer than a cart would. Dust comes up over the lip of the cut and hangs there. Nobody in the village rings anything. After about a minute you can see a man running down the haul road, and he is not pacing himself.

What you need to know

Nothing woke up. A quarry cut into a wall until the wall failed, and the wall is two thousand years old and hollow on the far side. Say exactly that if a player asks.

This is the session's centre of gravity, and it is the moment three problems become one problem — which the village did, over two years, on purpose, for money. The full incident, the twenty-five-minute clock, and what the collapse exposes are below.

Day 2 · Afternoon — the aperture is open, and unguarded

Fires: Durrow has to report the collapse to the Reeve, because fifteen feet of face is gone and the shipment will be short. Contingent on nothing, but it happens off-screen unless the party follows it.

Where the face was there is now a hole about fifteen feet across, and behind it the rock does not look like rock. A ramp runs down into the dark at a shallow grade, wide enough for two carts side by side, and the stone it is cut from has no joints in it anywhere you can see. Cold air comes out of it steadily, and it smells like the inside of a well. The nearest quarryman is forty yards away with his back to it.

What you need to know

Durrow posts nobody — he has fourteen men, one of them is hurt, and none of them will go near the lowest bench. No villager will enter the site, ever, for any inducement. The party will not find a guide and should be told so plainly if they ask.

Do not have anyone suggest they go in. The opening must be available and attractive, not assigned. Ordga Brune's “no chisel makes that cut” is the strongest pull in the village, and it is a statement about masonry, not a quest. WP08 §7.6

Day 2 · Evening — the village argues

Fires: the village argues about the quarry, informally, in the taproom and on the Green. Contingent.

The expansion argument has arrived a session early, because the face came down. Durrow needs the crew paid through winter. The waystone line

Somebody must say the old stones should be left alone before anyone is holding a hammer. This is that, and it is deliberately not a warning. Yorrin Fell, at the shrine, if anyone remarks on the foundation course:

“That's older than the village. Older than the wood we put on it. It holds the corner up and it has never asked anything of anybody, so we leave it be.”

He is untroubled by it, and that is the point. A man calmly telling you a thing is ancient and load-bearing lands harder than a man telling you not to touch it. Two other voices disagree: Halvard Vell says the tower's lower courses were here before the village and nobody has ever needed to know why; Ordga Brune says the opposite and wants it looked at.

Three of the six waystones are inside Greybank — the tower's base course, the shrine's foundation, and the north wall of the burial ground. A party that walks the village and looks at stone can find all three in one afternoon, with no check and no magic.

What they must be told before they can be blamed

Session one owes the party all three of these, and delivers them: Yorrin's line; the Watch-Glass — six rings, three dark; and that the tower, the shrine and the burial-ground wall are visibly the same stone as the Anchor Room. After that, what they do with a hammer is theirs. WP08 §9.7

Three things the room should do

1. Establish that Greybank has no money — somebody settles a debt in eggs. 2. Establish that the party is now the village's business; by the end of the watch everyone knows what they said they were here for. 3. Put the quarry in the air without explaining it.

The three problems, and what ignoring them costs

Three, simultaneous, none mandatory, each with a written cost across all four sessions. Each is a door rather than a quest: the fold opens the Red-Tusk thread, the Pale opens the site, the far bank opens the Laughing Hollow. The party may take none of them and the campaign still runs.

P1 — The Fold

Four sheep gone in eleven days. The fold is 260 yards from the Green, outside the ring, unlit, and 26 rounds away at a run — longer at night with no light. At Defense 2 the militia takes twenty minutes to muster. Anything that happens at the fold is finished before the village arrives.

Two things are taking from the fold, and in session one the party can still tell them apart. That is the staging decision, and it matters: from session two both take livestock and the diagnosis gets hard. A party that does the easy work early has bought itself the harder answer.

The quarry employs fourteen of Greybank's eighty-five able adults and is the reason the village exists. Nine of the eighteen militia are quarry crew. At the north end of the lowest bench there is an opening that is not natural and that the crew did not cut. Durrow has boarded it over, told the crew to leave it alone, and the boards are already loose because he checks them.

P3 — The Far Bank

Mundane, mechanical, and the quietest of the three. Its danger is positional. Ib Sallow's charcoal is four days late. He is across the river tending clamps that cannot be left, alone, out of earshot of the bell. Nobody has fetched it because Vadd Brune has been in the taproom instead.

Nothing has happened to Ib. He is fine, he is lonely, and he will talk for an hour if anyone crosses.

The quarry incident, and the twenty-five-minute clock

Morning watch, Day 2. It fires whether or not the party is anywhere near it. The lowest bench has been cut steadily north for two years, and this morning about fifteen feet of face gives way into a void nobody knew was there.

Dov Marrek is under it — twenty-six, on the militia roll, one of the fourteen. Pinned from the thigh down under about four hundredweight of broken limestone. He is conscious, and he is screaming, and the crew can hear him.

The next twenty-five minutes, without the party

Run this as a clock, out loud, and let the party hear it happen. This is the sequence if they are in the village and never come.

What is down there

The ramp goes down at a grade you could push a cart up, forty feet of it, cut square and wide enough for two carts abreast. There is soot on the left-hand wall about ten feet in, in one patch, at the height a man would hold a lantern. Below it there are bootprints in the dust that go a little further and then turn round. Everything past that point is clean, the way a floor is clean when something swept it for a long time and then stopped.

The hall is eighty feet by forty, with stone benches down both long walls and pegs cut into the wall above them. There are fifteen sets of pegs and nothing is hanging on any of them. Under one bench, where somebody set it down, there is a kit — a roll of something that was rope, a stone cup, rations gone entirely to dust. The far wall is cut with names from one end to the other, and the last part of the list is in a different hand, and whoever cut it was in a hurry.

The obligation is telegraphing danger, not preventing bad choices — and searching a room is a quarter of a watch. WP08 §9.3

The second permanent choice — the anchor

S3's recess warms under the hand before anyone swings. That is the warning, it is tactile, and it precedes the blow.

If they force it anyway: 2d6 force damage to every creature in the room, and Illefarn Instability 1 → 2.

At Instability 2, the two waystones outside the village — on Stonecrop Rise and at Wend's Barrow — become warm to the touch and audible at night. Nobody in Greybank will notice for a tenday. The party did that, in session one, in about four seconds.

The Watch-Glass

On a low stone table there is a shallow basin of dark liquid, quite still. Looking into it you see six rings of light arranged in an arc, as though you were looking at something from above and some distance away. Three of the rings are dark. The dark ones are on the side of the arc that faces north-west.

What it isWhat it does
Portable. It comes out of the tableThe party can carry it. About 6 lb, awkward, obviously ancient

Give them the image and refuse to explain it. Six rings. Three dark. Facing north-west. If a player draws it on a napkin, that napkin is the campaign. WP08 §9.5

If a player asks what it means

Do not tell them. Describe it again, precisely, and let them theorise. If they ask an NPC, nobody in Greybank knows — Ordga Brune will look at it for a long time and say the stone is the same. That is the most any villager can give them.

What they miss while they are down there

Durrow reporting the collapse to the Reeve · Maud walking back down the haul road · half the rumour cards, if they linger past the Evening · and the Night watch, when the fold is probed. None of this is a punishment and none of it is retroactive. It is a watch, spent.

The twelve rumour cards

Deal them face up as they come out of conversation. Do not hand over all twelve at once — Rosal Tunn's room delivers five of them in one Evening, which is enough. Ignored cards stay on the table: they are not discarded at end of session, they are the visible reason the players cannot do everything.

The front is what the players get. Everything under it is yours. Three cards are false or misleading — that is not a trick, it is what a village of 143 people sounds like.

R1 · true

“Four sheep in eleven days.”

Bessa Ruddock has lost four ewes since the moon turned. She says something goes over the wall, not through it.

Truth: the Moorgaunt. Three-night rhythm, drag trail to the moor. Ignored: another animal and Food −1; then goblins take animals too; then the flock is gone or Bessa is.

R2 · true

“Her dogs won't go out.”

Two nights running, the shepherd's dogs wouldn't leave the cott. They're good dogs.

Truth: this is the goblin half, not the beast half. The dogs will face a predator; they will not face three people who throw stones. Merges into R1 from session two.

R3 · true

“Six of the quarry men have gone quiet.”

Not one — six. The bunkhouse men. They come down early and they don't drink.

Truth: Sabel Quist will not work the lowest bench and cannot say why, and it has spread through the six. Resolves itself on Day 2 whether or not anyone acts.

R4 · true

“The stone at the Pale isn't cut right.”

Ordga Brune says no chisel makes that cut. She's said it twice. Nobody asked what she meant.

Truth: Illefarn masonry — the tower base, the shrine foundation and the burial-ground wall are the same. Ignored: nothing decays. It is simply still true in session four, which is its own kind of indictment.

R5 · true

“There's no charcoal.”

Four days late. Vadd was meant to fetch it and Vadd has been here every night.

Truth: Ib Sallow is fine, and alone, and out of earshot of the bell. Ignored: it arrives late and Vadd is shouted at — unless the aperture was opened, in which case there is a mark on a tree at the camp.

R6 · true

“The Reeve won't put the ring to the moot.”

Enna Fisk has costed the gaps three times. Forty gold and eight loads and six days. It never gets asked.

Truth: unfair to Ondra Vell — she will not raise it while the quarry households would walk out. Ignored: Defense stays 2 for the entire campaign, which is the single largest predictor of how session four goes.

R7 · true

“Nissa heard something at the fold.”

The bell-warden was up in the small hours and says she heard it. Nobody went.

Truth: she is Greybank's only reliable night witness. Ignored: she keeps being right and keeps not being believed — and in session three the party will have taught themselves not to listen.

R8 · Day 2 only · true

Fifteen feet of it. Dov Marrek's under the stone. There's a hole behind it and the air's coming out.

Truth: the collapse. Everyone has it within a watch. Ignored: the village handles it, and Stone Loads halve until the bench is shored — and there is no timber to shore it with.

R9 · half true

“The village was never meant to be here.”

He says the quarry was somebody else's before it was ours and they left it for a reason.

Truth: half true, and Old Tarn does not know which half. Nobody left the quarry for a reason — but the stone is somebody else's, and he is accidentally correct about the part that matters.

Truth: four silver, three months ago, and he does not know whose wagon. Ignored: nothing happens. It is here because a village should contain things that do not resolve.

A cart went through two winters back. It was never properly rebuilt.

Truth: anyone shoved or knocked prone within 5 ft of that edge risks a 20 ft fall into 9 ft of opaque water. Ignored: nothing, until there is a fight on the bridge. Then it is the most important fact in the village.

If the party does none of this

Ending the session

Run these nine steps in front of the players. Target ten to fifteen minutes. Doing it openly is the point: it is how the players learn that the state is real and that they moved it.
