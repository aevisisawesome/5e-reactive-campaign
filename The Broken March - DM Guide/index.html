The Broken March

Interactive DM Guide

Everything you need to run session one of a four-session reactive sandbox for D&D 5e, set in and around a village called Greybank, two days from Daggerford. Levels 3 to 5 or 6. Written for a DM who has never seen any of this before.

This guide is offline. There is no server, no build step and no network request in it — you can copy the folder to a laptop with the wifi off and it works. It is also not the source of truth: it is a doorway into the campaign documents, with a scratchpad attached. Where the guide and a work package disagree, the work package is right.

Start with the Introduction. Part 0 is two or three screens and it is the part that makes the rest make sense. Everything else in here is reference you can reach for at the table.

A scratchpad for the numbers you have to carry between sessions. It autosaves into this browser, but that is convenience only — some browsers refuse to store anything for a page opened from a folder, silently. Export the file. That is the mechanism that actually moves your campaign from one machine to another.

Entity links and previews

Names of people, places, factions, items and campaign terms link themselves. Hover one, or tab to it, and a card shows you who or what it is without moving you off the page. Click it and you go to the full entry; the browser's Back button, and the control that appears in the corner, bring you back to exactly where you were reading.

So: Bessa Ruddock keeps the sheep at the Fold, 260 yards outside the ring, and she is the first person in the campaign to tell the party that something is wrong. Kael Durrow runs the Quarry and does not want strangers near his face. Berrick Hone commands the militia, such as it is, at Defense 2. In the Evening watch everyone who matters is in the Sundered Cart. Under the quarry, a thing called the Watch-Glass has been working without interruption for two thousand years, and Illefarn Instability is the only clock that measures how much you have disturbed it. Out past the moor edge, the Red-Tusk are deciding something. West of the river, Laughing Hollow is watching and saying nothing.

Each name links once per section. That is deliberate — a page that links every occurrence of every name is unreadable. The second mention of Bessa Ruddock in this paragraph is plain text, and so is this one: Bessa Ruddock.

Read-aloud

Text to read at the table sits in its own box, in the body face, so you can find it at a glance. It describes only what the documents fix, never states a clock or a number the players have not earned, and never assumes what the party does. Names are not linked inside it — you should be able to read it straight down.

The road comes down off the ridge and the village is in front of you all at once — five hundred yards of it, end to end, on the north bank of the river. Pale limestone in every wall, and dust of the same colour worked into the road. A cart track climbs the hillside to the right toward a stepped cutting in the rock, and the sound of iron on stone carries down from it. Closer in, an old earth bank runs around the houses, grassed over and slumped, with a gap in it wide enough to drive a wagon through.

Nimble Escape. Disengage or Hide as a bonus action, every turn.

Sent to Look, Not to Fight. Withdraws the moment it drops below half hit points, no morale roll. Getting away is its objective; it has no other.

Actions
  • Shortsword. +5 to hit, reach 5 ft. Hit: 6 (1d6+3) piercing.
  • Shortbow. +5 to hit, range 80/320. Hit: 6 (1d6+3) piercing.

Morale. 1d6 on any trigger. 1 rout · 2 break · 3–4 waver · 5 hold · 6 harden.

Deadline

Three of these probe the fold on Day 2, Night, whatever the party is doing. Probes are survivable by both sides — a DM who kills a PC with this has broken canon. WP04 §6.2

Placeholders

Where the campaign documents do not yet contain something, the guide says so and names the work package that owes it. It never invents content to fill the gap. Every one of these is collected automatically into the pending-content report in Part 6.

Session one needs only two objects — the Watch-Glass and the resonance key — and both are in this guide. What anything else is worth, and what the party can sell where, is owed by WP13 and is not written yet. Note that Greybank holds roughly 260 gp of liquid coin in total, so the answer for most things is "not here".

Images

The art is generated separately and arrives after the guide is built. Until a file is there, its frame shows you the exact filename it is waiting for and which prompt produces it. Drop the file into assets/img/ under that name and the frame resolves itself — no code changes, and the layout does not shift, because every frame already reserves the right shape.

Callouts, chips and notes

DM only

Nothing at the site under the quarry advances on a timer. It moves when somebody does something to it, and not otherwise. WP06 §12

Worth knowing

The bell does not reach the quarry, the bunkhouse, the fold, the charcoal camp or the outlying farmsteads — where 40 of Greybank's 143 people are during the working day. WP02 §12.4

Source chips mark where material comes from: Lore is Forgotten Realms canon, Adapted is canon bent to fit, and Campaign is invented for The Broken March. Part 0 explains the three once and then they are just chips.
