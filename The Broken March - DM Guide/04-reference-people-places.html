Part 5.1

People & Places

Thirty-four named people, twenty-two places inside Greybank, and eighteen zones around it. This is the page most links in the guide land on. Use the search box, or the jump bars below.

Every NPC entry carries the same six things, because they are the six a DM actually needs mid-sentence: a voice you can perform, what they want, what they fear, what they hold that the party might need, what they are doing tonight, and what breaks if they die.

Read the death lines as a set

Five of these people are load-bearing — Maud Elleth, Ordga Brune, Sember Dray, Enna Fisk and Gedd Plank. Losing any of them removes a service the village cannot replace inside four sessions. Two more deaths cannot be repaired socially at all: Pell, which turns the Reeve permanently Hostile, and Enna Fisk, which costs 50% on every build for the rest of the campaign.

Ondra Vell Reeve of Greybank · G11 · Leadership · Neutral

Portrait. Middle years, plain, and visibly tired in a way she manages rather than shows.

Voice. Moves on results, not on courtesy. Does not fold under pressure, and says so once.

Want
To get Greybank to harvest without asking Daggerford for anything.
Fear
Being the Reeve who lost the village. Not death — record.
Resource
The labour levy — twenty volunteers at Unity 4. Every village project needs her. Also the moot chest and the granary key.
Attitude
Neutral. Moves on results.

Knows. Who owes what to whom. The terms of Greybank's stone contract with Daggerford. That the village cannot survive a bad harvest and a bad winter in the same year.

Tonight. Writing to Daggerford about the stone shipment, drafting it three times, and not sending it.

If they die. The moot must choose a Reeve — Unity −1 from the fight over it, and no village project can be organised until they do. Tam Orrick and Berrick Hone are the candidates, and they want different villages.

Secret. The moot chest is 9 gp short. She covered a shortfall on last year's Daggerford accounting out of it and has not replaced it. It is small, it is stupid, and it would end her. Surfaces if anyone audits the chest, Halvard asks the right question, or she has to open it publicly in a crisis.

Maud Elleth Herbwife · G22 · no bloc · Neutral

Portrait. A half-elf who looks fifty and is not. Forearms scarred from thirty years of the still, and her hands are always faintly green at the nails — she does not scrub it off.

Voice. Asks clinical questions in a flat order and does not react to the answers. Says “mm” instead of yes.

Want
A dry stone-floored room and a second still — the apothecary. 110 gp, 2 Stone, 3 Timber, 5 days, and Ondra's agreement.
Fear
Being needed by two people at once. It has happened twice and she lost one both times.
Resource
Every healing item in Greybank that is not a potion. Kits at one per three days, Trade-independent. Antitoxin. Treatment.
Attitude
Neutral, and slower to move than anyone on the roster.

Knows. Every scar in Greybank and how it was got. Which injuries are lied about. Which households are hungry before they admit it.

Tonight. Working the still past midnight with the shutters open — which is why she is one of the two or three people awake at Deep Night.

If they die. The worst single death in the campaign. All local medicine production stops. Kits and antitoxin become import-only at Trade ≥2, and at Trade ≤1 medicine simply stops. No replacement inside four sessions without deliberate player effort.

In a fight. She will not. She will walk into one afterwards to reach the wounded, including the enemy's.

Ordga Brune Smith · G5 · Craft · Wary

Portrait. Shield dwarf, forty-ish by dwarven reckoning, with a burn scar the width of a hand up her right forearm that she got badly and treated worse. A habit of testing a weld with her thumb even when it is cold.

Voice. Two registers: monosyllables at the forge, and long precise sentences about metal if you ask her about metal. Never says “probably.”

Want
Enough bar stock ahead of her that she never has to say “not until the cart comes” again.
Fear
Producing something that fails and kills the person carrying it.
Resource
All metalwork within two days' travel. Repairs, weapons, ammunition. The chain shirt and two martial weapons.
Attitude
Wary. Armed strangers are people who will want work done fast — and she refuses rush work at any reputation.

Knows. Metal, thoroughly. That the quarry's old stone is cut in a way no chisel she knows produces — she has said this out loud twice and been ignored.

Tonight. At the forge two hours past dark, redoing a repair that was fine.

If they die. No smithing at all. An intact building without a smith provides nothing. Wat manages simple repairs at doubled time, no new production, no ammunition. Not replaceable within the campaign.

Secret. She left a Daggerford smithy after a weld failed and a man died. It was not her weld. She has never said so, because saying so requires naming whose it was.

Sember Dray Storekeeper · G4 · Trade · Neutral

Portrait. Fifties, narrow, spectacles held together at one hinge with wire — an imported object repaired rather than replaced, which tells you everything.

Voice. Prices things aloud while talking about something else. Ends statements with “…which is what it is.”

Want
The Daggerford cart to come on six days instead of eight.
Fear
Calling in the debts. They know they could not pay, and so does Sember.
Resource
Imports and all ordinary goods. One potion, one kit, two component packages, two ammunition bundles. And credit, informally.
Attitude
Neutral. Strictly commercial until given a reason.

Knows. Exactly who in Greybank is desperate, from the ledger. What things cost in Daggerford. That the two component packages came out of a dead adventurer's kit and nobody has ever asked about them.

Tonight. Reconciling the ledger by one candle, moving one household's line back another tenday.

If they die. The store closes, with the stock sealed inside as a village-secured problem. Imports stop, and ordinary goods stop being freely obtainable even at Trade 4+. Nen can mind a counter but cannot order from Daggerford.

Secret. The credit ledger is a map of the village's private desperation — nine households, amounts, and how long each has been carrying. Sember has never shown it to anyone, including the Reeve.

Kael Durrow Quarry foreman · G14 · Quarry · Wary

Portrait. Forty, grey with stone dust to the point where his actual colouring is a guess, and missing the top joint of two fingers on his left hand. Sleeps at the face more nights than at home.

Voice. Speaks in instructions even when he is not instructing. Long silences that he does not fill and does not expect you to.

Want
To keep cutting. Expansion means the crew is paid through winter.
Fear
The lowest bench. He will not say this out loud, and will change the subject.
Resource
Stone Loads. Quarry access. Six charges of blasting powder.
Attitude
Wary from the first meeting. Bought with competence, not charm.

Knows. Stone. The quarry's whole face. That the old masonry runs further than the cut has reached. That the boards on the aperture are loose — he checks them.

Tonight. At the face after the crew has gone, with a lantern, not working.

If they die. Quarry output halves; the crew works on without direction. Expansion becomes impossible. And the powder store loses its only responsible keeper — treat that as a live hazard.

Secret. He opened the boards once, alone, at the end of a shift, and went in about ten feet with a lantern. He came out, boarded it tighter, and told nobody. He does not know what is in there. He knows the air moved.

Berrick Hone Militia captain · H1 / G12 · Militia · Neutral

Portrait. Fifty-one, a field-hand's build going soft, wearing a leather jack indoors more often than the weather justifies. One of two people in Greybank who can actually shoot.

Voice. Over-explains. Gives a three-step plan for a one-step problem, and gets flustered when interrupted.

Want
The ring closed and the tower manned at night.
Fear
Freezing again.
Resource
Militia command. The shed keys, the shield, two ammunition bundles. Eighteen names, of whom eleven turn out promptly.
Attitude
Neutral, and the easiest major NPC to move upward — train the militia and he is Friendly immediately, with Greybank Rep +1.

Knows. The eighteen names, and which nine are Durrow's. Every approach to the village — and, correctly, that three of the seven are unwatched. He has raised it twice.

Tonight. Cleaning the six poor shortbows in the shed, alone, for the third time this tenday.

If they die. The roll exists; the command does not. Muster times increase one step until the moot appoints someone, and the obvious someone is Kael Durrow, who does not want it.

Secret. In the wolf winter, eleven years ago, he ran. Two people died who might not have. Halvard Vell knows, and has never said a word in eleven years.

Enna Fisk Wright · H4 · Craft · Friendly

Portrait. Thirties, sawdust permanently in the weave of everything she owns, and a carpenter's rule tucked in her belt that she uses on things that do not need measuring. The only person who whistles in Greybank.

Voice. Cheerful, fast, and interrupts herself to point at a roofline. Answers “can we build X” with a number before a yes.

Want
To build something that is not a repair. Any village project.
Fear
Watching the village fall down slowly while everyone agrees it should be fixed.
Resource
All competent construction. Without her, every build and repair time is +50%.
Attitude
Friendly from the first conversation. She is the party's way in.

Knows. What every structure in Greybank needs and what it would cost — she can quote the whole catalogue from memory. Which cott roofs will not survive another winter. That the mill wheel's ironwork cannot be replaced locally.

Tonight. On her own doorstep with a rule and a scrap of slate, costing the ring gaps again.

If they die. All build and repair times +50%, permanently. The quarry crew can do rough work; nobody does joinery. This is the death the players will not see coming and will feel for the rest of the campaign.

Confirmed canonical, and deliberate. The obvious softening was a competent joiner on the quarry crew. There isn't one, and there will not be one. A village where every loss has a substitute is a village where none of them do. The obligation this creates is telegraphing, not softening — play her as visibly central to every project, and a party that has worked with her twice will understand exactly what they lost.

Rosal Tunn Taproom keeper · G3 · Trade · Friendly

Portrait. Sixties, broad, forearms like a woman who has moved barrels for forty years, with a cloth over one shoulder that never comes off.

Voice. Talks at people while working, never stopping. Every piece of news arrives inside an unrelated anecdote.

Want
A full room every Evening. It is how she measures whether the village is all right.
Fear
The room emptying. Not poverty — the room emptying.
Resource
The only lodging. And every rumour in Greybank, which passes through her room in the Evening watch.
Attitude
Friendly. Everyone is friendly until they break something.

Knows. Everything, roughly, and a day late. Who is fighting, who is courting, who has stopped coming in.

Tonight. Standing at the end of the bar after the room has thinned, working out from who left early that something is wrong at the Pale.

If they die. No lodging except by household invitation at Rep ≥ +1. Any attempt to persuade the village as a group is one step harder. And the rumour flow the campaign runs on stops.

The cheapest reputation in the campaign. Pay for someone else's meal in front of her. She notices, says nothing, and it goes round the village by morning. Greybank Reputation 0 → +1, for the price of a supper.

Corvin Ash Miller · G6 · no bloc · Neutral

Portrait. Fifties, deaf in the left ear from forty years beside the wheel, flour in the creases of his knuckles. Turns his whole body to listen, which reads as intensity and is just his ear.

Voice. Loud, because of the ear. Says “eh?” and then answers before you repeat it.

Want
To get to harvest with the wheel sound.
Fear
The wheel's ironwork failing — it cannot be replaced locally, and he knows it.
Resource
Flour, and therefore Food. The sluice, which raises the water at the shoal by about eighteen inches.
Attitude
Neutral. Civil to everyone, close to no one outside the mill.

Knows. The river — its levels, its moods, and the shoal — better than anyone. That the mill is loud enough that he sees nothing and hears less during a working watch.

Tonight. Standing in the mill with the wheel stopped, listening to a bearing.

If they die. Lisbet runs the mill — the only true redundancy in Greybank. Food is unaffected. What is lost is the river knowledge, and the man who noticed things at Millside.

Ask him about the river. He tells them about the shoal — one of the seven ways into Greybank, and he is one of only two people who will mention it.

Vaerelin Ossuine Warden-speaker, Laughing Hollow · the far bank · Neutral

Portrait. A moon elf who has been doing this a long time and dresses for weather rather than for meeting anyone. Carries a bow he does not draw. The stillness is the memorable thing — he does not shift his weight while he waits for an answer.

Voice. Answers the question asked, and stops. Long pauses that are not invitations. Never repeats himself.

Want
The site closed, and left closed.
Fear
Being right too late — again.
Resource
The truth about the site, which he will not give below stage 6. Ward-craft. Eight others.
Attitude
Neutral, and it moves on conduct at the site, and nothing else. Courtesy does not affect him. Neither does rudeness.

Knows. What is behind the boards. What happened the last time. That the Red-Tusk were displaced by it, and that this is the clearest evidence anyone has.

Tonight. Somewhere on the far bank with a view of the quarry road, waiting for a reason.

If they die. The Hollow goes to stage 5 immediately, and Nethra's position dies with him — she cannot carry an argument against a martyr. This is the fastest route to armed confrontation in the campaign.

Hook. He gives exactly one explicit warning, and then acts. A party that reads his brevity as hostility will misread the whole faction.

Nethra Warden-scout, Laughing Hollow · Neutral

Portrait. Younger than Vaerelin, and dressed like someone who spends her life within sight of humans without speaking to them. She has a villager's habit of glancing at the tower when she talks, and does not know she does it.

Voice. Asks about people by name — “the one with the ledger”, “the child who runs” — and is embarrassed to be caught knowing them.

Want
To tell Greybank the truth, and be allowed to.
Fear
That Vaerelin is right, and she is about to get a hundred and forty-three people killed by trusting them.
Resource
A second opinion inside the Hollow. Eleven years of observation. And one of the three names that open the deepest part of the site.
Attitude
Neutral — and the only Hollow attitude that moves on courtesy. Moves +1 the first time the party keeps a promise to anyone. She has been keeping score for eleven years.

Knows. Greybank by name and habit, better than Daggerford does. What Vaerelin knows. That the quarry has been cutting toward the aperture for two years and nobody in the village has noticed.

Tonight. On the knoll above the burial ground, in the dark, watching the village she is not allowed to warn.

If they die. The Hollow loses the only voice arguing for alliance. Cooperative containment becomes unreachable.

Do not introduce her in session 1. She is the most valuable NPC the party has to earn rather than find. Reaching her converts the Laughing Hollow from an obstacle into a faction — and meeting her early costs the campaign its best reveal.

Ghesh Two-Knife War-leader, Red-Tusk · with the band · Hostile

Portrait. Twenty-nine, and lean in the way of someone who has been giving his share away. Scarring across the jaw from whatever they ran from. Both knives are new — he has replaced them since.

Voice. Short, declarative, and repeats the important part twice. Speaks Common badly and deliberately, so that nobody assumes he is confused.

Want
A place, before winter, by any means.
Fear
Burying more of the thirty-nine.
Resource
Twenty-two fighters. Complete mobility. Knowledge of the ruins' outer reaches.
Attitude
Hostile, and the only starting Hostile in the campaign. Moves to Wary the first time a human keeps a word; to Neutral if the party fights something beside him.

Knows. What displaced them — and his description is testimony, not truth. Every approach to Greybank. Exactly how many fighters the village has, because he has counted them twice.

Tonight. Awake, walking the edge of the camp, counting his people again.

If they die. Orbal's position wins by default — Compact +3. But if the party killed him, add War +3 first, and the two do not cancel: the band goes to war and nobody is leading it well.

He is not a villain. And he must never be played as one. He is a young man with sixty-one dependents and twenty-two spears, and he has done the arithmetic.

Orbal the Rememberer Keeper of the cords, Red-Tusk · with the band · Wary

Portrait. Old for a goblin, small, and carrying a bundle of knotted cords that is the band's entire written record. Missing most of one ear, an ancient injury.

Voice. Tells you what was agreed, by whom, and when — including agreements from before anyone present was born. Treats a promise as an object.

Want
A bargain. Any bargain that holds.
Fear
That the cords end with her, and the band forgets there was ever another way.
Resource
The band's memory, including old agreements with people in this region. The envoy is hers — and so is one of the three names.
Attitude
Wary, not Hostile — she is the exception to the faction default, and a party that notices this has learned something.

Knows. That there were arrangements here before. The old routes. What the band lived beside, in more detail than Ghesh and with less certainty.

Tonight. Sitting with the thirty-nine, telling them something that happened eighty years ago, because it is the only thing she has to give them.

If they die. The Compact branch becomes unreachable. Ghesh wins every tie, permanently. If the party killed her, the War branch locks.

Why she matters. She sends the envoy at stage 2, over Ghesh's objection. She is the reason the campaign has a peaceful branch at all.

Corben Threll Factor, Daggerford trading house · two days away · Neutral

Portrait. Fifties, travels with a clerk and a strongbox, and is visibly uncomfortable more than a day from a decent bed. Wears good boots, badly maintained — he does not walk if he can ride.

Voice. Restates your position back to you more favourably than you put it, then names a number. Says “let's be practical” before every hard clause.

Want
A supply contract and a commercial post.
Fear
The stone contract collapsing on his watch.
Resource
Capital — more than exists in Greybank by an order of magnitude. Credit. Contracts. Hired guards, for cargo only.
Attitude
Neutral, and strictly performance-based. He likes people who deliver, and is unmoved by anything else.

Knows. What everything is worth. That Greybank has no idea what its stone is valued at in Daggerford. That there is something odd about the quarry, and that he would rather it stayed a rumour — an investigation is a delay.

Tonight. In Daggerford, two days away, reading a shipment ledger and noticing that Greybank is late again.

If they die. The house sends someone worse. The Commercial Clock does not reverse — Daggerford's interest survives the individual.

Hook. The only route to funding a village project without the village's own hands — and every coin comes with a claim attached.

Serina Malk Collector's agent · Neutral, and does not move

Portrait. Thirties, travels alone and light, and the only outsider in the campaign who arrives in Greybank without an escort and is not worried about it. Keeps her hands still and visible, always, which is a trained habit.

Voice. Pleasant, direct, and asks the question everyone else is avoiding. Never haggles — names one price and lets the silence work.

Want
Illefarn artifacts, authenticated and exclusive.
Fear
The Laughing Hollow reaching the site first.
Resource
A buyer. The only real money in the region, a standing price list, and no questions.
Attitude
Neutral. Does not move. She is transactional by profession and treats affection as a discount she has not offered.

Knows. What artifacts are worth to a collector, which is far more than Threll would pay. That there are elves involved — and she has decided this is a reason to hurry rather than to stop.

Tonight. In the Sundered Cart, buying drinks for the quarry crew and asking pleasant questions about the lowest bench.

If they die. The only buyer in the region is gone. Artifacts become unsellable at value, the village's coin ceiling reasserts itself, and the Hollow's problem solves itself for free.

Her secret is her employer, and she does not know much either. She was hired by letter, is paid by letter, and has never asked. A genuine loose end, not a concealed plot. She is the campaign's most dangerous NPC and she never threatens anybody.

Part 5.2

The Location Directory

Twenty-two places inside Greybank, G1 to G22. Each entry says who runs it, what it provides, what breaks if it is lost, and how it behaves in a fight.

Four facts that recur

Thatch is everywhere except one roof. Dray's Store is the Street's only slate, which makes it the firebreak. · The quarry is seven minutes away, and help from it never arrives in time for anything in the village. · Three buildings stand on waystones — the tower, the shrine and the burial-ground wall. · The ring is open in four places, and closing it costs 40 gp, 8 Timber Loads and six days.

G1 · Marrow Bridge District I · the only cart crossing for a day's travel

Three stone piers and a timber deck, ninety feet long and eleven feet wide, with no parapet on the downstream side since a cart went through it two winters ago. The piers are old and good; the deck is newer and worse.

Runs it
Nobody. The Reeve is nominally responsible.
Provides
The only vehicle crossing of the Marrow for a day's travel in either direction. Trade depends on it entirely.
If lost
Immediate, unrolled: Trade −1. Greybank's own answer is the skiff at the wharf, which moves people but not stone.
Tactical
Eleven feet — two Medium creatures abreast, holdable by three defenders. The deck is timber over stone: it burns and can be cut in hours with axes; the piers need the quarry's blasting powder. Anyone shoved or knocked prone within 5 ft of the open downstream edge risks a 20 ft fall into 9 ft of opaque water.

G2 · The Toll-shed District I · south bank, at the bridge foot

A one-room stone hut on the south bank. Greybank charges carts, not people — two copper a wheel, collected by whoever the Reeve has assigned that tenday. Half the time nobody is in it.

Runs it
Rotating villagers. Currently Denk Ottersby most days.
Provides
A trickle of village income, and the crossing book — Greybank's only written record of who has arrived and left, going back three years.
If lost
The book is the loss, not the hut. Burn the toll-shed and you burn the evidence of who came into Greybank in the last three years.
Tactical
Stone, slate roof, one door, one shuttered window facing the bridge. The single best hard point on the south bank, and the only thing blocking sight of the last thirty yards of the south road.

G3 · The Sundered Cart District II · taproom, and the only lodging

Two-storey, stone ground floor, timber above, thatch. A common room thirty by twenty-five with a central hearth, a kitchen, four rentable pallets in the loft, and a stable-lean for two animals.

Runs it
Rosal Tunn.
Provides
Lodging at 5 cp a night per pallet — four pallets only — hot food, and the village's information exchange. Every rumour in Greybank passes through this room in the Evening watch.
If lost
The party has nowhere indoors to sleep unless a household takes them in, which is a Greybank Reputation ≥ +1 favour. The village loses its gathering place: treat as a standing obstacle to raising or persuading a crowd.
Tactical
Thatch, and the highest fire risk on the Street. The common room is bad ground for a fight and excellent ground for a confrontation with an audience. The loft has one ladder and one gable window.

G4 · Dray's Store District II · the village's import channel

Stone lower course, timber above, and slate — Sember Dray reroofed it in stone after a scare, making it the only thatch-free building on the Street. Shop front, storeroom behind, living rooms above, a barred back door onto a yard.

Runs it
Sember Dray, assisted irregularly by Wat Dray.
Provides
All ordinary goods while Trade ≥2, the village's import channel, and credit. Holds 1 Potion of Healing · 1 healer's kit · 2 spell-component packages · 2 ammunition bundles.
If lost
Sember's death closes the store. The stock stays physically inside and becomes a village-secured problem rather than a shopping opportunity.
Tactical
The only slate roof on the Street — it will not carry fire, which makes it the firebreak. Barred back door, shuttered front. Defensible for one person for a short time.

G5 · The Smithy District II · the only metalwork within two days

An open-fronted forge under a slate lean, with a stone workshop and a locked tool-room behind. A buttress narrows the Street to nine feet in front of it.

Runs it
Ordga Brune, shield dwarf. Apprentice: Wat Dray.
Provides
Metal repair, arrow and bolt production, limited martial weapon production. Holds 1 chain shirt · 2 martial weapon-quality items. Without a functioning smith, damaged metal gear cannot reliably be repaired and metal armour freezes at current stock.
If lost
Note the rule sharply: an intact building without a smith does not provide smithing. Wat performs simple repairs at doubled time, no new production, and no ammunition.
Tactical
The nine-foot buttress pinch is the village's natural barricade line, and the militia knows it. A live forge is a hazard and a weapon: coals, quench trough, loose iron stock.

G6 · The Mill District IV · flour, and the sluice

An undershot water mill on a stone sluice, timber superstructure, thatch. The wheel is the single most valuable piece of engineering in Greybank and the hardest to replace.

Runs it
Corvin Ash. His daughter Lisbet can run it alone — the only redundant skilled role in the village.
Provides
Flour, which underpins Food. And the sluice gate, which raises the water at the shoal by roughly eighteen inches — enough to make the shoal impassable, or to flood the low ground east of Millside.
If lost
Food falls at the next dawn if the mill is Disabled. The sluice is the interesting part: a party that understands it can close one of the seven ways in, and a party that breaks it loses that option permanently.
Tactical
Thatch, over a working machine, beside a granary. Fire here is a village-level disaster. The mill floor is cramped, with a ten-foot drop to the wheel race.

G7 · The Granary District IV · two-thirds full, harvest six weeks out

A raised stone store on staddle stones, slate roof, one heavy door, one high vent.

Runs it
The Reeve holds the key; Corvin Ash holds the working key.
Provides
The physical backing of Food 4. This building is the Food variable made visible.
If lost
Granary loss is the fastest route to a Food crisis. Destroyed → Food −2 — the one place a two-step change is recommended.
Tactical
Stone, slate, raised on staddle stones with a thirty-inch gap beneath — vermin-proof, and just large enough for a Small creature or a prone Medium one to hide under. One door, barred from outside. It is the obvious civilian shelter and the obvious siege objective at the same time.

G8 · The Wharf District IV · the skiff

A thirty-foot timber landing, a fish-drying rack, and one flat-bottomed skiff that seats four.

Runs it
Denk Ottersby.
Provides
River fish, a marginal Food contribution — and the skiff, which is Greybank's entire answer to a lost bridge for the movement of people. It does not move stone or carts.
If lost
Losing the skiff while the bridge is down isolates the village from the far bank completely. It is a discrete tracked asset: if it burns or drifts, it is gone.
Tactical
The only water access. The place a body goes in. The place something comes out.

G9 · Shrine of Chauntea District III · older than the village

A single stone room, open-fronted, with a grain-sheaf altar and a slate roof. The foundation course is not villager work, and this is visible to anyone who looks.

Runs it
Lay-keeper Yorrin Fell. He has no spellcasting of any kind — a deliberate campaign decision.
Provides
Burial rites, the moot's formal setting, sanctuary by custom (a social protection, not a magical one), and a strongbox holding 1 Potion of Healing, held against village emergency and not for sale.
If lost
Destruction or public desecration: Unity −1.
Tactical
Stone, slate, open-fronted, no door to bar. Cannot be defended and cannot be burned — which is why civilians run here, and to the granary, in a fire. Its foundation is one of the three waystones inside the village.

G10 · The Green, the Well and the Bell Post District III · sixty yards of open turf

The well is stone-collared, forty feet deep, good water. The bell post is an eight-foot frame carrying a bronze ship's bell bought from Daggerford forty years ago.

Runs it
Bell-warden Nissa Crake, who sleeps in the nearest cott to the post.
Provides
The alarm — the mechanical trigger for every mobilisation. And the well, which is the village's water and its firefighting supply.
If lost
Cut the bell down and Greybank's muster time roughly doubles. Foul the well and you have created a slow crisis that no amount of combat solves.
Tactical
Open ground, sixty yards across. The well-head gives half cover to one creature; the bell post three-quarters cover to one. Four Cott lane-mouths open onto it. Crossing the Green under fire from the lanes or the Moot Hall roof is a real decision — and it is the ground the village will make the party cross.

G11 · Moot Hall District III · the Reeve's house and the village's hall

A long stone-and-timber building with a slate roof, a hall that holds sixty standing, and private rooms at the north end.

Runs it
Reeve Ondra Vell.
Provides
Village decision-making, and the labour levy — the mechanism by which every upgrade project gets its volunteers. Holds the moot chest: 31 gp and 40 sp, the deeds, the crossing-book archive, and the granary key.
If lost
The labour levy stops. No collective project can be organised without either the Reeve or a moot, and the moot needs the hall or the shrine.
Tactical
Slate roof, and the only building overlooking the whole Green. Two doors, five shuttered windows. If the village fights the party, archers go here first.

G12 · The Militia Shed District III · the entire military inventory

A timber shed with a slate roof and a good lock, twenty yards off the Green.

Runs it
Militia captain Berrick Hone. Two keys: his, and the Reeve's.
Provides
The muster point. Contents: 14 billhooks, 9 hunting spears, 6 shortbows in poor repair, 4 lanterns, a horn, coils of rope, and a barrel of pitch. Holds 1 shield · 2 ammunition bundles.
If lost
If it is looted or burned before a muster, the militia turns out with farm tools and Defense is treated as one step lower for that engagement. The single cheapest way for anyone — including the party — to disarm Greybank.
Tactical
The barrel of pitch is a fire accelerant sitting twenty yards from the Green. Its presence is deliberate: a tool, a hazard, and a temptation.

G13 · The Tower District IX · 22 ft ancient, 8 ft villager-built

A stone drum on the knoll north of the village: twenty-two feet of original ancient work and eight feet of much cruder villager courses on top, roofed in shingle. Interior ladder. The old stonework has no visible joints and the villagers have stopped remarking on it.

Runs it
The militia rota. Manned Dawn through Evening. Unmanned Night and Deep Night.
Provides
Early warning. While manned and in clear conditions, Greybank gets a full watch of warning of anything approaching openly, and the bell rings before contact rather than after.
If lost
Loss of warning, and Defense −1 if no alternative exists. The lower twenty-two feet cannot realistically be destroyed by anything the party can bring; the villager-built top and the roof can.
Tactical
Commands everything. Two archers on the tower dominate the north approach and the fields — and it is the most obvious first objective for any attacker who has scouted the village. Its base course is one of the three waystones, and it is the same stone as the quarry aperture.

G14 · The Quarry, “the Pale” District VI · 500 yards up the haul road

A stepped limestone cut into the hillside, worked by fourteen men. Three benches, a crane derrick, a powder store, spoil heaps — and, at the north end of the lowest bench, an opening in the rock face that is not natural and that the crew did not cut.

Runs it
Foreman Kael Durrow.
Provides
Stone Loads, the material input for every stone project. Employment for fourteen of Greybank's eighty-five able adults. The village's principal export income. Holds the powder store: 6 charges of blasting powder, the only thing in Greybank capable of dropping a bridge pier or a building at speed.
If lost
Expansion increases Illefarn Instability, and that price is stated in advance. After the bench collapses, Stone Loads halve to 1 per 2 days until it is shored.
Tactical
Benches give elevation; spoil heaps give cover; the derrick and the powder store are both usable and both dangerous. The haul road cut is the only quick route between quarry and village, and it is seven minutes at a run — help from here does not arrive in time for anything.

G15 · The Stonecutters' Bunkhouse District VI · outside the ring

A long timber building with a thatch roof, beside the haul road. Sleeps sixteen. Six of the quarry crew are Daggerford men who live here rather than in the village.

Runs it
Kael Durrow.
Provides
Housing for the six non-village quarrymen — a bloc that is in Greybank but not of it. They sleep outside the ring, which puts them outside every mobilisation, every alarm, and every count of the village's strength.
If lost
Six workers displaced into the village, and quarry output halves until they are rehoused.
Tactical
Outside the ring, upslope, with a view down the haul road. An enemy that takes the bunkhouse has a dry base within sight of Greybank.

G16 · The Wagonyard and Stable District II · three carts, two stone-wagons

A hard-standing yard, a four-stall stable, a cart shed, and a small hay loft. Timber, thatch.

Runs it
Carter Gedd Plank.
Provides
The Daggerford run — Greybank's physical Trade link — and cartage for any project. Holds 2 riding horses. If they are killed, stolen, sold or taken, they are gone, and a four-session campaign may never replace them.
If lost
Losing the carts does not immediately reduce Trade — Daggerford's own carts still come — but it removes Greybank's ability to send stone, and every project's haulage time increases by half.
Tactical
The carts here are the barricade material the militia uses at the second chokepoint. Hay loft plus thatch equals the second-worst fire in the village after the mill.

G17 · The Cotts District V · twenty-two dwellings, four lanes

Cob and timber, all thatch, in four short irregular lanes north of the Street, with eaves nearly meeting over lanes six to eight feet wide. Households H1–H22.

Runs it
—
Provides
Where most of Greybank sleeps.
If lost
Each cott lost displaces a household. Three or more lost in one night is a Unity −1 event.
Tactical
The most important tactical fact in the village. No missile line longer than twenty-five yards exists inside the Cotts. Thatch eaves nearly touch, so fire crosses lanes. Four lane-mouths open onto the Green. For a party fleeing a mobilisation the Cotts are cover; for a party fighting there they are a trap with a hundred witnesses in it.

G18 · The Fold and Byres District VIII · 260 yards outside the ring

Drystone sheepfolds and three timber byres on the poorer western ground.

Runs it
Head shepherd Bessa Ruddock.
Provides
Wool, mutton and milk — a standing contribution to Food. A major livestock attack is a Food −1 event.
If lost
Food −1, and the fold is the classic Stage 2 “Incident” location on the threat ladder. Anything hunting Greybank eats here first.
Tactical
Outside the ring, unlit, 260 yards from help — roughly twenty-six rounds at a run from the Green, longer at night with no light. Anything that happens at the fold is over before the village arrives. This is the intended location for the campaign's first Level 2 threat signal.

G19 · The Burial Ground District IX · ninety years of Greybank

Low stone markers and a lych-gate on the knoll below the tower, bounded on its north side by a wall of the same jointless stone as the tower base.

Runs it
—
Provides
The village's memory. Every NPC death in this campaign ends here, and the players will watch it fill.
If lost
Desecration is a Unity −1 and Greybank Reputation −1 event, and one of the few acts that will turn Yorrin Fell against a party permanently.
Tactical
High ground, low cover, on the north footpath. Its north wall is one of the three waystones inside the village.

G20 · The Charcoal Camp District X · far bank, half a mile from the bridge

Two burning-clamps, a turf shelter, and a stack yard in the coppice on the far bank.

Runs it
Ib Sallow, the closest thing Greybank has to a woodsman.
Provides
Charcoal for the forge — without it, the smithy's output halves — and Timber Loads for every building project.
If lost
Smithy production halves; timber projects stall. Sallow's death additionally removes the only villager who reads the far-bank woods.
Tactical
Across the river. If the bridge goes down, Sallow is on the wrong side of it, and so are Greybank's timber and charcoal. Burning clamps are open fire, permanently.

G21 · The Earth Ring encircling Districts II–V · breached in four places

A turf-and-stake bank, five feet high where it survives, thrown up ninety years ago and never maintained. Breached north-west (haul road), north (footpath), east (fields) and south-east (riverside path). The gaps are twenty to forty feet wide and completely open.

Runs it
—
Provides
Nothing, currently. No defensive benefit at all.
If lost
It is already broken. Closing it is the Palisade project — 40 gp, 8 Timber Loads and 6 days — the campaign's first-tier defensive goal and the most likely thing a party invests in.
Tactical
The surviving bank still gives half cover to anyone crouching behind it, and still channels movement. It shapes a fight even though it stops nothing.

G22 · The Herbwife's Cottage District V, north edge · all of Greybank's medicine

A cott set apart at the north edge, with a walled physic garden, drying racks under the eaves, and a still.

Runs it
Maud Elleth, half-elf.
Provides
The entirety of Greybank's medicine. Treatment of wounds and illness, herbal remedies, and healer's kits made locally rather than imported. Holds 1 healer's kit · 1 antitoxin.
If lost
This is the most load-bearing single point in Greybank. Maud's death or the loss of the garden removes local medicine production entirely; medicine then depends on Trade ≥2 and Sember's imports, and at Trade ≤1 it simply stops.
Tactical
Set apart at the north edge, nearest the north gap in the ring, ninety yards from the nearest other dwelling. She is the most isolated important person in Greybank.

The fold and byres. Bessa Ruddock and two dogs. 260 yards and twenty-six rounds from the Green — help does not arrive.

Stage 2 — Incident. The campaign's designated first Level 2 threat location. Red-Tusk scouts from Kettlerock reach here in a night.
Faction
The proposed Red-Tusk site on the Compact branch.
Changes when
Red-Tusk W4 → raids. Red-Tusk C4 → negotiation over this exact ground — and the farms bloc lives nearest it.
Known by
Bessa Ruddock, and she has been saying so.

Z5 · The Coppice and Charcoal Ground ½ watch, far bank from Greybank

Terrain
Managed coppice on the south bank, two burning-clamps, a turf shelter, a stack yard.
Why go
Timber Loads and charcoal — one Timber Load per day with four workers. Without charcoal, the smithy halves.
What's there
The charcoal camp. Ib Sallow, alone, every night, out of earshot of the bell.
Threat
Stage 1 — Sign. Something uses the coppice at night. Ib talks about it and is not listened to.
Faction
The wardens' first contact point — at Hollow stage 2, a mark appears on a tree here.
Changes when
Bridge lost → Ib is on the wrong side and timber stops. Hollow 2 → the mark appears.
Known by
Ib Sallow, completely. Vadd Brune, the route only.

Z6 · Reedwater 1 watch from Greybank

Terrain
The Marrow spreads across low meadows east of Greybank. Reed beds, cattle-cropped grass, standing water in patches. Floods in spate.
Why go
Fishing and fowling — the region's best foraging. The riverside track to the ford runs through it.
What's there
Old drainage ditches nobody maintains. Two derelict eel-traps. The track east.
Threat
Stage 1 — Sign. Things come out of the reeds.
Faction
—
Changes when
Spate → impassable marsh throughout, and the track to the ford is closed on top of the ford itself closing.
Known by
Denk Ottersby.

Z7 · Stonecrop Rise 1 watch from Greybank

Terrain
The upland shoulder above the quarry. Thin soil, limestone breaking through, wind-cropped thorn.
Why go
It overlooks everything. From the Rise a party sees Greybank, the Pale, the Home Fields, both road approaches, and in clear weather the smoke from Kettlerock.
What's there
A scatter of low cairns, genuinely old and genuinely graves. And among them, Waystone 4 — which is not a cairn, and which anyone who has looked at Greybank's tower will recognise.
Threat
None at start. No water and no cover.
Faction
—
Changes when
Red-Tusk W5 → watched. Instability ≥3 → the waystone changes.
Known by
Kael Durrow, vaguely — quarrymen walk up here. Nobody has connected the waystone to the tower.

Z8 · The Cut 1 watch from Greybank

Terrain
Where the Daggerford road climbs south-east out of the valley through a defile — two hundred yards long, banks twenty feet, room for one cart.
Why go
It is the road. Everything from Daggerford comes through here.
What's there
A boundary stone, a drover's shelter, and the last place from which Greybank is visible.
Threat
Stage 1 — Sign. It is the obvious ambush site in the region and every carter knows it.
Faction
Red-Tusk W5 closes this, and closing it isolates Greybank from Daggerford entirely. Threll prices protection for it rather than providing it.
Changes when
Red-Tusk W5 → watched, then closed. Trade −1. The Daggerford clock stalls or reverses.
Known by
Gedd Plank, intimately. He will describe it unprompted if asked about the road.

Z9 · The Hollowway 1 watch, far bank from Greybank

Terrain
A sunken lane running west along the south bank, cut eight feet below the fields by centuries of use, roofed over with hazel.
Why go
The fast, unseen route west — and it is the route the wardens use.
What's there
Nothing built. The lane simply runs, and it runs toward the Hollowmarch.
Threat
Stage 1 — Sign. Something has been using it that is not a warden and not a villager.
Faction
Laughing Hollow. This is how nine elves reach Greybank's far bank without ever being seen.
Changes when
Hollow 4 → wardens use it openly. Hollow 5 → it is watched, and a party on it is stopped.
Known by
Ib Sallow. Nobody else. It does not appear on the player map.

Z10 · Anvil Gorge 1½ watches from Greybank

Terrain
Seven miles upstream the Marrow narrows into a limestone cleft thirty feet deep and twelve wide. The banks are rough going on both sides.
Why go
The span — a collapsed natural arch, four feet wide at its narrowest, wet with spray. A foot crossing that works when nothing else does.
What's there
The span. Spray-slick stone. A drop into fast water in a slot the party could not climb out of.
Threat
Stage 2 — Incident. The crossing itself. Falling is sufficient; it needs no monster.
Faction
—
Changes when
Spate → the span is still there and is genuinely dangerous. Bridge destroyed → this becomes strategically important, and the party will discover it is not a solution for cargo.
Known by
Corvin Ash — he reads the river. Ib Sallow knows of it. Not on the player map.

Z11 · Hagsfoot Moor 2 watches from Greybank

Terrain
Open upland scrub and peat north of Stonecrop Rise. No trees, no water, no shelter, and visibility for miles in every direction — including of you.
Why go
The only route north to Kettlerock and the Old Delving.
What's there
Nothing. That is the point. Two watches of ground on which the party can be seen from the moment they enter it.
Threat
Stage 1 — Sign. Red-Tusk scouts cross it nightly.
Faction
Red-Tusk scouting ground. They will know the party is coming before the party is halfway.
Changes when
Red-Tusk W4+ → crossing this is a running fight. Red-Tusk C5 → they guide the party across it.
Known by
Nobody in Greybank has been past here. Villagers describe it as “the moor” and stop.

Z12 · Bythe Ford 2 watches from Greybank

Terrain
Twelve miles downstream the Marrow broadens over a gravel bed. Knee-deep in normal water, forty yards across.
Why go
It is the bridge detour — the only cart crossing of the Marrow other than Greybank's bridge.
What's there
The ford, a ruined tollhouse from when someone tried to charge for it, and a marker post cut with the water heights of bad years.
Threat
Stage 1 — Sign normally. Stage 3 in spate — and the marker post is the explicit warning. A party that fords anyway has been told.
Faction
—
Changes when
Spate → impassable, one to two days. Red-Tusk W5 → watched, because interdicting the ford completes Greybank's isolation.
Known by
Gedd Plank and Denk Ottersby. Every carter in the region.

Z13 · Fenrow Holding 2½ watches from Greybank

Terrain
Three farmsteads and a shared barn on the south bank half a mile beyond the ford. Nineteen people.
Why go
A roof that is not Greybank's, and the only other people in the region who watch the ford.
What's there
No store, no smith, no shrine, no services of any kind. They buy in Greybank and they know it.
Threat
None at start.
Faction
—
Changes when
Greybank Rep −2 or −3 → Fenrow will still take the party in, for a while, because news travels at the speed of the ford. Greybank Reaction 6 evacuation → nineteen people cannot feed a hundred and forty-three.
Known by
Everyone. Nobody thinks about it.

Z14 · Wend's Barrow 2 watches from Greybank

Terrain
A long low mound in birch scrub, a mile south of the Daggerford road.
Why go
Local belief says a chieftain is buried here and that digging is unlucky.
What's there
It is not a barrow. Under the turf is Waystone 5 — the same jointless stone as Greybank's tower, standing upright — and the mound is spoil heaped against it by people who did not build it.
Threat
Stage 1 — Sign. Nothing is buried here, and something about that is wrong.
Faction
—
Changes when
Instability ≥3 → the waystone changes.
Known by
Everyone knows the story. Nobody has dug, which is why the story survives.

Z15 · The Hollowmarch 3 watches from Greybank

Terrain
Old woodland, thickening westward, with clearings that are too regular. Three watches from Greybank and a different world.
Why go
To find the elves — and there is no other reason. Nothing here is for sale.
What's there
Waystone 6. Warden sign, for those who can read it. Paths that go where they were meant to go. This is the outer edge of elven-watched country, not Laughing Hollow itself — the Hollow is off the map and the party does not go there.
Threat
Stage 2 — Incident for anyone unwelcome. The wardens are forbidden to fight fair.
Faction
Laughing Hollow. Vaerelin and Nethra are one to two watches from Greybank's far bank when they choose to be.
Changes when
Hollow 5 → wards across the approaches, and Hollow Reputation decides whether the party passes at all.
Known by
Nobody in Greybank. Halvard Vell knows there were elves once and cannot tell you which day it is.

Z16 · Kettlerock 3 watches from Greybank

Terrain
A bowl of broken ground north-west — shattered limestone, thorn, and a spring that does not fail. Defensible, miserable, and the best available.
Why go
The Red-Tusk are here. Sixty-one of them.
What's there
Twenty-two fighters, thirty-nine who cannot fight, eleven wounded. Ghesh, Orbal, and Orbal's knotted cords.
Threat
Stage 2 — Incident at Red-Tusk Rep −1. Stage 4 — Response on the War branch.
Faction
Red-Tusk. Scouts from here reach the fold in a night.
Changes when
W5 → fortified. C5 → the party is expected and fed. F4 → the weak camps are already abandoned and the bowl is emptying.
Known by
Nobody in Greybank. Berrick Hone believes the goblins are “up past the moor” and is right by accident.

Z17 · The Old Delving 4 watches from Greybank

Terrain
A worked cleft in the uplands beyond Kettlerock. Cut stone at the entrance, spoil that is centuries old, and ground that is wrong underfoot in a way the party will notice before they can say why.
Why go
This is where the Red-Tusk lived, and what they ran from is here.
What's there
A cut ramp, a lintel of the same jointless stone, and a doorway the goblins piled shut from the outside and did not stay to watch. The pile is visibly maintained by hand, recently.
Threat
Stage 3 — Escalation, and the only Stage 3 on the map at campaign start. The physical evidence at the threshold is the Level 3 warning: a settlement abandoned in a hurry, piled shut from outside.
Faction
Red-Tusk, formerly. The Laughing Hollow watches this and will intervene if the party opens it — Hollow +1 minimum.
Changes when
Instability rises. This gate opens into level 4–5 content, and the world will not scale down for a level 3 party that digs in.
Known by
Ghesh and Orbal, and their accounts differ. Both are testimony, not truth.

Z18 · Coldwell Vale 5 watches, via the Hollowmarch only from Greybank

Terrain
A sheltered valley far to the west with water, wood and no people. Better ground than Kettlerock in every respect.
Why go
Nobody in Greybank ever will. It exists for the Red-Tusk.
What's there
Space for sixty-one goblins to survive a winter. This is the Flight branch's destination, and it is why that branch exists at all.
Threat
Unknown, and it is not the party's problem.
Faction
Red-Tusk, prospectively. The only route runs through elven-watched country — sixty-one goblins migrating through it is its own encounter.
Changes when
Red-Tusk F5 → they are moving toward it. F6 → they are there, and nobody is left to maintain the pile at the Old Delving.
Known by
Orbal. It is in the cords.
