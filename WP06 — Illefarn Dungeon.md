# The Broken March — WP06: The Illefarn Dungeon

**Status:** Canonical campaign site document
**Consumes:** `Campaign design doc.md`, `WP01 — Campaign State Model.md` (§15 mandatory), `WP02 — Greybank Village.md`, `WP03 — NPC Roster.md`, `WP04 — Factions.md`, `WP05 — Regional Map.md`
**Feeds:** WP07, WP08–WP12, WP13, WP14, WP15
**Scope:** The Marrowward — four stages, two gates, sixteen sectors, seven systems, two residents, and the overworld ledger that says what every activation does to the region above

Five packages have pointed at a boarded opening in a quarry face and stopped. WP06 opens it. The site is an Illefarn frontier work whose ward line still runs across WP05's map as six standing stones, and whose defensive construct has spent an age enforcing that line with a corrupted instruction set — which is why the Red-Tusk fled, why the Laughing Hollow is right, and why nothing in Greybank has been safe for longer than anyone has been counting. Every system here has a written consequence in the overworld. Every sealed door has at least two ways through it. No stage requires a single successful check to progress, and every stage can be walked out of at the level it is entered.

- **What fills the gap if the Red-Tusk take the Flight branch.**

## 1.3 Design decisions — reviewed and confirmed canonical

These seven were the largest calls in the campaign, and were **reviewed and confirmed by the DM**. They are canon. Later work packages consume them as fixed; they are not open questions.

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **The site is a ward line, not a vault.** Its purpose was to hold a perimeter — WP05's waystone arc *is* that perimeter, anchored on two gate-works. | Design doc §19 calls it a border-defence and infrastructure installation. A perimeter explains the arc, both gates, the transit route between them, and why activating things changes the *region* rather than yielding loot. |
| 2 | **The apex threat is the site's own defensive construct, running on a corrupted instruction set.** It is not evil, not intelligent in any negotiable sense, and it is doing its job. | Design doc §28 forbids cartoonish evil. A machine enforcing a rule badly is more frightening than a monster, and it is **solvable** four different ways (§11.2) rather than merely survivable — design doc §2.2. |
| 3 | **This is what emptied the Old Delving.** The Red-Tusk were inside the ward line; it classified them as an incursion. | WP04 and WP05 left this open. It makes the goblins' testimony accurate-but-uncomprehending, ties the two gates together, and means the party's first real information comes from refugees nobody wants to talk to. |
| 4 | **The Red-Tusk have been keeping the Kettlerock Gate shut** without understanding what they were doing. | Answers WP04's *"what fills the gap if they leave."* The Flight branch's cost becomes concrete and horrifying: **the Sentinel gets out at the north end** (§15). |
| 5 | **The transit route makes Greybank and the Old Delving half a watch apart underground**, against four watches on the surface. | Design doc §19 lists transit routes. Turning a WP05 travel cost into a dungeon reward is the cleanest possible expression of *"dungeon progression alters the campaign rather than producing loot."* |
| 6 | **The water control governs the Marrow itself** — and therefore the shoal, Bythe Ford, and spate. | WP05 built three crossings whose behaviour depends on water level. Handing the party the tap makes Stage II matter above ground immediately. |
| 7 | **The Laughing Hollow's position is correct and incomplete.** The line is failing and the construct is loose; they believe it cannot be repaired, only sealed. **They are wrong about that, and being wrong does not make them exaggerating.** | WP04 §16 binds the elves to being right about the danger. This preserves it exactly — they are right about every fact and wrong about one possibility, which is what makes Nethra's dissent worth something. |

# 2. Changes to Upstream Canon

**None.**

The seven design decisions of §1.3 — the site as a ward line, the construct as apex threat, what emptied the Old Delving, the Red-Tusk holding the north gate shut, the transit route's effect on WP05 travel, the water control governing the Marrow, and the Laughing Hollow being right-but-incomplete — were **reviewed and confirmed by the DM**. They are canonical and appear in design doc §33.6.

WP06 uses WP01 §15 exactly as written and invents no scale of its own:

- **Instability** is WP01 §6.5's 0–6 clock. No second meter.
- **Sectors** use WP01 §15.2's six states: *Unknown / Discovered / Accessible / Locked / Breached / Sealed*, with paired descriptors where needed.
- **Systems** use WP01 §15.3's six states: *Unknown / Identified / Inactive / Active / Damaged / Disabled*.
- **Every activation** is written to WP01 §15.4's five-part template: local effect, regional effect, instability change, faction awareness, reversibility.
- **Breaches** are factual and permanent per WP01 §15.5 — *"the sector does not magically reset because Instability later falls."*

**Inherited and honoured:**

- WP04 §16: **the Laughing Hollow is right about the danger and was never exaggerating.** §3.4 keeps this intact.
- WP04 §16: every other faction's account is **testimony, not truth.** §3.4 scores each account against what is actually true.
- WP05: two gates at fixed distances; six waystones in an arc; the Old Delving **piled shut from the outside**; **no villager will enter** (WP03 §14.8).
- WP02 §15.7: **quarry expansion is Instability +1**, stated in advance.

# 3. What the Site Is

## 3.1 The Marrowward **[CAMPAIGN, on ADAPTED Illefarn foundations]**

**[LORE]** Ancient Illefarn held this country and left works in it.

**[CAMPAIGN]** The Marrowward was a **frontier ward-line** — not a fortress, not a vault, and emphatically not a tomb. It held a perimeter against incursion from the north-west, and it did so with three things working together:

1. **A ward line**, anchored on standing stones — WP05's six waystones, and others long gone.
2. **Two gate-works** at the line's ends, with living galleries, workshops and stores between them.
3. **A sentinel**, which walked the line and turned back what crossed it.

It was a border post with a very long fence and one very patient guard.

## 3.2 The arc — what WP05 found

WP05 §10 established, discoverably and without magic: six identical jointless stones forming an **arc open to the north-west**, with the two doors at its focus and its end. **Three of them stand inside Greybank.**

**What that means:**

| WP05 observation | WP06 meaning |
|---|---|
| Six stones, same cut, no joints | **Ward anchors.** Each holds one span of the line |
| The arc opens to the north-west | **That is the direction the line faces.** The threat came from there |
| Two doors at the focus and the end | The **Greybank Gate** (Z2) and the **Kettlerock Gate** (Z17) |
| W6 in the Hollowmarch is the only one anybody tends | **The wardens have been maintaining one anchor for centuries.** One, out of six |
| W5 at Wend's Barrow is buried under heaped spoil | A span that failed long ago, and people piled earth on the stone rather than ask why |
| Greybank built a tower, a shrine and a wall onto three of them | **Ninety years of a village leaning on a fence it cannot see** |

## 3.3 What went wrong

Not a catastrophe. **Attrition.**

- The line has been degrading for an age. Anchors cracked, spans lapsed, and nobody replaced them.
- **Three of the six spans are dead.** The north-west quarter — the quarter that mattered — has been open for longer than Greybank has existed.
- Things came through. Most of them died or dispersed. **One got into the water galleries and stayed** (§11.1).
- **The Sentinel kept working.** Its instruction set survived; its *map* did not. It still knows it must turn back anything inside the line, and it no longer knows correctly where the line runs or who is authorised to be within it.

> **The site is not waking up. It never stopped.** Instability 1 — *Dormant Disturbance* — is what an ancient machine looks like when it has been quietly failing for two thousand years and nobody has poked it. Everything the party does from here is poking it.

## 3.4 Who believes what

WP04 §16 makes every account except the Hollow's **testimony rather than truth**. Scored against §3.3:

| Who | Believes | Accurate? |
|---|---|---|
| **Vaerelin Ossuine** | The line is failing; the construct is loose; the site cannot be repaired, **only sealed** | **Right about the first two. Wrong about the third** — and it is the one thing he will not discuss |
| **Nethra** | The same, except that she thinks people who live on the line could hold it | **Right, and she cannot prove it** |
| **Ghesh Two-Knife** | Something came out of the deep place at night, killed methodically, and could not be fought | **Entirely accurate, and he does not know it was a machine** |
| **Orbal** | There were agreements with "the stone-folk" once, and a bargain was kept | **Accurate, and older than she thinks** — the cords remember authorisation, not friendship |
| **Kael Durrow** | There is a room behind the boards and the air moved | Accurate as far as ten feet |
| **Ordga Brune** | No chisel makes that cut | **Accurate, and the single best free lead in the campaign** |
| **Serina Malk** | There are portable valuables | **Accurate.** She is not wrong; she is just indifferent to the rest |
| **Corben Threll** | It is a rumour that delays shipments | Accurate to his interests and to nothing else |

> **Nobody in the campaign holds the whole picture, including the elves.** Design doc §7's no-mastermind rule extends to knowledge: there is no NPC who can simply explain the dungeon, and the party assembling §3.3 from six partial accounts **is** the investigation.

## 4.1 The two gates

|---|---|---|
| Surface | **Z2, the quarry** — ¼ watch from the village | **Z17, the Old Delving** — 4 watches, beyond Kettlerock |

## 4.5 Getting out

**Every stage has at least two exits available at the level it is entered.** No stage is a one-way door, and no exit requires a check.

| Stage | Exit 1 | Exit 2 | Exit 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Back up the ramp to Z2 | — | *(one is sufficient; the ramp cannot be closed from inside)* |
| **II** | Back through S5 to Stage I | The Sluice Hall's overflow shaft — climbable, wet, comes out in the quarry's lowest bench | |
| **III** | Back through S9 to Stage II | **The Kettlerock Gate (S12)** — opens outward, from inside, without tools | The transit route, once active |
| **IV** | Back through S13 | The transit route | **S16 the Broken Quarter** — a scramble out through the dead spans onto Z11 Hagsfoot Moor |

> **S16 is an exit and an entrance.** It is the hole in the fence. Things have been using it for an age, and once the party finds it, so can they — which is why Stage IV is reachable by a route that skips Stages I–III entirely, for anyone reckless enough to climb down a failed ward span on the open moor.

# 5. Progressive Unlock

## 5.1 The barriers

**Every barrier has at least two routes through it, and none of them is a single check.** Design doc §23 forbids gating campaign continuation behind one roll; the brief forbids it per stage. Where a route is *hard*, it is hard in time, risk or consequence — never in one d20.

| Barrier | Between | Route 1 | Route 2 | Route 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The boards** | Z2 → S1 | **Pull them.** They are already loose | — | *(trivial by design — the site's front door is not a puzzle)* |
| **S4 Watch Chamber** | S2 → S4 | A **resonance key** from the muster floor (§5.2) | The chamber's shutter is warped; **prise it, permanently** — S4 becomes *Breached* | — |
| **S5 Lock Gallery** | Stage I → II | A **resonance key** (found in S2) | **The overflow shaft** — climb down from the quarry's lowest bench, wet and awkward, bypasses S5 entirely | **Break the mechanism.** S5 → *Breached*, permanent, and the elves notice |
| **S9 barrier** | Stage II → III | A **resonance key** from the workshops (S6) | **The Drowned Stair (S8)** — swim it, through the Listener's water | **Enter Stage III from the Kettlerock Gate instead** — 4 watches overland |
| **S13 Approach of Names** | Stage III → IV | **Authorisation** — present a name the site accepts. **Orbal's cords hold one** (§5.3) | A resonance key from S11 | **S16 the Broken Quarter** — climb in from Hagsfoot Moor through a dead span |
| **S15 Ward Heart** | S14 → S15 | Authorisation carried forward from S13 | **Force it.** Instability **+2**, S15 → *Breached*, permanent | The **Watch-Glass** (System 2) identifies which anchor governs the door, allowing it to be opened from S3 |
| **S12 Kettlerock Gate** *(from outside)* | Z17 → S12 | **Dig out the pile.** Hours of work, and the Red-Tusk will know | **From inside it opens outward, without tools** | The Red-Tusk will clear it themselves at **Rep +2** — and will not go in |

## 5.2 Resonance keys

Design doc §20 names the **Illefarn Resonance Key** as an example item. **WP13 owns its mechanics and value.** WP06 fixes only the economy:

- **Three exist in the site**: one in the muster floor (S2) in a warden's kit, one in the workshops (S6), one in the deterrent gallery (S11).
- **A fourth is held by the Laughing Hollow.** Vaerelin will not give it up; **Nethra will, at Hollow Rep +3**, and it is the most valuable thing the party can be given by anyone.
- Each key is **tuned to a span**, not to a door. A key opens what its span governs, which is why three keys open three different barriers and not one master lock.
- Design doc §20's choice stands: keep, sell, give to the elves, destroy. **Malk will pay for one. The Hollow will take one back by force at stage 4+.**

## 5.3 Authorisation — the Approach of Names

The Stage IV barrier is **not a lock.** It is a challenge, and it accepts a name.

**Orbal's knotted cords hold one** — the Red-Tusk remember an agreement with "the stone-folk" and remember the form of words without remembering what it was for (§3.4). WP04 already made her cords a physical handout; **this is what is on them.**

**Three ways to hold a name:**

| Route | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Orbal recites it** | Red-Tusk Rep **≥ +1**, and reaching her. The Compact branch makes this easy; the War branch makes it nearly impossible |
| **The site's own records (S6)** | Reading them takes a full watch and does not require magic — the names are cut, not written |
| **Nethra** | Hollow Rep **≥ +2**. She has watched the Hollowmarch anchor for eleven years and knows the form |

> **This is the campaign's best structural argument for talking to people.** Every route to the deepest part of the dungeon runs through a faction relationship, an hour of patient reading, or a climb down a broken fence on an open moor. **None of them runs through a fight.**

## 6.2 System 1 — The Ward Anchor

**S3, Stage I.** A drum of the same jointless stone as the waystones, floor to ceiling, with a recess at chest height worn smooth by use.

**Identified by:** anyone who has seen a waystone. It is obviously the same object, indoors. **Ordga Brune would identify it in a sentence if she would go inside, and she will not.**

**Activated by:** a resonance key in the recess, or by carrying an already-active anchor's resonance — which is to say, **once the party activates one, the others become easier**, and that is the site's only escalating mechanic.

| WP01 §15.4 | |
|---|---|
| **Local** | Seals or opens the gate corridor at will. The Stage I sectors become defensible — this is the party's first safe ground underground |
| **Regional** | **Suppresses creature movement within about a mile of Greybank.** The fold stops losing livestock to anything that is not a goblin. WP05's Z4 and Z5 hazards drop one stage |
| **Instability** | **−1 if stabilised correctly** (key, unforced, unhurried). **+1 if forced** (broken, jammed, or powered by damaging another span) |
| **Faction** | **The elves notice immediately and unmistakably.** Hollow clock **+1** either way. If stabilised, **Hollow Rep +1** — this is the first act that makes Nethra's argument look plausible. If forced, **Hollow Rep −1** |
| **Reversible** | **Yes.** Freely, from S3 |

**Who wants what:** Vaerelin wants it *stabilised and then abandoned*. Nethra wants it stabilised and **manned**. Threll does not know it exists. Malk would sell the recess plate if she could prise it out.

## 6.3 System 2 — The Watch-Glass

**S4, Stage I.** A basin of dark, still liquid that is not water, set in a stone table, ringed with the same cut marks as the waystones.

**Identified by:** looking into it. It is not subtle and it is not dangerous.

**Activated by:** nothing. **It has never stopped working.** It is the one thing in the Marrowward that still does exactly what it was built to do.

| WP01 §15.4 | |
|---|---|
| **Local** | Shows the **state of the ward line** — six anchors, three dark, one tended, two failing. The dead quarter is unmistakable |
| **Regional** | **Information, and nothing else.** It shows the party the map WP05 §10 let them guess at, and confirms it |
| **Instability** | **0.** Observation is not interference, and this is the campaign's one free lunch |
| **Faction** | **Nobody notices.** It makes no sound and reaches nothing |
| **Reversible** | n/a |

> **This is deliberately generous.** A level 3 party, one room past the front door, can learn the shape of the entire campaign for free. Design doc §2.1: dangerous situations should be *readable* before they become fatal. The Watch-Glass is the campaign keeping that promise, early and without charging for it.

**Who wants what:** **Malk wants this badly and would pay more for it than for anything else in the site** — it is portable, beautiful, obviously ancient, and demonstrably functional. Selling it costs the party the only reliable view of the ward line they will ever have. Design doc §20's loot philosophy, in one object.

## 6.4 System 3 — The Sluice Works

**S7, Stage II.** A hall of channels, gates and counterweights over a black race of moving water. Loud. It has been running unattended for an age and it is **still running.**

**Identified by:** watching it for ten minutes. The mechanism is not concealed; the gates are labelled in cut names.

**Activated by:** turning the gates. **No key. No magic. Physical labour and nerve** — the walkways are wet and the drop is real.

| WP01 §15.4 | |
|---|---|
| **Local** | Floods or drains the lower galleries — **including S8, the Drowned Stair.** Draining S8 makes it walkable and **takes away the Listener's water** (§11.1) |
| **Regional** | **Changes the Marrow itself.** Raising the flow closes the shoal and **makes Bythe Ford impassable** — an artificial spate. Lowering it opens the shoal permanently, drops the ford to ankle-deep, and **stops Greybank's mill wheel** (WP02 G6) |
| **Instability** | **+1 if forced or slammed. 0 if worked slowly**, gate by gate, over a full watch |
| **Faction** | **Greybank notices within a day** — the mill, the ford, the river at the bridge piers. Corvin Ash will know before anyone (WP03 §5.9). **The elves notice; Hollow clock +1.** The Red-Tusk notice only if the ford was their crossing |
| **Reversible** | **Yes, slowly.** One watch to undo, and the river takes another day to settle |

> **The most consequential thing a level 4 party can touch.** WP05 built three crossings whose behaviour depends on water level, and this is the tap. A party that drains the lower galleries to fight the Listener has **also stopped the village mill and made the ford fordable for a goblin warband.** Say so before they turn the gate — design doc §29.

**Who wants what:** **Threll wants this and will pay for it** — a controllable ford is worth more to him than any artifact. **Greybank wants it never touched** once they understand it. The Hollow regards it as the least dangerous system here and will not act over it.

## 6.5 System 4 — The Transit Ring

**S9 to S12, Stage III.** Not a portal. A **cut way** — a level, dead-straight gallery running the whole spine of the line, built for moving wardens and materiel between the gates fast.

**Identified by:** standing in it. It is obviously a road.

**Activated by:** clearing and lighting it, and opening the far end. **It is not magical and it has not failed** — it is a corridor with rubble in it and a shut door at one end.

| WP01 §15.4 | |
|---|---|
| **Local** | Free movement between S9 and S12 |
| **Regional** | **Greybank ↔ the Old Delving in ½ watch, against 4 watches on the surface** (WP05 §4.2). It bypasses Hagsfoot entirely, which means it bypasses **Red-Tusk observation** |
| **Instability** | **+1.** The gallery runs the length of the line, and moving through it disturbs three spans |
| **Faction** | **The elves become alarmed in a way nothing else in this document achieves.** Hollow clock **+1, and +1 again** if it is used to move anything out of the site. A route past their perimeter is the specific thing Vaerelin exists to prevent. **The Red-Tusk notice the party arriving from an impossible direction** |
| **Reversible** | Yes — shut the far gate and it is a corridor to nowhere |

> **This is the campaign's clearest example of dungeon progress rewriting the overworld.** A four-watch journey becomes half a watch, and WP05's whole northern travel economy changes. It is also the thing most likely to push the Laughing Hollow to stage 5.

**Who wants what:** **The Hollow wants this sealed above all else.** Threll would pay a great deal for it and must never learn it exists. The Red-Tusk would use it and are not stupid enough to want it.

## 6.6 System 5 — The Deterrent Chain

**S11, Stage III.** A run of resonating plates along the gallery wall, built to make a specific sound at a specific depth — the sound that tells things bred for this place to go elsewhere.

**Identified by:** the workshop records (S6), or by watching what happens when a plate is struck by accident.

**Activated by:** striking the chain in sequence. **A full watch of careful, tedious work**, and it can be done badly.

| WP01 §15.4 | |
|---|---|
| **Local** | **Drives the Listener out of whichever gallery the party chooses** and into another. It does not kill it and it does not calm it — **it moves it** |
| **Regional** | **Reduces wilderness threat near Greybank by one ladder stage** while active — Z4, Z5 and Z6 hazards all drop one. The Red-Tusk notice things stop coming at them from underground |
| **Instability** | **−1 if run correctly** through the full sequence. **+1 if run badly** — a partial chain agitates rather than repels |
| **Faction** | **The Red-Tusk notice first and are grateful in a way they will not articulate** — Red-Tusk Rep **+1**, and in the §8.3 branch count this is worth Compact +1. The elves notice; Hollow clock **+1**; **Hollow Rep +1** if run correctly, because it is repair rather than exploitation |
| **Reversible** | Yes. It stops when it stops |

> **The only system in the site that makes both the goblins and the elves think better of the party at once.** It is also tedious, unglamorous, and produces no treasure. That is the point.

## 6.7 System 6 — Sentinel Authorisation

**S14, Stage IV.** The Sentinel's walk — a long chamber with a recess at each end, and between them a floor worn into a groove by an age of the same footsteps.

**Identified by:** the Approach of Names (S13), the workshop records, or **watching the Sentinel and understanding what it is doing** (§11.2).

**Activated by:** presenting an authorisation at the recess **while the Sentinel is elsewhere on its circuit.** This requires knowing its circuit, which takes observation, which takes time in a gallery it patrols.

| WP01 §15.4 | |
|---|---|
| **Local** | **The Sentinel stops treating the bearer as an incursion.** Not friendship — reclassification. It will walk past them, and it will not walk past anyone else |
| **Regional** | If the authorisation is **corrected rather than merely claimed** — the party fixes its map instead of adding themselves to its list — **the Sentinel resumes patrolling the line as built.** It stops killing things inside the perimeter and starts turning back things crossing it. **Wilderness threat drops one stage across the whole region.** Z11, Z16 and Z17 all become survivable |
| **Instability** | **−2 if corrected. 0 if merely claimed. +1 if the recess is forced** |
| **Faction** | **The elves do not believe this is possible** (§3.4). Doing it: **Hollow Rep +2**, Hollow clock moves toward **6 — cooperative containment**, and **Nethra's position wins.** This is the single largest reputation swing available in the campaign. The Red-Tusk understand it immediately and completely |
| **Reversible** | **Hard.** Claiming can be revoked by another claim. **Correcting cannot be undone without breaking the Heart** |

> **This is the Type F apex threat's solution** (design doc §14F): the encounter is about information, survival and preparation, and the creature is *resolved* rather than defeated. A party that fixes the Sentinel has beaten the campaign's most dangerous thing without a single successful attack roll.

## 6.8 System 7 — The Ward Heart

**S15, Stage IV.** Where all six spans terminate. A chamber of six seats, three of them dark, one warm to the touch.

**Identified by:** arriving. There is nothing else it could be.

**Activated by:** re-anchoring the line — which requires **at least three living spans**, and the Broken Quarter (S16) holds the three dead ones.

| WP01 §15.4 | |
|---|---|
| **Local** | The Heart runs, or it does not. There is no middle setting |
| **Regional** | **Re-anchored correctly: Instability → 0.** The line holds. The Broken Quarter closes. What has been coming through, stops. **Forced or broken: Instability → 5, and S16 becomes permanently Breached at scale** — the whole north-west quarter opens, and WP01 §6.5 stage 5's *containment failure affecting Greybank directly* is now the campaign's situation |
| **Instability** | **→ 0 if re-anchored. → 5 if forced.** The only system in the site that sets Instability rather than adjusting it |
| **Faction** | **Everyone notices.** The elves know within a watch, from the Hollowmarch anchor. Greybank feels it — WP06 leaves *how* to WP11. The Red-Tusk know exactly what happened |
| **Reversible** | **No.** Whatever the party does here is the campaign's ending condition |

**Requirements to re-anchor** — and every one of them is earned in an earlier stage:

1. **System 1 stabilised**, not forced.
2. **At least two more spans living** — the Hollowmarch anchor is one, and it is tended, which means **Hollow Rep ≥ +2 and Nethra's cooperation.**
3. **The Sentinel corrected** (System 6), or it will interrupt the work as an incursion.
4. **A full watch**, uninterrupted, in the deepest room of the site.

> **The good ending is expensive, cooperative, and available.** It requires the elves, a level 5–6 party, and three earlier systems handled correctly. It is not the only legitimate outcome — design doc §24 — and a party that forces the Heart has produced a real ending too.

# 7. Stage I — The Greybank Gate

**Level 3 · Sectors S1–S4 · ¼ watch from the village**

Cold, dry, dark, and entirely survivable. **Stage I contains no creature that can kill a careful level 3 party**, and that is deliberate: it is the room where the campaign proves it is not lying about danger being readable.

---

### S1 — The Aperture and Ramp

A cut ramp descending forty feet at a shallow grade, wide enough for two carts abreast. The lintel is the jointless stone. **Kael Durrow's boards are nailed across it and two of them are loose** (WP02 G14).

**What's here.** Durrow's lantern-soot on the wall at the ten-foot mark, and his bootprints turning round (WP03 Secret S2). Nothing else. The ramp is clean because something swept it for an age and stopped.

**Danger.** None. **Telegraphing, Level 1:** the air moves. It has been moving for two thousand years.

**Exits.** Up to Z2. Down to S2. **The ramp cannot be closed from inside** — this stage is not a trap.

---

### S2 — The Muster Floor

A hall eighty feet by forty, with stone benches down both walls and pegs cut into the stone above them. A garrison's waiting room.

**What's here.** Fifteen sets of pegs, all empty. **One warden's kit**, complete, under a bench where it was set down and never collected — and in it, **a resonance key** (§5.2). Cut names run the length of the far wall: the roster, and it is legible to anyone who takes a watch over it.

**Danger.** None. **Telegraphing, Level 2:** the roster's last third is cut in a different, hurried hand.

**Exits.** S1, S3, S4 (locked), S5 (locked).

---

### S3 — The Anchor Room

A drum of jointless stone floor to ceiling, with a worn recess at chest height.

**What's here.** **System 1 — the Ward Anchor** (§6.2). The stone is the same as the tower Greybank has been standing under for ninety years, and a party that has seen the tower will say so out loud.

**Danger.** None, unless the anchor is forced.

**Exits.** S2.

---

### S4 — The Watch Chamber · *Locked*

A small round room with a stone table and a basin of dark, still liquid.

**What's here.** **System 2 — the Watch-Glass** (§6.3). It has never stopped working.

**Danger.** None. **Telegraphing, Level 3 — explicit:** the Glass shows six anchors, **three of them dark**, and the dark ones face north-west. A party that looks into it has been told what is wrong with the world in one image, at level 3, for free.

**Exits.** S2. *(Locked — key, or prise the warped shutter and mark S4 **Breached** permanently.)*

# 8. Stage II — The Service Works

**Level 4 · Sectors S5–S8**

Where the site's people lived and worked. **The first stage with something in it that will kill you**, and the first with a system that reaches the surface.

---

### S5 — The Lock Gallery · *Locked*

A hundred feet of corridor with a stone leaf across the middle, counterweighted, opened by resonance.

**What's here.** The Stage I–II barrier (§5.1). The counterweight housing is exposed and can be broken — permanently.

**Danger.** None inherent. **Breaking it makes S5 *Breached*, and the elves notice** (WP01 §15.5 — a breach does not reset).

**Exits.** S2, S6.

---

### S6 — The Workshops

Four connected chambers: a stone-shop, a metal-shop, a store, and a records room with cut tablets in racks.

**What's here.** **A resonance key.** Tools, most useless, some not — **WP13's business.** The records: the site's purpose, the ward line's design, the roster, **the Deterrent Chain's sequence** (System 5), and **the forms of authorisation** (§5.3, route 2). Reading them takes **a full watch and no magic** — the names are cut, not written.

**Danger.** None. **Telegraphing, Level 3:** the records describe the Sentinel by function, plainly, in the register of a maintenance manual.

**Exits.** S5, S7, S9 (locked).

---

### S7 — The Sluice Hall

A hall of channels and counterweighted gates over a black race of moving water. **Loud enough to mask a conversation and everything else.**

**What's here.** **System 3 — the Sluice Works** (§6.4). Wet walkways, a real drop, and gates labelled in cut names.

**Danger.** The hall itself — **a Type D environmental hazard** (design doc §14D), needing no creature. **And the noise: nothing in here hears anything, including the party.**

**Exits.** S6, S8, and **the overflow shaft** — climbable, wet, and coming out in the quarry's lowest bench (§4.5).

---

### S8 — The Drowned Stair · *Breached*

A broad stair descending into standing water. The water is moving, fed from the race above, and it is deep enough at the bottom that the stair simply disappears.

**What's here.** **The Listener** (§11.1). And, on the dry steps above the waterline, **eleven bodies** — goblins, decades old, and two wardens far older.

> **The bodies are the puzzle.** Every corpse is on **dry stone**. There is not one in the water. Design doc §14C requires the weakness be discoverable; this room hands it to any party that counts.

**Danger.** **Type C — puzzle predator.** See §11.1.

**Exits.** S7, and S9 by swimming the flooded gallery.

### S10 — The Long Gallery

Dead straight, level, twelve feet wide, and running the whole spine of the line. It is the longest sightline in the campaign and there is nothing in it to hide behind.

**What's here.** Nothing. **That is the danger.**

**Danger.** **The Sentinel patrols here** (§11.2). **Telegraphing, Level 4 — immediate:** at the gallery's midpoint the floor is worn into a groove **an inch deep by footsteps**, and a party that has read S6's records knows exactly what makes it.

### S11 — The Deterrent Chain

A side gallery lined with resonating plates, each cut with a depth-mark.

The northern terminus, and a ramp up to a doorway that has been **piled shut from the outside** with rock and timber for a generation.

# 11. The Residents

## 11.1 The Listener — Type C, puzzle predator

**S8, the Drowned Stair, and the flooded galleries.** **[CAMPAIGN]**

**What it is.** A **deterrent**, bred or bound by the Illefarn and kept in the water system to discourage anything that came up the channels. It has outlived its keepers, its instructions and its purpose. It is blind, it has always been blind, and it has never needed not to be.

**Goblins call it the Listener.** The records in S6 call it a Deep Ward.

**How it hunts.** **Vibration through stone.** A footfall on dry rock is a beacon. It is fast in water, patient out of it, and it does not pursue what it cannot feel.

### The weakness

> **Running water hides you.** Standing still in the moving channel, the current's noise drowns a body's signature through the stone. **The water is the safe place and the dry stair is the killing ground** — precisely inverted from what any party will assume.

### How it is discovered

**Five routes.** Design doc §14C requires the weakness be discoverable; this is not a single check.

| Route | What they learn |
|---|---|
| **Count the bodies (S8)** | Eleven dead, **every one on dry stone, none in the water** |
| **The records (S6)** | It is described by function: *a ward that reads the floor* |
| **Ghesh or Orbal** | Goblins died on the stairs. **None ever died in the channels** — and the goblins never worked out why |
| **Watch it** | It ignores a thrown stone that lands in water and goes instantly to one that lands on stone |
| **The Deterrent Chain (S6 records)** | The sequence exists specifically to move it |

### The solutions

All of these work. None is "correct."

| Solution | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Stand in the water and wait it out** | Nerve, and time. Free |
| **Cross in the channel rather than on the stair** | Free, once understood |
| **Drain S8 with System 3** | **Stops Greybank's mill and changes the ford** (§6.4). The village pays for it |
| **Drive it with System 5** | A full watch in S11, and it goes somewhere else — possibly somewhere worse |
| **Kill it** | **Type B, expensive combat** at level 4–5. Possible, costly, and it is the only resident that can be killed |
| **Feed it** | It is not fussy |

**If killed.** The lower galleries are safe. **Instability +1** — it was a working part of the site, and the water system was the last thing keeping anything else out of the channels. WP07 owns what notices.

**If ignored.** It stays in S8. It does not roam upward. **The party can complete the entire campaign without ever meeting it**, provided they take the dry routes — which is itself a decision, since the wet route bypasses the S9 barrier.

## 11.2 The Sentinel — Type F, apex threat

**S10, S14, and the whole length of the line.** **[CAMPAIGN]**

**What it is.** The Marrowward's defensive construct. Stone and something else. **It is not intelligent, not evil, and not negotiable** — it is a machine executing an instruction set that survived while its map did not.

**Its instruction, intact:** *turn back what crosses the line.*
**Its map, corrupted:** it no longer knows where the line runs, and it no longer holds a current list of who is authorised to be inside it.

**Consequently:** anything inside the perimeter that is not on its list is an incursion. **The Red-Tusk were inside the perimeter** (§3.3). So is Greybank. **So is the party, from the moment they step past the boards.**

**Goblins call it the Counter**, because it walks the same path and takes the same number of steps, every time.

### Why it cannot be fought

**At levels 3–6, it cannot be meaningfully damaged.** WP07 will supply the numbers; WP06 supplies the ruling: **there is no attack sequence available to this party that produces a dead Sentinel.** A party that commits to a stand-up fight in the Long Gallery should die there, and the campaign will have told them four times before they do.

### Telegraphing — all four levels, in order

| Level | Where | What |
|---:|---|---|
| **1 — Suspicion** | Z17, and Ghesh | *"Something came at night. It did not hurry."* |
| **2 — Warning** | S6 records | It is described by function, in a maintenance register, without alarm |
| **3 — Explicit** | S10 | **A groove worn an inch into stone by footsteps** |
| **4 — Immediate** | S10, S14 | It arrives. It does not run. **It does not stop** |

### The four solutions

Design doc §14F: the encounter is about survival, information, escape, diversion, sealing, and future preparation.

| # | Solution | Requires | Permanence |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Be outside the line.** It does not pursue past the perimeter — up the ramp to Z2, out through S16 onto Hagsfoot | Understanding where the line runs. **The Watch-Glass shows you, at level 3, for free** | Always available. **This is why Stage I is generous** |
| **2** | **Seal it behind a barrier.** The S5, S9 and S13 leaves all hold it | A key, and being on the right side | Temporary. It walks another way |
| **3** | **Get authorised.** System 6, *claimed* | Reaching S14 with a name (§5.3) | Works, and works only for the bearer |
| **4** | **Correct its map.** System 6, *corrected* | Everything above, plus understanding the problem | **The real solution.** Instability −2, region-wide threat drops, and the elves are proved wrong in the way they most needed to be |

**If the Red-Tusk leave.** See §15. **This is what fills the gap.**

## 11.3 Everything else

**WP07 owns the rest of the site's occupants**, and there should not be many. WP06 fixes only where they may be and what they may not do:

| Where | What may be there | What may not |
|---|---|---|
| S8 and the flooded galleries | Things that came up the water | Anything that makes the Listener redundant |
| **S16 and the Broken Quarter** | **Whatever is currently coming through the hole** — this is the site's open door and WP07's main opportunity | Anything that could not plausibly walk in from Hagsfoot Moor |
| S6, S11 | Nothing living. These are working rooms | — |
| **Stage I entirely** | **Nothing that can kill a careful level 3 party** | — |

> **The site is not densely populated and must not become so.** Two named residents, one hole letting things in, and a great deal of empty stone that has been empty for two thousand years. The dread is architectural.

**Reading the ledger:**

- **Two systems lower Instability without cost** — the Anchor and the Deterrent Chain, both stabilising rather than exploiting. **The site rewards repair.**
- **Two systems are pure information or pure utility** — the Watch-Glass and the Sluice — and one of them stops the village mill.
- **The Transit Ring is the only system with no downside for the party and a serious one for the region**: it is the thing most likely to push the Laughing Hollow to armed confrontation.
- **Every system except the Watch-Glass moves at least one faction clock.** There is no quiet way to use this place.

# 15. If the Red-Tusk Leave

**The answer WP04 and WP05 both deferred.**

The Red-Tusk piled the Kettlerock Gate shut and have maintained the pile for a generation, out of terror, **without understanding what they were containing.** Sixty-one goblins living at Kettlerock have been, entirely by accident, the only thing keeping the north gate shut.

**On the Flight branch (WP04 §6.4, F4–F6):**

| Stage | What happens at the site |
|---|---|
| **F4 — Withdrawal** | Camps abandoned. **Nobody is walking up to the Delving to re-stack fallen rock any more** |
| **F5 — Migration** | The band is inside elven country for two days. **The pile is unmaintained.** Weather and the slope do the rest |
| **F6 — Gone** | **The Kettlerock Gate is open** — sector S12 becomes *Breached*, permanently (§13) |

**And then:**

> **The Sentinel walks out.**
>
> Its corrupted circuit runs the length of the line, and the line runs to Greybank. It does not hurry. It is not hunting anyone. It is patrolling a perimeter that has three villages' worth of unauthorised people standing inside it, and the nearest of them is a hundred and forty-three people who have built a tower, a shrine and a wall onto its anchors.

**Timing:** WP04 §12.2 puts F5–F6 in session 4. **The Sentinel appears on the surface at Z7 Stonecrop Rise, then Z14 Wend's Barrow, then Greybank** — walking the arc, in that order, one waystone per night.

**Why this is fair.** Design doc §29 forbids retroactively defeating player plans. Nothing here is retroactive:

- **Ghesh and Orbal both say what they ran from**, from session 2, and both accounts are accurate as far as they go (§3.4).
- **The pile is visibly maintained.** Any party that reaches Z17 sees rock stacked by hands, recently.
- **The Watch-Glass shows the line**, and the line ends at Greybank.
- **The party chose Flight** by fighting refugees instead of asking what they were running from — WP04 §14.4 already worked this example.

**Reversing it.** Re-stack the pile (hours, and it holds for a while). Shut the gate from inside. Or solve the Sentinel properly — §11.2, solution 4 — which is what the campaign has been pointing at since the Watch-Glass.

# 16. Map Specifications

For WP15. **Four stage maps plus one section.**

**Per-stage maps (S1–S4, S5–S8, S9–S12, S13–S16):**

1. Sectors keyed **S1–S16** with WP01 §15.2 state boxes printed empty, for the DM to fill in play.
2. **Barriers marked with their route count** — every one shows *"2 routes"* or *"3 routes"* (§5.1), so the DM never forgets there is another way.
3. **Systems marked ★** with their number, and a margin box carrying §14's ledger row for each system on that map.
4. **Exits highlighted** — §4.5's list, per stage, with the surface zone each reaches.
5. Scale bar. The Long Gallery is the longest sightline in the campaign and must read as such.

**The section drawing** — one sheet, cutaway, showing all four stages in vertical relation, **both gates**, the overflow shaft, and **S16 breaking the surface at Hagsfoot Moor.** This is the sheet that makes the site legible as one place.

**Must show on the DM copy only:** the Sentinel's circuit, drawn as a line through S10, S14 and — at Instability 3+ — up and out to Z7.

**No player map of the interior.** The players draw it. That is the genre.

# 17. Worked Examples

---

## 17.1 Example A — Session 1, level 3, forty minutes underground

**Situation.** The party pulls Kael Durrow's loose boards and goes in.

### A1. What happens

**S1** — a clean ramp, moving air, Durrow's soot at the ten-foot mark and his bootprints turning round. *(He will deny it. WP03 Secret S2.)*

**S2** — an empty garrison room, fifteen sets of pegs, **a warden's kit with a resonance key in it**, and a roster whose last third is cut in a hurried hand.

**S3** — the Anchor Room. Someone says *"this is the same stone as the tower."*

**S4** — locked. The key opens it. **The Watch-Glass.**

### A2. What they take away

**No combat.** Nothing in Stage I can kill them (§7).

And from the Glass: **six anchors, three of them dark, and the dark ones face north-west.** They now know — at level 3, in session 1, for free — that there is a line, that it is broken, and where the break is.

### A3. Why this is right

Design doc §2.1: *dangerous situations should usually be readable before they become fatal.* The Marrowward's front room hands the party the shape of the whole campaign, and asks nothing for it.

**And it makes every later refusal fair.** When the Sentinel walks up the Long Gallery in session 3 and cannot be hurt, the party will have known for two sessions that there was a line and something walking it.

### A4. State changes

| System | Change |
|---|---|
| Sectors | S1, S2, S3 → **Accessible.** S4 → **Accessible** *(or Breached if they prised the shutter)* |
| Systems | 1 → **Identified.** 2 → **Active** — it never stopped |
| Instability | **0.** Looking is not touching |
| Factions | **None.** Nobody notices |

> **A whole session's worth of dungeon, with no fight, no loss and no clock movement.** Then they walk back up the ramp into a village where the fold has been raided again while they were down there.

---

## 17.2 Example B — The Listener, solved by counting

**Situation.** Session 2–3. Level 4. The party comes down the Drowned Stair.

### B1. What they see

Standing water, moving. A stair disappearing into it. **And eleven bodies — nine goblins, two wardens — every single one on dry stone.**

### B2. The moment

A player says: *"why is nobody in the water?"*

**That is the puzzle solved**, and it took no check, no spell and no roll. Design doc §14C: *the weakness must be discoverable.* §11.1 gives five routes in and this is the cheapest.

### B3. Confirming it

They throw a stone. It lands on dry rock, and something very large moves at once. They throw another into the channel. **Nothing.**

### B4. Their options

| Choice | Result |
|---|---|
| Wade the channel and stand still while it passes | **Works.** Free. Terrifying |
| Cross in the water rather than on the stair | **Works**, and bypasses the S9 barrier entirely (§5.1) |
| Drain it with the Sluice | Works — **and stops Greybank's mill** (§17.3) |
| Drive it with the Deterrent Chain | A full watch, and **Red-Tusk Rep +1** |
| Fight it | **Type B.** Winnable at level 4–5, expensive, and **Instability +1** |

### B5. The point

The party crossed the campaign's Type C encounter **by counting corpses and standing still in cold water.** No resource spent, no hit point lost, and the DM should let that feel like a victory — because it is one, and it is the exact victory design doc §2.2 asks the campaign to make possible.

---

## 17.3 Example C — Draining the stair stops the mill

**Situation.** The party decides the Listener is not worth the risk. They go up to S7 and open the sluice gates.

### C1. Immediately

They work the gates over a full watch — slowly, per §6.4, so **Instability does not rise.** The lower galleries drain. **S8 → Breached** (§13): the Listener's water is gone, and **it goes somewhere**, and where it went is now a fact WP07 owns.

### C2. Within a day, above ground

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The Marrow drops** | Corvin Ash knows before anyone (WP03 §5.9) |
| **The mill wheel stops** | Undershot wheel, insufficient flow. **Mill → Disabled** |
| **Food −1 at next dawn** | WP01 §14.2 |
| **The shoal opens permanently** | WP05's route 6 is now a road. **Anything that has been watching Greybank can walk in below the mill** |
| **Bythe Ford drops to ankle-deep** | The detour is now easy — a genuine benefit |
| **Hollow clock +1** | §6.4 |

### C3. What the village does

Corvin Ash goes to the Reeve. Ondra Vell writes it down. **Nobody in Greybank connects a stopped mill to four adventurers who went into a hole in the quarry** — until Ordga Brune, who has been saying the stone is wrong for two years, connects it in front of witnesses.

**Greybank Reputation −1** if it becomes known. **Greybank Reaction +1** either way: the mill stopping is a public crisis.

### C4. Undoing it

**One watch at the gates, and a day for the river to settle** (§6.4). Entirely reversible — and in the meantime the party has spent two days at Food −1 with the shoal standing open.

> **They solved a dungeon problem and created a village problem.** WP01 Example C's principle exactly: *the tactical solution created a strategic problem rather than being punished as a "wrong" choice.*

---

## 17.4 Example D — Meeting the Sentinel and living

**Situation.** Session 3. Level 5. The party is in the Long Gallery when it arrives.

### D1. What they have already been told

Four times (§11.2): Ghesh's account; the S6 records describing it in a maintenance register; **the groove worn an inch into the floor**; and now it is here.

### D2. What it does

It comes down the gallery at a walking pace and does not stop. It does not shout, charge, or feint. **Damage does not slow it.** Two rounds of this and any competent party understands.

### D3. Their options, all available

| Option | Works? |
|---|---|
| **Run for S9 and shut the leaf** | **Yes** — §11.2 solution 2. Temporary; it walks another way |
| **Run for S12 and out onto Z17** | **Yes.** The gate opens outward from inside without tools. They are now four watches from home on the wrong side of Hagsfoot |
| **Run for S13 and hold** | Only if they have a name (§5.3) |
| **Stand and fight** | **No.** There is no attack sequence at this level that produces a dead Sentinel |
| **Get outside the line** | **Yes** — and the Watch-Glass told them where the line runs, in session 1 |

### D4. What they learn by surviving

Watching it walk tells them the thing they need: **it takes the same path, and it counts.** That is System 6's precondition (§6.7) — using the recess requires knowing its circuit, and knowing its circuit requires having watched it and lived.

> **The apex threat's first appearance is an information-gathering encounter**, exactly as design doc §14F specifies. They ran, and running was the correct play, and it bought them the solution.

# 18. Edge Cases and Adjudication Guidance

## 18.1 The party wants to bring help in

**Nobody from Greybank will go**, for any inducement (WP03 §14.8, canon). Not Berrick, not Ordga, not for coin, not at Reputation +3.

Who will: **nobody the party can hire locally.** Imported help means Daggerford — Threll will not fund it (WP04 §11: he does not go into ruins and does not send free swords), and **Malk will**, at her price, which is first refusal on everything they find.

## 18.2 A level 3 party enters at the Old Delving or through S16

**Let them, and do not scale anything down.** Design doc §2.1: the world does not scale.

Both routes drop them into level 4–5 content, and both are telegraphed: the Delving is **Stage 3 — Escalation** on WP05's hazard table with a door piled shut from outside, and S16 is a collapsed slope with bones in it. **Both have exits** (§4.5). A party that walks in, sees the Long Gallery's groove, and walks out has played correctly.

## 18.3 The party destroys a waystone

**Allow it, and make the cost explicit afterward, not before.** §13's last row: that span cannot be re-anchored, and destroying a second one **puts the good ending permanently out of reach.**

WP08 must have somebody say the stones are old and should be left alone **before** anyone is holding a hammer — design doc §29's fairness contract. After that, it is theirs.

## 18.4 The party commits to fighting the Sentinel

Give them two full rounds of unmistakable evidence: damage that does nothing, and a thing that does not react to being hurt. **Then let the consequences stand** (design doc §6, Level 4).

**Escape remains possible for as long as they want it.** It never sprints, and it never stops. A party that keeps fighting has chosen.

## 18.5 The party never enters the site

**Entirely valid.** WP04 §12.2 already establishes that Illefarn stays at Instability 1 across four sessions if nobody touches it, and the Laughing Hollow never advances past Observation.

That campaign is about Greybank and the Red-Tusk, ends with the village at war or in compact, and **the elves are a rumour Ib Sallow tells.** Per design doc §22, unused material stays unused.

## 18.6 Long rest inside

**Permitted, and it costs 2 watches** (WP01 §11). Stage I is genuinely safe. Stage II is safe away from S8. **Stages III and IV are not** — the Sentinel walks a circuit and two watches is several circuits.

Do not invent a rule forbidding it. **Let the circuit be the reason.**

## 18.7 The party sells the Watch-Glass

Malk will pay more for it than for anything else in the site (§6.3), and it is portable.

**Let them.** They lose the only reliable view of the ward line, permanently, and every later decision is made blind. Design doc §20's loot philosophy is that items create strategic choices; this is the sharpest one in the campaign.

## 18.8 The wardens follow them in

At Hollow stage **4+**, yes — to remove artifacts, not to fight (WP04 §5.3). They will take a resonance key or the Watch-Glass out of a camp inside the site as readily as out of Sember's store.

At Hollow **Rep +2** with Nethra's cooperation, **she comes with them**, and she is the only outside help available in this document.

## 18.9 Instability reaches 6 in session 3

Fine. WP01 §6.5: *"Clock 6 creates a new situation rather than automatically ending the campaign."* **Do not slow the clock to protect WP11's material** — design doc §22 forbids it.

The rest of the campaign is then played in whatever region stage 6 produced, which is more interesting than the one before it.

## 18.10 The party splits between stages

Use §4.2's level bands honestly. A pair in Stage I is safe; a pair in Stage III is not, and the Long Gallery is half a watch from the Anchor Room. **Do not reunite them for convenience** (WP01 §21.6).

## 18.11 The players ask what the Sentinel wants

**Nothing.** It is not a character, it has no goal, and it cannot be bargained with, frightened, or persuaded. Say so plainly if a player tries.

**The negotiable thing is its instruction set, and that is in S14** — which is the whole design. The campaign's most dangerous entity is solved at a recess in a wall, by people who read a maintenance register.

# 19. Final Canonical WP06 Rules

## What it is

**The Marrowward** — an Illefarn frontier **ward line**, not a vault. WP05's six waystones **are** its anchors; the arc **is** the perimeter; the two gates sit at its focus and its end. **Three of the six spans have been dead for an age**, and they face north-west.

## The framework

WP01 §15, unmodified. **Sixteen sectors** on WP01 §15.2's six states; **seven systems** on §15.3's six states; every activation written to §15.4's five-part template; breaches permanent per §15.5. **No second meter anywhere.**

## The stages

**I** Greybank Gate, level 3 · **II** Service Works, level 4 · **III** Transit Ring, level 4–5 · **IV** Ward Heart, level 5–6.
**Every stage is exitable at the level it is entered**, by at least two routes, none of which is a check (§4.5).

## Unlock

**Every barrier has at least two routes**, and no stage requires a single successful check. **Three resonance keys are in the site**, one is held by the Hollow. The Stage IV barrier is **a challenge that accepts a name, and does not punish a wrong answer** — and **Orbal's cords hold one**.

## Stage I is generous on purpose

**Nothing in Stage I can kill a careful level 3 party.** The Watch-Glass shows the entire ward line, free, in session 1. This is the campaign keeping design doc §2.1's promise before it starts charging.

## The residents

**Two.** The **Listener** (Type C) in the flooded galleries — blind, hunts vibration through stone, and **running water hides you**, discoverable five ways, of which counting the corpses is the cheapest. The **Sentinel** (Type F) walking the line — a construct with an intact instruction and a corrupted map, **not intelligent, not evil, not negotiable, and not killable at these levels.** Four solutions, none of them a fight.

**The site is not densely populated and must not become so.**

## The truth

**The Laughing Hollow is right about every fact and wrong about one possibility** — that the site can only be sealed, never repaired. This preserves WP04's binding rule exactly: they never exaggerated. **Nobody holds the whole picture, including them.**

## The ledger

**§14 is binding: every system has a written regional consequence.** Two systems lower Instability by repairing rather than exploiting. **Every system except the Watch-Glass moves at least one faction clock. There is no quiet way to use this place.**

## Instability

**Nothing here advances on a timer.** The site sat at 1 for two thousand years. Every step up the clock has a name and a person who took it.

## The Flight consequence

**The Red-Tusk have been holding the Kettlerock Gate shut without knowing it.** If they leave, S12 breaches permanently and **the Sentinel walks out** — Stonecrop Rise, then Wend's Barrow, then Greybank, one waystone per night. Telegraphed from session 2 by two goblins and a stack of rock maintained by hand.

## The endings

**System 7 sets Instability to 0 or to 5 and is not reversible.** Re-anchoring requires the Anchor stabilised, **two more living spans including the Hollowmarch anchor** (Hollow Rep ≥ +2), the Sentinel corrected, and an uninterrupted watch. **The good ending is expensive, cooperative, and available** — and forcing the Heart is a real ending too.

# WP06 Completion Criterion

**The judging test:** every activation has a written overworld consequence — §14 is that table — and every stage is enterable and exitable at its stated level band — §4.5 is that list.

WP06 is complete when later packages can answer questions like these without inventing site content:

- What do the six waystones mean, and what happens to them at Instability 2?
- A level 3 party goes in on day one. What can they learn, and what can kill them?
- What are the three ways past every locked door?
- The party drains the flooded gallery. What happens to Greybank's mill, the shoal, and the ford?
- How does a level 4 party cross the Drowned Stair without fighting anything?
- Why can the Sentinel not be killed, and what are the four things that work instead?
- What does activating the transit route do to WP05's travel times, and which faction reacts?
- What exactly did the Red-Tusk run from, and how much of their account is true?
- What happens to the site if the Red-Tusk migrate away?
- What does it take to set Instability to 0, and who must the party be friends with to do it?
- Which single act puts the good ending permanently out of reach?
- What does the Laughing Hollow have wrong?
