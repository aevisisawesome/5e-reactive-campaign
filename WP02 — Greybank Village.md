# The Broken March — WP02: Greybank Village

**Status:** Canonical campaign location document
**Consumes:** `Campaign design doc.md`, `WP01 — Campaign State Model.md`
**Feeds:** WP03, WP04, WP05, WP07, WP08–WP12, WP13, WP14, WP15, WP16
**Scope:** Greybank's physical layout, keyed locations, households, services, economy, critical-stock placement, defences, fire behaviour, infrastructure states, the costed upgrade catalogue, and the village daily cycle

This document makes Greybank a place a DM can run from memory by the second session, and makes every WP01 variable visible on the ground. WP01 says *what Trade 2 does*; WP02 says *which cart fails to arrive, whose shelves empty, and who complains about it first*. It defines buildings, ownership, stock, distances, sightlines, muster points, and what each structure costs to build, break, and repair. It deliberately leaves personalities, wants, fears and secrets to WP03, faction politics to WP04, everything outside the village boundary stone to WP05, and the Illefarn interior to WP06.

## 1.3 Design decisions — reviewed and confirmed canonical

These nine points were decided during drafting rather than escalated, then **reviewed and confirmed by the DM**. They are canon. Later work packages consume them as fixed; they are not open questions.

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Population fixed at **143** at campaign start. | Design doc §3.2 allows 120–160. A fixed number lets households, militia rolls and casualty counts be real. |
| 2 | Greybank sits on the **north bank** of a river called **the Marrow**; locals say *"the Grey."* | The village needs a named river for the bridge, mill, wharf and ferry to be discussable. |
| 3 | The village is named for the pale grey limestone silt its river carries down from the quarry. | Ties name, geology, quarry and Illefarn site into one fact. |
| 4 | The shrine's keeper is a **lay-keeper with no spellcasting whatsoever**. | WP01 §14.5 defers religious function here and states magical services exist only if an NPC actually possesses them. A caster would silently undo the campaign's medicine scarcity. See §9.5. |
| 5 | The village's governing office is the **Reeve**, chosen by the moot, serving until the moot removes them. | Design doc gives no title. "Reeve" is period-plausible and implies removability, which makes Unity politically consequential. |
| 6 | The watchtower is an **Illefarn-era stone stub** the village roofed over, not a village-built tower. | Explains a stone tower in a 143-person settlement, and puts Illefarn masonry in the players' eyeline from session 1. |
| 7 | The **palisade does not exist yet** — only a decayed earth-and-stake ring around the core, breached in four places. | Required to justify Defense 2, and makes the design doc §18 palisade upgrade a real first-tier goal. |
| 8 | Greybank's principal export is **dressed stone**, not food. | Explains why a village this small has a bridge, a road, a wharf and Daggerford's attention, and makes quarry disruption an economic event rather than a local inconvenience. |
| 9 | Names are assigned here for **placement purposes**. | Households cannot be tracked anonymously. WP03 inherits these names and owns everything else about them. |

# 3. Greybank at a Glance

## 3.1 The one-paragraph pitch

**[CAMPAIGN]** Greybank is a stone village that ran out of reasons to be one. A hundred and forty-three people live on the north bank of the Marrow at the only bridge for a day's walk in either direction, cutting pale limestone out of a hillside their great-grandparents did not dig and cannot account for. The stone goes to Daggerford. The coin comes back thin. The quarry face has begun opening onto worked walls nobody built, the militia is eleven farmers with a shed of billhooks, and the earth ring that used to enclose the village has been breached in four places for thirty years because nothing has needed to come through it. Something has now started needing to.

# 4. Site and Geography

## 4.1 The Marrow **[CAMPAIGN]**

A tributary running south-east to join the Delimbiyr **[LORE]** below Daggerford. At Greybank it is:

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Width at the bridge | ~60 ft |
| Depth mid-channel, normal | 8–11 ft — not fordable |
| Depth mid-channel, late summer low water | 4–5 ft at the shoal below the mill — **fordable with difficulty** |
| Current | Moderate; strong enough to carry a body downstream overnight |
| Colour | Milk-grey with limestone silt, opaque below 2 ft |

Three consequences the DM should hold onto:

> **The water is opaque.** Anything dropped in the Marrow is functionally hidden. This matters for evidence disposal under WP01 §7.4 concealment.

> **The river is a wall.** It stops most land creatures. The bridge is therefore both Greybank's economy and its only reliable breach.

> **The shoal is the campaign's back door.** In late summer, the shoal below the mill is passable on foot. Villagers know it. Anything that has watched the village long enough may learn it.

## 4.2 Terrain **[CAMPAIGN]**

Greybank occupies a shallow north-bank shelf between the water and rising ground.

- **South:** the river, then the far bank — coppiced woodland, charcoal ground, and the Daggerford road climbing away south-east.
- **North:** the ground rises through open field to a knoll bearing the tower, then breaks into rough moor-edge scrub within a mile.
- **North-west:** the quarry, cut into the hillside, 500 yards upslope of the village, reached by a stone-rutted haul road.
- **East:** the best arable, flat, along the water.
- **West:** poorer ground — the fold, byres, and grazing.

The village is **overlooked from the north**. The knoll and the quarry lip both command it. This is why the tower is where it is, and it is a standing tactical fact for every encounter set in Greybank.

## 4.5 Internal distances and movement

Greybank is **about 500 yards end to end**. WP01 §10.2 rates local movement as negligible time — that holds for the watch clock. These figures exist for **rounds**, which is where they matter: pursuit, mobilization, fire, and getting to someone before something else does.

| From → To | Distance | Walk | Run | Rounds (30 ft move) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bridge → Green | 220 yd | 2.5 min | 50 s | ~22 |
| Green → Mill | 180 yd | 2 min | 40 s | ~18 |
| Green → Tower (uphill) | 300 yd | 4 min | 90 s | ~30 |
| Green → Quarry face | 700 yd | 9 min | 3.5 min | ~70 |
| Store → Smithy | 40 yd | — | — | 4 |
| Cotts (centre) → Green | 90 yd | 1 min | 20 s | ~9 |
| Fold → Green | 260 yd | 3 min | 60 s | ~26 |
| Anywhere in core → anywhere in core | ≤300 yd | ≤4 min | — | ≤30 |

> **Rule of thumb:** anywhere inside the earth ring is **under 30 rounds** from anywhere else at a run. The quarry is not — it is **70 rounds**, roughly seven minutes, and that gap is deliberate. Help from the quarry never arrives during a fight in the village.

## 4.6 Sightlines, cover and chokepoints

**Sightlines**

| Position | Commands | Blocked by |
|---|---|---|
| Tower top (G13), +30 ft | Whole village, fields, both road approaches, quarry lip, 1.5 mi in clear weather | Nothing by day. Useless in fog or Deep Night without light. |
| Quarry lip (G14) | The whole village core from above, 500 yd | Spoil heaps if the observer is careless |
| Bridge (G1) | The south road for 400 yd; the Street for 200 yd | The toll-shed blocks the last 30 yd of the south approach |
| Mill roof (G6) | Millside, the wharf, the shoal, 150 yd of river both ways | The granary blocks the view west |
| Green (G10) | Street, shrine, Moot Hall, militia shed, four Cott lane-mouths | Everything else |

**Cover in the core**

- The Street's buildings present continuous wall on both sides — **full cover** to anything in a doorway, **half cover** behind the water butts set at every third building against fire.
- The Green is **open ground**, 60 yd across, with the well-head (half cover, 1 creature) and the bell post (three-quarters cover, 1 creature) as its only features. Crossing the Green under missile fire is a genuine decision.
- The Cotts lanes are 6–8 ft wide between thatch eaves. **No missile line longer than 25 yd exists inside the Cotts.**

**Chokepoints** — the six places that decide any fight in Greybank

| # | Chokepoint | Width | Why it matters |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **The bridge** | 11 ft | Two abreast. The only vehicle crossing. Holdable by three people. |
| 2 | **The Street, at the smithy** | 15 ft narrowing to 9 ft at the forge buttress | The village's natural barricade line; carts are already stored 20 yd away at G16 |
| 3 | **The four Cott lane-mouths** | 6–8 ft | Whoever holds these controls civilian movement and escape |
| 4 | **The mill causeway** | 8 ft, water both sides | The only dry approach to Millside from the east |
| 5 | **The haul road cut** | 12 ft, banked 6 ft both sides | Between village and quarry; anything coming down from the Pale comes through here |
| 6 | **The four ring gaps** | 20–40 ft each | NW, N, E, SE. Currently the village's four open doors. Closing them *is* the palisade project (§15.1). |

## 4.7 Every way into Greybank

Seven. A DM should know all of them before running a siege, an escape, a burglary, or a mobilization.

| # | Route | Vehicles? | Notes |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | South road → bridge | Yes | The obvious one. Toll-shed, then the Street. |
| 2 | Haul road from the quarry (NW gap) | Yes | Wide enough for stone wagons. Unwatched at night. |
| 3 | North gap, from the moor edge | No | Footpath past the burial ground. Where the tower is *supposed* to be looking. |
| 4 | East gap, through the fields | No | Open approach, but 600 yd of no cover in daylight. |
| 5 | South-east gap, from the riverside path | No | Comes up behind the wagonyard. Overgrown. Locals forget it exists. |
| 6 | **The shoal**, below the mill | No | Late summer only. Waist-deep, slick, 40 ft of exposure. |
| 7 | The wharf, by boat | Small craft | One skiff at Greybank (G8). Anyone with a boat has a route the village does not watch. |

> **DM note.** Routes 5, 6 and 7 are the three the village does not think about. Any intelligent enemy that scouts Greybank for two nights finds at least one of them. Telegraph accordingly — a villager mentioning that "the Ottersby lad crosses at the shoal to save the walk" is a Level 1 signal under design doc §6.

# 7. Keyed Locations

Each entry gives what the DM needs at the table. Personality and dialogue belong to WP03.

**Reading the entries:**
- **Runs it** — the named NPC whose removal changes the location's function. WP03 owns who they *are*.
- **Provides** — mechanical output. If a location provides nothing, it is not keyed.
- **Holds** — Class B critical stock, per §11.
- **WP01** — the infrastructure entry this location is tracked under, if any.
- **If broken** — Greybank-specific consequence, beyond WP01 §14's generic effect.
- **Tactical** — what it does in a fight.

---

## G1 — Marrow Bridge · District I **[CAMPAIGN]**

Three stone piers, timber deck, no parapet on the downstream side since a cart went through it two winters ago and was never properly rebuilt. **11 ft wide.** Ninety feet long. The piers are old and good; the deck is newer and worse.

- **Runs it:** nobody. The Reeve is nominally responsible.
- **Provides:** the only vehicle crossing of the Marrow for a day's travel in either direction. Trade depends on it entirely.
- **WP01:** §14.1 Bridge — currently **INTACT**.
- **If broken:** see §14.1. The immediate, unrolled consequence is **Trade −1** (WP01 Example C). Greybank's own answer is the skiff at G8, which moves people but not stone.
- **Tactical:** 11 ft — two Medium creatures abreast. Holdable by three defenders. **The deck is timber over stone**: the deck burns and can be cut; the piers do not and cannot. Destroying the deck is a matter of hours with axes, or one good application of fire; destroying the piers requires the quarry's blasting stock or something much larger.
- **Note:** the missing downstream parapet is a real hazard in a melee. Any creature shoved or knocked prone within 5 ft of the downstream edge risks a 20 ft fall into 9 ft of opaque water.

## G2 — The Toll-shed · District I **[CAMPAIGN]**

A one-room stone hut on the *south* bank, at the bridge foot. Greybank charges carts, not people: 2 cp a wheel, collected by whoever the Reeve has assigned that tenday. Half the time nobody is in it.

- **Runs it:** rotating villagers. Currently **Denk Ottersby** most days.
- **Provides:** a trickle of village income; a record-book of who has crossed and when — **this is Greybank's only written record of arrivals and departures**, and it matters enormously to WP01 §8 investigations.
- **Holds:** the toll box (rarely over 4 gp), the crossing book.
- **If broken:** the book is the loss, not the hut. Burn the toll-shed and you burn the evidence of who came into Greybank in the last three years.
- **Tactical:** stone, slate roof, one door, one shuttered window facing the bridge. The single best hard point on the south bank. Also the only thing blocking sight of the last 30 yards of the south road from the bridge.

---

## G3 — The Sundered Cart · District II **[CAMPAIGN]**

The taproom, and Greybank's **only lodging**. Two-storey, stone ground floor, timber upper, **thatch**. Common room, kitchen, four rentable pallets in the loft, a stable-lean for two animals.

- **Runs it:** **Rosal Tunn**.
- **Provides:** lodging (5 cp/night per pallet, 4 pallets only); hot food; the village's information exchange. **Every rumour in Greybank passes through this room in the Evening watch.**
- **Holds:** nothing on the Class B list. A cellar with roughly 40 gp of drink.
- **If broken:** the party has nowhere indoors to sleep unless a household takes them in, which is a **Greybank Reputation ≥ +1** favour. The village loses its gathering place — treat as a standing obstacle to any attempt to raise or persuade a crowd. See §14.11.
- **Tactical:** thatch roof, and the highest fire risk on the Street. Common room is 30 × 25 ft with a central hearth — bad ground for a fight, good ground for a confrontation with an audience. The loft has one ladder and one gable window.

---

## G4 — Dray's Store · District II **[CAMPAIGN]**

Greybank's general store. Stone lower course, timber above, **slate** roof — Sember Dray reroofed it in stone after a scare, and it is the only thatch-free building on the Street. Shop front, storeroom behind, living rooms above, a barred back door onto a yard.

- **Runs it:** **Sember Dray**. Assisted irregularly by their nephew **Wat Dray**, who also apprentices at the smithy.
- **Provides:** all Class C ordinary goods per WP01 §5.3 while Trade ≥2; the village's import channel; credit, informally.
- **Holds:** **1 Potion of Healing · 1 healer's kit · 2 specialized spell-component packages · 2 large ammunition bundles.** See §11.
- **WP01:** §14.4 General Store — currently **INTACT + shopkeeper active**.
- **If broken:** WP01 Example A is written about this building. Sember's death **closes** the store; the stock stays physically inside and becomes a village-secured problem, not a shopping opportunity. See §9.3 and §14.4.
- **Tactical:** the only slate roof on the Street — it will not carry fire, which makes it the **firebreak** in §13. Barred back door, shuttered front. Defensible for one person for a short time.

---

## G5 — The Smithy · District II **[CAMPAIGN]**

Open-fronted forge under a slate lean, with a stone workshop and a locked tool-room behind. A buttress narrows the Street to 9 ft in front of it.

- **Runs it:** **Ordga Brune**, shield dwarf **[ADAPTED]** — dwarven smiths on the Sword Coast frontier are lore-plausible; this one is campaign-original. Apprentice: **Wat Dray**.
- **Provides:** metal repair; arrow and bolt production; limited martial weapon production; the only metalworking within two days. Per WP01 §5.7, without a functioning smith, damaged metal gear cannot reliably be repaired and metal armour availability **freezes at current stock**.
- **Holds:** **1 chain shirt · 2 martial weapon-quality items.** The chain shirt was repaired for a militiaman who died before collecting it; the village considers it his family's, which makes buying it a social transaction rather than a commercial one.
- **WP01:** §14.3 Smithy — currently **INTACT**.
- **If broken:** see §14.3. Note the WP01 §14.3 rule sharply: **an intact building without a smith does not provide smithing.** Wat can perform simple repairs at **doubled time and no new production** — he is a partial, not a replacement.
- **Tactical:** the 9 ft buttress pinch is the village's natural barricade line, and the militia knows it (§12.2). A live forge is a hazard and a weapon: coals, quench trough, and loose iron stock.

---

## G6 — The Mill · District IV **[CAMPAIGN]**

Undershot water mill on a stone sluice, timber superstructure, **thatch**. The wheel is the single most valuable piece of engineering in Greybank and the hardest to replace.

- **Runs it:** **Corvin Ash**. His daughter **Lisbet Ash** can run it alone — the only redundant skilled role in the village.
- **Provides:** flour. Underpins **Food**. Also the sluice gate, which can raise the water at the shoal by roughly 18 inches — enough to make the shoal impassable, or to flood the low ground east of Millside.
- **WP01:** §14.2 Mill — currently **INTACT**.
- **If broken:** see §14.2. Food falls at the next dawn if the mill is Disabled. The **sluice** is the interesting part: a party that understands it can close the shoal, and a party that breaks it loses that option permanently.
- **Tactical:** thatch, over a working machine, beside a granary. Fire here is a village-level disaster (§13.4). Mill floor is cramped, with a 10 ft drop to the wheel race.

---

## G7 — The Granary · District IV **[CAMPAIGN]**

Raised stone store on staddle stones, slate roof, one heavy door, one high vent. Currently about two-thirds full; harvest is six weeks out.

- **Runs it:** the Reeve holds the key; **Corvin Ash** holds the working key.
- **Provides:** the physical backing of **Food 4**. This building *is* the Food variable made visible.

## 9.3 What "the store closes" actually means

WP01 Example A establishes the rule. WP02 supplies the physical detail, because players always ask.

If Sember Dray dies:

1. The store is **CLOSED**, not looted. Villagers bar it. Greybank does not casually break into a dead neighbour's shop.
2. The stock inside — **1 Potion of Healing, 1 healer's kit, 2 component packages, 2 ammunition bundles** — physically remains, and is **inaccessible during an active investigation** (WP01 §14.4, Example A4).
3. Wat Dray inherits, and Wat is sixteen and apprenticed elsewhere. He cannot run imports.
4. Ordinary goods (WP01 §5.3) stop being freely obtainable in Greybank **even at Trade 4+**, because the import channel was a person, not a building.
5. If the party is suspected, attempting to buy from the closed store is an **investigation accelerant** under WP01 §8.3.

## 9.4 Service availability by reputation

WP01 §4.2 governs Greybank Reputation effects. This is what those tiers look like at the counter.

| Greybank Rep | At the store, forge, taproom and fold |
|---:|---|
| +3 | Sold at cost. The moot chest will fund a project the party proposes. Lodging free. |
| +2 | Discounts (−10%). Credit extended. Class B stock offered rather than withheld. |
| +1 | Friendly. Class B stock will be sold if asked directly. A household will take the party in. |
| 0 | Normal trade. Class B stock exists but is **not volunteered** — the party must ask for specific items. |
| −1 | Prices at the worst applicable scarcity tier regardless of actual supply. Class B stock "isn't for sale." |

| −3 | Hostile. See §12.6. |

> **Reputation does not change what physically exists.** At −2 the potion is still on Sember's shelf. It is simply not for sale to the party, and taking it is theft with a witness. That distinction is the whole point of tracking stock separately from attitude.

## 9.5 The shrine has no spellcaster **[CAMPAIGN — deliberate]**

Yorrin Fell is a lay-keeper. He performs rites, keeps the shrine, speaks at moot and buries the dead. **He cannot cast a single spell**, cannot *Cure Wounds*, cannot *Lesser Restoration*, cannot *Speak with Dead*, and cannot *Remove Curse*.

This follows WP01 §14.5 — *"magical services exist only if an NPC actually possesses them"* — and it is load-bearing for three campaign systems:

1. **Medicine scarcity (WP01 §5.5)** survives. Two potions, two kits and one antitoxin remain the real limit on how much punishment the party can absorb.
2. **Death stays expensive (design doc §5).** No local raise, no local restoration. A body goes to G19.
3. **Investigation magic (WP01 §8.8)** stays rare without being nerfed. WP01 is explicit that the campaign does not weaken *Speak with Dead* or *Zone of Truth* — it limits **access**. Greybank has no access. Daggerford might; that is a two-day journey and a favour owed, which is exactly the shape of decision the campaign wants.

> If the party wants divine magic in Greybank, they must **bring it, import it, or become it.** A cleric PC is not a problem — they are the answer to a real regional scarcity, and the village will notice.

## 11.2 What the placement is doing

The distribution is deliberate. Four principles:

1. **No single building holds it all.** Burning the store costs the party a potion, a kit, both component packages and half the ammunition — a real loss, not a total one.
2. **Half of it is not purchasable.** The shrine potion, the shield, the ammunition in the militia shed, the chain shirt and the horses are all **social transactions**, gated behind consent, reputation, or theft. WP01 §4.2 reputation effects therefore have something concrete to bite on from session 1.
3. **Medicine is split across the two most fragile providers.** Sember and Maud between them hold every healing item in Greybank. Lose both and the campaign's medical supply is whatever is in the party's packs.
4. **The blasting powder is the loaded gun on the mantel.** Six charges, at the quarry, guarded by a man who has already boarded over something he does not want opened. It is the only thing in the village that can drop a bridge pier — which is to say, it is the toolkit for design doc §2.2's *"lure it beneath a collapsing quarry ceiling"* and *"seal the entrance."* WP07 and WP08 should assume the players will find it.

## 12.5 Shelter and evacuation

The village has a plan, in the sense that everyone knows where to run without having been told.

- **Children and infirm → the granary (G7).** Stone, slate, one barred door. Holds 60 standing.
- **Overflow → the shrine (G9).** Stone, cannot burn, cannot be defended.
- **Livestock → the byres**, or driven east into the fields if there is warning.
- **Full evacuation → south over the bridge**, toward Daggerford. Two days on foot with children. This is why the bridge is Greybank's life in more than one sense.
- **If the bridge is gone → east along the north bank**, which is slower, exposed, and has never been done.

## 12.6 Greybank as a hostile environment

WP01 §9 forbids running village hostility as balanced encounters, and requires the settlement be treated as **terrain**. This is that terrain, on this map.

**What the village does, in order** (WP01 §9.1, localized):

| Phase | On this map |
|---|---|
| Alarm | Bell (G10). Doors barred along the Street. |
| Minutes | Civilians to G7 and G9. Militia to the Green. Dogs loosed from H13–H20. |
| Organized | Carts from G16 to chokepoint 2. **Archers to the Moot Hall roof (G11) — it overlooks the whole Green.** The four Cott lane-mouths watched. Bridge held by three. |
| Denial | No lodging (G3), no forge (G5), no store (G4), no medicine (G22). The party cannot rest, resupply, or be treated. |
| External | A runner for Daggerford, over the bridge — **two days out, two days back** |

**The environmental effects, not an initiative order:**

- Crossing the **Green** (60 yd, open, overlooked from G11) draws missile fire.
- The **Cotts** give cover and take it away — no long lines, but a hundred people who know the lanes.
- Every **door on the Street** is barred. Entry is a Strength check and a noise problem.
- **No safe rest anywhere inside the ring.** Long rest is impossible; short rest is possible only outside.

**Escape routes to telegraph** (WP01 §9.3 requires this):

1. The **shoal**, below the mill — if it is late summer and the party knows about it
2. The **skiff** at G8 — one boat, four seats

4. **North past the burial ground** to the moor edge
5. The **haul road** to the quarry and the Pale — which puts them at the aperture
6. Surrender to the Reeve at the Moot Hall

> **The village will not chase them past the boundary stones on the first night.** Greybank is eleven frightened men who want the party gone more than they want them dead. Per WP01 §9.3, the consequence is *"the village is now hostile and Greybank content may become inaccessible"* — not a party wipe. What follows is a runner to Daggerford, and everything in §9.4 at −3 forever.

## 13.2 Spread

This is a **local procedure, not a new tracked system.** It is the WP01 §12 escalation ladder run at combat tempo, and it stops the moment the fire is out.

| Stage | Interval | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Caught | — | One building alight. Extinguishable by 4 people with buckets in 3 rounds. |
| 2 — Established | +5 rounds | Building lost. Extinguishable only by 10+ people; takes 1/4 watch. |
| 3 — Spread | +5 rounds | **In the Cotts, or the wagonyard, or the mill/granary pair:** the adjacent thatch catches. Elsewhere it does not. |
| 4 — District | +10 rounds | The whole lane, or the whole of Millside, is going. Requires the entire village to fight. |
| 5 — Loss | end | The district is gone. |

**Spread only happens where eaves nearly meet or accelerant is present:**

- **The Cotts (G17)** — lanes 6–8 ft, thatch nearly touching. **Spread is automatic** at stage 3 unless the fire is being fought.
- **The mill and granary (G6/G7)** — 25 ft apart. The granary is slate and stone and will **not** catch, but its contents burn if the doors are open or burned through.
- **The wagonyard (G16)** — hay loft. Add one stage interval's worth of intensity; the fire is worse, not faster.
- **The Street** — buildings are close, but **G4's slate roof breaks the chain.** A fire starting at the taproom does not reach the smithy.

## 13.3 Response

| Resource | Detail |
|---|---|
| Water | The well (G10), the river (150 yd from the Street), butts at every third building on the Street |
| Buckets | ~20 in the village |
| People | Whoever is awake. See §16 — a Deep Night fire has **4–6 responders in the first 5 rounds**; an Afternoon fire has 30 |
| Firebreak | Pull down the adjacent thatch. Enna Fisk (H4) will call for this and be right. |

> **Time of watch decides everything.** The same fire at Afternoon is an incident; at Deep Night it takes the district. §16 is the table that tells you which.

## 13.4 The two catastrophes

**Millside.** The mill is thatch over a working machine beside the granary. A mill fire that reaches stage 4 costs Greybank the mill *and* whatever grain was out of the granary: **Mill → DESTROYED, Food −2.** This is the single worst non-lethal outcome available to a careless party.

**The Cotts.** Twenty-two thatched dwellings with touching eaves. A stage-4 Cotts fire takes a whole lane — five or six households homeless. **Three or more cotts lost in one night is Unity −1** (§7, G17). Six or more is **Unity −2** and begins the abandonment discussion.

## 13.5 Accelerants on the map

Four, and the players will find all of them:

1. **The pitch barrel** in the militia shed (G12), 20 yards off the Green.
2. **The hay loft** at the wagonyard (G16).
3. **The charcoal stacks** at G20, across the water.
4. **Six charges of blasting powder** in the quarry powder store (G14). Not a fire tool — a demolition tool. See §11.2.

# 15. Infrastructure Upgrade Catalogue

Design doc §18 lists the upgrades. This costs them.

**How to read these:** every project needs **coin, materials, labour, time, and consent**. Coin is usually the easiest of the five. Consent and Unity are usually the hardest, and that is the design intent — the party's real currency in Greybank is reputation, not gold.

**Standing rules for all projects:**

1. Any project needing a levy requires the **Reeve's agreement**, or a moot vote if she refuses.
2. Volunteer numbers scale with Unity (§14 preamble). At **Unity 2** they halve; at **Unity 1** Defense cannot rise above 3 by collective action; at **Unity 0** there is no levy.
3. **Without Enna Fisk (H4), every build and repair time increases by half.**
4. Materials cannot be conjured: Stone needs the quarry working, Timber needs the coppice and the far bank reachable.
5. Work proceeds while the party is elsewhere. Projects are a reason for time to pass, not something the party must supervise.
6. A project under way is **visible and attackable.** Half-built defences are a target.

## 16.3 Sound

- The **bell** (G10) carries across the core and the near fields. **Not** to the quarry, bunkhouse, fold, charcoal camp, or outlying farmsteads.
- The **horn** (G12) carries to the quarry and the far bank. Not to the outlying farmsteads.
- A **scream in the Cotts** wakes the lane. A scream at the fold reaches nobody.
- The **mill** runs day-loud; anything happening at Millside in a working watch is heard by nobody and seen by Corvin Ash alone.
- **Combat in the core, in any watch, brings the village.** There is no such thing as a quiet fight inside the ring.

# 17. Worked Examples

Each traces an ordinary event through WP02 geography into concrete WP01 state.

## 17.1 Example A — Fire at the wagonyard, Deep Night

**Situation.** A lantern goes over in the hay loft at G16 during the Deep Night watch. Nobody intends it; a PC knocked it, or an enemy set it, or a drunk did. The mechanics are identical.

### A1. Who is awake

§16.1, Deep Night: **2–3 people.** Nissa Crake (H2), possibly Maud Elleth (G22), nobody else.

Nissa is 90 yards away and wakes easily. She reaches the bell in **~1 minute**.

### A2. The fire

§13.2. The wagonyard is thatch **with a hay loft** — accelerant present.

| Round | Stage |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Caught. Four people with buckets could stop this. **There are not four people.** |
| ~5 | Established. The cart shed and stable are lost. |
| ~10 | Spread — but §13.2 says the Street does not chain, and the **store's slate roof (G4) breaks it.** The fire does not reach the smithy or the taproom. |

The firebreak holds. This is the store's roof paying for itself, and the DM should say so out loud afterwards — design doc §2.1 asks that the world be readable.

### A3. What is lost

> **G16 Wagonyard → DESTROYED**

Three carts, two stone-wagons, the hay, and — unless a PC acts in the first five rounds —

> **Both riding horses → DEAD**

Per WP01 §5.8: *"If killed, stolen, sold, or taken: they are gone."* A 4–5 session campaign will not replace them. **This is permanent.**

### A4. State changes

| System | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Infrastructure | Wagonyard **DESTROYED** | Direct |
| Critical stock | **Horses 2 → 0**, permanently | WP01 §5.8 |
| Trade | **No immediate change** | Daggerford's carts still come. Greybank has lost the ability to *send*, not to receive |
| §15 projects | All haulage times **+50%** | §7, G16 |
| Greybank Reputation | −1 **if the party is blamed**; 0 if not | Attitude tracks belief, not fact |

Note what did **not** happen: Trade did not fall, Food did not fall, Unity did not fall. WP01 §16 caps cascades at two links, and WP01 Example C's guidance applies — *"do not instantly reduce Food, Unity, Defense; future triggers may do so."*

### A5. The second-order consequence, arriving later

Gedd Plank (H3) has no carts. The next Daggerford run does not happen. **Two days later**, if the party has not solved it, the quarry has 2 Stone Loads sitting on the bench with no way to move them, and Kael Durrow raises it at moot. *That* is where Trade moves — as a new direct situation, not as an automatic cascade.

### A6. If the party is suspected

WP01 §7 and §8 apply. §16.2 says the Street at Deep Night yields **weak** evidence, *unless Nissa Crake saw something* — and she is the one person awake. Whether she did is a decision the DM makes once and does not revisit.

## 17.2 Example B — The party builds the palisade

**Situation.** After something gets into the fold, the party decides Greybank needs its ring closed. They go to the Reeve.

### B1. What it costs

§15.1: **40 gp · 8 Timber Loads · 20 volunteers · 6 days.**

The party has the coin. They do not have the timber or the people.

### B2. Timber

1 Timber Load per day from the coppice (§14 preamble), which is **across the river** at G20 and depends on Ib Sallow. Eight loads is **8 days of cutting**, which can run in parallel with nothing else Ib does.

If the party helps cut, or hires the six bunkhouse men off the quarry, it goes faster — at the cost of Stone Loads and Kael Durrow's goodwill.

### B3. The levy

Twenty volunteers requires the Reeve's agreement at **Unity 4**. Ondra Vell will agree — but the levy takes twenty adults off the fields and the quarry face for six days.

**Consequences the party must be told before they commit:**

- The quarry runs at half crew for six days: **3 Stone Loads not cut.**
- Kael Durrow objects at moot. WP04 owns how hard.
- The farm households support it; the quarry households do not. **No Unity change yet** — WP01 §3.4 says Unity moves after a major scene, not after every argument.

### B4. Six days pass

This is the part that matters. Per WP01 §10.3 and §11.2, **the world runs while the project does.** Over six days:

- Six dawns of replenishment checks (§11.3).
- The Daggerford cart arrives once at Trade 3.
- Every active threat advances on its own triggers.
- Any investigation advances.
- Faction clocks do what they were going to do.

> **The palisade is not free because the party paid gold. It is expensive because it costs six days of a world that does not wait.** WP08–WP10 should always have something the party would rather have been doing.

### B5. Completion

| System | Change |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure | Palisade **NONE/BREACHED → INTACT** |
| Defense | **2 → 3** |
| Muster | 20 min → **12 min**; the four ring gaps are now held (§12.3) |
| Greybank Reputation | **+1** — visible, collective, and it was the party's idea |
| Unity | No change |

### B6. What it does not do

The palisade **does not close the shoal, the riverside path, or the wharf** (§4.7, routes 5–7). It closes the four gaps. A party that believes they have sealed the village has misread the map, and the DM should let a scout walk in through route 5 to teach them — a Level 1 signal, not a punishment.

## 17.3 Example C — The medicine runs out

**Situation.** The bridge is **Damaged** after a fight on the deck. Two sessions in, the party has used their potions. Then Maud Elleth is badly hurt.

### C1. The chain, one link at a time

| Step | State |
|---|---|
| Bridge Damaged | Carts unloaded and carried. **Trade cannot exceed 4** — no change yet at Trade 3 |
| A caravan turns back rather than unload | **Trade 3 → 2.** This is an event, not an automatic consequence of the damage |
| Trade 2 | Imported restock takes twice as long; imported prices +25% (WP01 §3.2) |
| **Maud injured — Status: Injured, "cannot work the still, 6 days"** | Per WP01 §13.2 |

### C2. What is actually available

§11.1 and §11.3, checked honestly:

- **Potions of Healing:** the store's one is bought or gone. The shrine's one exists, is **not for sale**, and needs Ondra Vell's consent. That consent is a **scene**, not a purchase.
- **Healer's kits:** 2 existed. Local production was **1 per 3 days from Maud** — and Maud cannot work. **Production is zero.**
- **Antitoxin:** 1 existed. No production.
- **Import route:** Trade 2, twice as long, and potions were never routine stock anyway (§11.3).

> **Greybank's medical supply is now whatever is in the party's packs, plus one potion behind the Reeve's consent.** No roll produced this. Four ordinary decisions did.

### C3. The party's actual options

All of these work. None of them is free.

1. **Ask the Reeve for the shrine potion.** Greybank Rep ≥ +1 makes this a conversation; below 0 it makes it a refusal. Taking it anyway is theft from a shrine — §14.5 *Desecrated*, **Unity −1, Rep −1**.
2. **Repair the bridge.** §14.1: 4 Timber, 6 workers, 2 days, 20 gp. Restores Trade's ceiling, not Trade itself — the caravan has to choose to come back.
3. **Build the apothecary anyway.** §15.5 requires Maud *alive*, not healthy. Five days of work she can direct from a chair. When she recovers, kits come at **1 per 2 days** and survive any future Trade collapse.
4. **Go to Daggerford.** Two days each way. WP05 owns the road; the Daggerford Commercial Clock (WP01 §6.4) advances if they make contact.
5. **Treat her themselves.** A PC with a healer's kit or medicine proficiency shortens "6 days" — and earns something no purchase would.
6. **Accept it and be careful.** Legitimate. The campaign is designed to make this a real choice.

### C4. The lesson the table learns

This is design doc §30's target reaction. Nobody was punished. A bridge got damaged in a fight the party chose, a caravan made a commercial decision, and a healer got hurt. The consequence is that **the next dungeon delve happens without a safety net**, and the players can see exactly which of their own decisions built that.

## 17.4 Example D — Greybank Reputation −2

**Situation.** The party has done something the village has collectively decided against them. Rep is **−2**: *services refused* (WP01 §4.2, §9.4).

### D1. What changes at the counter

Per §9.4:

- Sember will not sell. The store is open; it is open **to other people**.
- Ordga will not take their work. The forge is lit; there is a queue and they are not in it.
- Rosal will not let rooms. **No lodging** — and no long rest inside the ring unless a household takes them, which at −2 none will.
- Gedd will not hire the horses.
- Maud will treat someone dying, because she is a healer and that is a WP03 matter, but she will not sell.

### D2. What does not change

**The stock still exists.** §11.1 is unaltered. The potion is still on Sember's shelf; the shield is still in the militia shed; the horses are still in the yard.

> This is the distinction §9.4 exists to enforce. Reputation changes **access**, not **inventory**. The party can see exactly what they cannot have, which is far more useful to the campaign than the stock quietly vanishing.

### D3. What the party can actually do

| Option | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Buy through a third party** | The six bunkhouse men (G15) are Daggerford's, not Greybank's, and are not bound by village feeling. They will buy on commission at a markup. **This is the intended workaround and it should work.** |
| **Buy in Daggerford** | Four days round trip. Advances the Daggerford clock |
| **Steal** | Theft in a village of 143 with §16.2's witness table. Weak evidence at Deep Night; Strong at any working watch |
| **Repair the relationship** | The only route back. WP01 §4 governs; a public, costly, visible act is what moves it |
| **Leave** | Legitimate. The campaign has a regional map |

### D4. The line the DM must hold

−2 is **not** −3. Per WP01 Example A's reasoning, the village has decided not to deal with the party; it has **not** decided to kill them. Nobody attacks. The bell does not ring. §12.6 mobilization is a **−3** event.

The correct texture of −2 is **quiet**: conversations stopping, a door closing, the taproom finding it has no rooms. Not a mob.

# 18. Edge Cases and Adjudication Guidance

## 18.1 The party splits across the village

Common, because Greybank has several simultaneously interesting places. Use §4.5: anywhere in the core is **under 30 rounds** from anywhere else at a run; the quarry is **70**. Resolve the two locations in alternating scenes, and let the distance table decide honestly whether the other half arrives. Per WP01 §21.6, do not secretly reunite them.

## 18.2 The party wipes

Design doc §33: a wipe means new characters and a modest time skip. Greybank's part:

- **The village finds the bodies** if they died within a mile, and buries them at **G19**. The players will walk past their own graves.
- Whatever they were carrying is **in the ground with them, or in the moot chest**, depending on who found them. Both are recoverable and both are socially expensive.
- Every project under way **stops** at whatever stage it reached. A half-built palisade stays half-built.
- Every clock advanced during the skip. Greybank is **worse** and the new party inherits it.
- **Replacement characters can come from Greybank itself** — H22 is empty (§8.2), and the roster has eighteen names WP03 will flesh out. A dead PC's replacement being the sibling of an NPC they failed is exactly the campaign this is.

## 18.3 The party ignores the village and camps outside

Entirely legitimate. Consequences:

- They can long rest without the taproom. **Time still costs 2 watches** (WP01 §11).
- They cannot restock, repair metal, or be treated.
- **Greybank Reputation stays at 0 and stops being a resource.** Nothing in §9.4's positive tiers ever opens.
- Villagers notice. Per WP01 §4.6, individual NPCs form opinions about people who avoid them, and none of those opinions are good.

Do not punish this. Do let it mean that when they need the shrine potion at Rep 0, the answer is no.

## 18.4 The party buys everything on day one

Let them. §11.1 is small, the coin ceiling is real (§10.5), and stripping Greybank of its two potions, one antitoxin and one shield **is a state change with consequences** — the village has no reserve the next time something happens to it. Note it in the dashboard's critical-stock block and let the militia discover the shield is gone at the worst possible moment.

## 18.5 The party intimidates or extorts

Works once, on one person, and then §16.2 applies: someone always hears. Extortion is a **Moderate** crime under WP01 §7.1 with a village of witnesses, and the durable consequence is Reputation, not an investigation clock. Two applications of force move Greybank Rep to **−2** and §17.4 becomes the party's life.

## 18.6 The party wants to build something not in §15

Good. Price it with the same five inputs: **coin, materials, labour, time, consent.** Use the §15.10 table as a calibration reference — a project comparable in scale to the palisade costs comparable resources. State the WP01 effect explicitly before they commit, per design doc §29's fairness contract. Do not invent a new tracked variable to represent it.

## 18.7 Villagers in a fight

Greybank's civilians are not combatants and should not be given initiative counts. During any fight inside the ring:

- Civilians **leave**, toward G7 and G9 (§12.5).
- They obstruct movement in the Cotts lanes for the first few rounds.
- They **witness** (§16.2). This is their mechanical function.
- If a PC action endangers or kills a civilian, that is a WP01 §7 crime with the whole §16.2 witness apparatus behind it.

## 18.8 The party disposes of evidence

§4.1 and §16.2: the Marrow is opaque and unwatched. **The river works.** Do not invent a reason it fails. Per WP01 §7.4, a successful concealment should genuinely reduce evidence quality — but the *absence* of a body is itself a fact the village will eventually notice, and WP01 §8.6 (murder without witnesses) governs from there.

## 18.9 The party wants to take over Greybank

The Reeve is chosen and removable by the moot (§3.2). A PC **can** be made Reeve. It requires Greybank Rep **+2**, a moot, and Unity ≥3, and it hands them the labour levy and the moot chest — and every decision, every complaint, and the responsibility for anything that goes wrong afterwards. This is a legitimate and interesting outcome, not a loophole. WP04 owns what the blocs do about it.

## 18.10 Season and the shoal

The shoal (§4.1, route 6) is **passable only at late-summer low water**. The campaign begins six weeks before harvest, which is late summer — so the shoal is open from session 1. If the DM extends the campaign past the autumn rains, the shoal closes, and so does one entry route, one escape route, and one of the two blind spots in §16.2. Say so when it changes.

## 18.11 What if the players never care about Greybank?

Then Greybank still changes, and they still live in the result. Food, Trade, Defense and Unity move on the events of §10 and §12 whether or not anyone is invested. Per design doc §22, unused material stays unused. The village's job is not to be loved; it is to be **the same place, differently**, every time they come back to it.

# 19. Final Canonical WP02 Rules

The compressed rule set. Later packages cite this section.

## Scale and site

Greybank is **143 people, 34 households**, on the north bank of the **Marrow**, two days from Daggerford, at the only bridge for a day's travel. It exists to cut **limestone**. It is **500 yards end to end**, overlooked from the north by the tower knoll and the quarry lip, and enclosed by an earth ring that is **breached in four places**.

## Movement

Anywhere inside the ring is **under 30 rounds** from anywhere else at a run. The quarry is **70 rounds** — roughly seven minutes — and help from the quarry never arrives during a fight in the village.

## Ways in

**Seven:** the bridge, the haul road, the north footpath, the east fields, the south-east riverside path, the shoal at low water, and the wharf by boat. The village watches the first two.

## Keyed locations

**G1–G22**, per §7. Every location with a gameplay function is keyed; nothing else is.

## Services

Fifteen services, per §9.1. **Five are load-bearing**, in order: **Maud Elleth** (medicine), **Ordga Brune** (metal), **Sember Dray** (imports), **Enna Fisk** (construction), **Gedd Plank** (the Daggerford run). Per WP01 §5.11 their deaths stop the service immediately, leave the stock in place, and produce no automatic replacement.

## The shrine

**No spellcaster.** Yorrin Fell is a lay-keeper who cannot cast. Divine magic must be brought, imported, or played.

## Critical stock

Placed per §11.1 across **five locations**: store, smithy, militia shed, herbwife, shrine. **Half of it is not purchasable** — the shrine potion, the shield, the militia ammunition, the chain shirt and the horses are social transactions, gated behind consent, reputation, or theft. Plus **6 charges of blasting powder** at the quarry, tracked as discrete stock.

## Replenishment

**Kits and arrows come back. Potions, armour and horses do not.** Healer's kits replenish **locally** at 1 per 3 days and are therefore **Trade-independent** — this is Greybank's single most important economic fact.

## Coin

**≈260 gp of liquid coin exists in the entire village.** Greybank cannot buy the party's loot. Selling requires Daggerford, which advances the Daggerford clock.

## Defence

**18 on the roll, 11 prompt at Defense 2, ~20 minutes to muster.** Nine of the eighteen are quarry crew: **Greybank cannot mobilize and quarry at the same time.** The bell does not reach the quarry, the bunkhouse, the fold, the charcoal camp, or the outlying farmsteads.

## Village hostility

At **−3**, run §12.6: the village as terrain, not as encounters. Archers on the Moot Hall roof, carts at the smithy pinch, the Green as killing ground, no rest inside the ring, a runner to Daggerford. **Six escape routes are telegraphed.** At **−2**, nothing attacks — services close, quietly.

## Fire

Thatch: taproom, mill, wagonyard, bunkhouse, the Cotts, byres, herbwife. **The store's slate roof is the Street's firebreak.** Spread is automatic only in the Cotts, the wagonyard, and the mill/granary pair. The two catastrophes are **Millside** (Mill destroyed, Food −2) and **the Cotts** (3+ lost in a night = Unity −1).

## Infrastructure

Per §14, with Greybank repair costs. **All build and repair times increase by half without Enna Fisk.** Stone: **1 Load/day** from the quarry. Timber: **1 Load/day** from the coppice. Volunteers: **20 at Unity 4**, halved at Unity 2, none at Unity 0.

Specific functional states used beyond WP01's five: **Watchtower — manned / unmanned at night**; **Shrine — Desecrated**; **Farms — Threatened / Partially abandoned / Abandoned** (WP01 §14.9's own vocabulary); **Taproom — Intact / Closed** (a WP02 addition, §14.11).

## Upgrades

Nine projects, §15, each priced in **coin, materials, labour, time, and consent**. Consent is the expensive one. **No project is a correct purchase** — every one costs the village something it needs.

## The village day

Six watches, §16. **Deep Night has 2–3 people awake.** The two blind spots are **the fold at night** and **the river at any time**. Adjudicate WP01 §7.4 concealment against §16.2, not against general impressions.

## Deferred, unchanged

Personalities and biographies → **WP03**. Bloc politics → **WP04**. Everything past the boundary stones, including bridge detour time → **WP05**. The quarry aperture's interior → **WP06**. Stat blocks → **WP07**.
