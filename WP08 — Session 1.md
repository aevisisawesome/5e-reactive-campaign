# The Broken March — WP08: Session 1

**Status:** Canonical campaign session document
**Consumes:** `Campaign design doc.md` (§4, §6, §16, §22, §26, §27, §29); WP01 §10, §11, §12, §18, §19; WP02 §3.3, §4, §7, §11, §12, §15, §16; WP03 §5, §6, §7, §8; WP04 §5.3, §6.3, §7.3, §12.2; WP05 §6, §8, §9; WP06 §7, §17.1; WP07 §5, §6.5, §9, §12.1, §13.1
**Feeds:** WP09–WP12, and the handout and dashboard packages WP14 and WP16
**Scope:** The first session — the opening, the people who must land, three simultaneous problems, the quarry incident, the rumour card set, and the starting and expected end dashboard states.

This document stages the world WP02–WP07 built into one evening of play. It fixes what is **already true when the party arrives**, what fires **on a deadline whether or not they act**, and what each of the three live problems becomes if ignored. It does **not** route the party, require any rumour to be taken, or make the Illefarn site mandatory — the site is made available and attractive, and going down is a choice with a written cost.

| Day | Watch | What is true |

| **1** | Afternoon | **The party arrives.** Greybank is working. Nothing is wrong yet |

**A party that goes down at the Pale in the Afternoon of Day 2 is underground for one watch and comes up to the fold's news.** That is WP06 §17.1's promise, kept.

## 3.5 Levelling

**Level 3 → 4, unconditionally, at the end of session 1.** Milestone, not experience points.

Announce it as a fact at the start of play if the players ask. It is not a reward for engagement and it must not be presented as one — **WP07 §17.3 makes ignoring content legitimate, and design doc §22 forbids fake choices.** A party that spent the session drinking in the Sundered Cart and talking to Halvard Vell levels up with everyone else.

# 4. The Opening

## 4.1 Why the party is here **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Pick one. None of them is a patron and none of them gives the party an instruction.**

| # | Hook | What it gives | What it does not give |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Passing through, southbound.** Greybank is the only bridge for a day's travel; the party is crossing and needs a bed | Total freedom, and a reason to already be at G1 and G2 | Nothing. The party may leave, and the campaign lets them |
| **2** | **Hired by Sember Dray, by letter, to see why the cart is late** | A reason to talk to Gedd Plank in the first hour, and 15 gp | Any authority. **Sember cannot pay more and will say so** |
| **3** | **One PC is from here** — the empty cott H22 is theirs, or their family's | Free village knowledge, an existing attitude, and a household | Popularity. WP02 §8 puts them in a specific cott with specific neighbours |
| **4** | **Following the stone.** Somebody in Daggerford mentioned Greybank's pale limestone is cut wrong | A reason to go to the Pale on Day 1, and a connection to Malk that costs nothing | Any idea what that means |

**Recommended: hook 1 or 3.** Hook 2 is the safest for a group that wants to be told why they are somewhere, and its ceiling is deliberately low — 15 gp against a village that holds 260.

> **Do not combine a hook with a deadline.** If the party has somewhere to be, session 1 has failed before it starts.

## 4.2 The arrival — Day 1, Afternoon

**Approach from the south, over the bridge.** Every hook above arrives this way except hook 3, and hook 3 arrives this way too because there is no other cart route.

**Run it in this order. It takes ten minutes and establishes six things.**

| Beat | What they see | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The road** | Eight miles of ordinary road, then a defile with banked sides that a carter would go quiet through — **the Cut (Z8)** | Z8 exists. Print it on the player map |
| **2. The toll-shed (G2)** | A stone hut on the south bank. **Denk Ottersby, damp, wants 2 cp a wheel and does not want conversation.** He writes them in the book | The crossing book exists and someone writes in it. WP01 §8 has teeth |
| **3. The bridge (G1)** | Ninety feet, eleven wide, **no parapet on the downstream side.** Nine feet of opaque water below | The bridge is the village's life, and it is not in good repair |
| **4. The Street** | Stone and thatch, one slate roof among them, water butts every third building. **Twenty or thirty people who all look up** | 143 people, and the party is now a known quantity |
| **5. The Green (G10)** | Sixty yards of open turf, a well, a bell post. **Children.** Old Tarn on a bench, watching | The bell. And the fact that everyone can see everyone |
| **6. The Pale** | Look north and up: a stepped white cut in the hillside, a derrick, and **the sound of work stopping early** | The quarry, and that something is already slightly wrong |

**Beat 6 is the only one that is not neutral.** The crew comes down the haul road roughly a watch early, and they do not talk on the way.

## 4.3 The first ten minutes at the table

- **Do not open with a problem.** Open with a place that works.
- **Name three villagers in the first five minutes** and let the party ignore all three. Suggested: Denk Ottersby (unavoidable), Pell (she will find them), and Rosal Tunn (they need a bed).
- **Someone asks what they are here for**, and the answer goes round the village by Evening. WP02 §16.2: the Street in the Afternoon is *certain* witnesses.
- **Do not have anyone ask for help yet.** Bessa Ruddock arrives at Dawn on Day 2, and she is worth waiting for.

## 4.4 What the village does about them

Five armed strangers in a village of 143 is an event. **Greybank Reaction is 1 — routine governance — so the response is curiosity, not suspicion.**

| Who | Reaction, Day 1 |
|---|---|
| **Pell** | Attaches herself within a watch. Asks what everything is. **Will answer any question she can and half she cannot** |
| **Rosal Tunn** | Four pallets at 5 cp. Feeds them. Talks at them while working. **The village's news, a day late** |
| **Ondra Vell** | Sends word she would like to know who they are, and does not send for them. She is drafting a letter |
| **Berrick Hone** | Comes to look at them, over-explains the militia, and **tells them about the ways in unprompted if asked anything at all** |
| **Old Tarn** | Watches from the bench. Says something discouraging. **He is the isolationists' temperament with nothing behind it** |
| **Nissa Crake** | Not seen in daylight. She sleeps in the afternoon |
| **Kael Durrow** | **Not in the village.** He is at the face and will be until after dark |

> **Nobody is hostile. Nobody is suspicious. Greybank has no reason to be either yet, and the campaign has three sessions in which to change that.**

# 5. The People Who Must Land

WP03 owns who these people are. **WP08 owns which ones the session cannot end without, and the specific moment each becomes memorable.**

## 5.1 The five who must land

**If the session ends and the players cannot describe these five, run the session again.**

---

### Bessa Ruddock — the warning with a face on it

**Lands at: Dawn, Day 2, on the Green, with a dead ewe on a hurdle.**

She does not ask for help. She reports, the way she talks to dogs — short, clear, not unkind — and she has a count.

> *"Four in eleven days. That's the fourth. Something went over the wall, not through it, and it took her uphill. I'd like somebody to come and look before it's a story instead of a warning."*

**What she puts in front of them, physically:** the ewe, and **the slate** (§10, card R1; WP07 §12.1) with her tally on it in chalk — one gone, three quiet nights, one gone.

**Why she must land:** she is design doc §6's Level 2 with a person attached, she is the door to the Red-Tusk thread, and she is 260 yards outside the ring at night with two dogs and a lantern.

**If the party ignores her:** she says *"right"* and goes back to the fold. **She does not ask twice.** §6.1 is what happens next.

---

### Rosal Tunn — the village, delivered

**Lands at: Evening, Day 1, the Sundered Cart.** §11 is the scene.

She talks *at* them while working and never stops. Every rumour in §10 arrives inside an unrelated anecdote about someone's roof.

**Why she must land:** she is the rumour-delivery route, and the cheapest genuine reputation gain in the campaign is buying someone else's meal in front of her.

**Her line for tonight**, and she says it to whoever will listen:

> *"Six of the quarry men have gone quiet. Not one — six. That's never once meant nothing."*

---

### Maud Elleth — the medicine economy, made visible

**Lands at: Afternoon, Day 2**, in the open, over Dov Marrek. §7.4 is the scene.

She asks clinical questions in a flat order, says *"mm"* instead of yes, and **uses one of Greybank's two healer's kits in front of the party.** Then she says what it costs to replace it and how long that takes.

**Why she must land:** WP01's entire resource model becomes real in ninety seconds. She is also the worst single death in the campaign and the players should have met her before that is true.

**Her ask, if they engage at all:** the apothecary. 110 gp, 2 Stone, 3 Timber, 5 days, and Ondra's agreement. **She has asked twice and will not ask a third time** — so she mentions it once, flatly, and drops it.

---

### Enna Fisk — the way in

**Lands at: any time she is spoken to.** She is Friendly from the first conversation, whistles, and is genuinely pleased that adventurers have arrived, because adventurers mean projects.

**Why she must land:** she quotes WP02 §15's catalogue from memory in plain speech, which is the intended discovery route for everything the party can build. And she is **unrecoverable** — the campaign's proof that death is permanent — so she must be visibly central before it matters.

**Her line, unprompted, pointing:**

> *"Forty gold and eight loads of timber and six days and that gap's shut. I've costed it three times. Ask her why it isn't done."*

---

### Kael Durrow — the door with a man standing in front of it

**Lands at: Evening, Day 1** at the Sundered Cart if the party is looking, or **Morning, Day 2** at the collapse if they are not.

**Wary from the first meeting.** Speaks in instructions. Long silences he does not fill.

**Why he must land:** he has been ten feet inside the aperture with a lantern and told nobody, and after §7 he cannot keep that up. He is bought with competence, not charm.

## 5.3 The waystone line **[CAMPAIGN dialogue on ADAPTED Illefarn masonry]**

**WP06 §18.3 requires that somebody say the old stones should be left alone, before anyone is holding a hammer.** This is that, and it is deliberately not a warning.

**Yorrin Fell, at the shrine, if anyone remarks on the foundation course** — which is visibly not villager work and which any PC who looks will notice:

> *"That's older than the village. Older than the wood we put on it. It holds the corner up and it has never asked anything of anybody, so we leave it be."*

**He is untroubled by it, and that is the point.** A man calmly telling you a thing is ancient and load-bearing lands harder than a man telling you not to touch it.

**Two other voices, and they disagree:**

| Who | Says |
|---|---|
| **Halvard Vell** | That the tower's lower courses were here before the village, that his grandfather said the same, and **that nobody has ever needed to know why** |
| **Ordga Brune** | The opposite. **"No chisel makes that cut."** She wants it looked at. She is the campaign's best free lead into WP06 and she is arguing for exactly the thing Yorrin is arguing against |

> **Three of the six waystones are inside Greybank** — the tower's base course (G13), the shrine's foundation (G9), and the north wall of the burial ground (G19). A party that walks the village and looks at stone can find all three in one afternoon, in session 1, with no check and no magic. **Destroying one is available to a level 3 party with a hammer and it is the campaign's most expensive irreversible act** (WP06 §13).

# 6. The Three Problems

**Three, simultaneous, none mandatory, each with a written what-happens-if-ignored across all four sessions.**

Each is a **door** rather than a quest: the fold opens the Red-Tusk thread, the Pale opens Illefarn, the far bank opens the Laughing Hollow. **The party may take none of them and the campaign still runs** (WP04 §12.2).

## 6.1 P1 — The Fold · Z4 / G18

**Type C + E · WP07 §12.1 is the full encounter. This is its session-1 staging.**

### What is true when the party arrives

**Four sheep gone in eleven days.** The fold is 260 yards from the Green, outside the earth ring, unlit, and **26 rounds away at a run** — longer at night with no light. At Defense 2 the militia takes twenty minutes to muster. **Anything that happens at the fold is finished before the village arrives.**

### The two hands, staged

WP07 §12.1 fixes that two things are taking from the fold. **WP08 stages them so the diagnosis is available in session 1 and not yet forced.**

| | **The Moorgaunt** | **Red-Tusk scouts** |

# 7. The Quarry Incident **[CAMPAIGN, on ADAPTED Illefarn masonry]**

**Morning watch, Day 2. It fires whether or not the party is anywhere near it.**

## 7.1 What happens

The lowest bench at the Pale has been cut steadily north for two years. **This morning it gives way** — about fifteen feet of face, into a void behind it that nobody knew was there, because the wall the quarry has been cutting toward is two thousand years old and hollow on the far side.

**Nothing woke up.** A quarry cut into a wall until the wall failed. That is the whole of it, and the DM should say so if asked (§1.3, decision 2).

**Dov Marrek is under it** — twenty-six, on the militia roll, and one of the fourteen. He is pinned from the thigh down under about four hundredweight of broken limestone. **He is conscious, and he is screaming, and the crew can hear him.**

## 7.2 The next twenty minutes, without the party

**This is the sequence if the party is in the village and never comes. Run it as a clock, out loud, and let the party hear it happen.**

| Elapsed | What happens |
|---:|---|
| 0 | The face goes. Dust reaches the village in about a minute. **Nobody in Greybank knows what it was** |
| +1 min | Durrow has the crew on the stone. Ten men lifting, badly, with bars |
| +2 min | **A man starts running down the haul road.** Seven minutes at a run (WP02 §4.5) |
| +9 min | He reaches the Green. **He does not ring the bell** — nobody rings the bell for an accident, and it would not have reached the Pale anyway |
| +12 min | Somebody goes for Maud Elleth at G22 |
| +18 min | **Maud walks up the haul road.** She is not young and she does not run |
| +25 min | She reaches the face. Dov has been under the stone for twenty-five minutes |

> **This is the bell lesson, and it costs nothing to teach.** The bell does not reach the quarry. The horn is in the militia shed and the shed is locked and Berrick has the key. **Twenty-five minutes is the fastest Greybank can put a healer in front of an injured man 700 yards away**, and the players should watch that number happen.

## 7.3 What the collapse exposes

Behind the fallen face: **worked stone.** A cut ramp descending at a shallow grade, wide enough for two carts abreast, and a lintel of jointless pale stone.

**It is the same aperture Durrow boarded over, and it is now four times the size and cannot be boarded again.**

| Fact | Consequence |
|---|---|
| The opening is now **fifteen feet wide** | Durrow cannot cover it, cannot hide it, and cannot keep the crew from seeing it |
| **The air moves** | It has been moving for two thousand years. Level 1 signalling, per WP06 §7 |
| The crew has seen it | **Sabel Quist walks off the site and does not come back that day.** He still cannot say why |
| Durrow's secret is over | **Secret S2 surfaces**, exactly as WP03 specifies: *"if expansion is approved and he has to give the order"* — or if the face opens it for him |
| The lowest bench is unsafe | **Stone Loads halve to 1 per 2 days** until it is shored. Enna Fisk can shore it: 2 days, 4 Timber Loads — **and there is no timber, because of §6.3** |

> **Three problems just became one problem, and the party did not do that. The village did, over two years, on purpose, for money.**

## 7.4 Dov Marrek, and the medicine economy

**This is where WP01's resource model stops being a table.**

Getting him out from under the stone takes ten men and levers, or four people and a good idea, or the derrick — **which is right there and which Durrow will not use because it is rated for dressed blocks and this is rubble.** A party that argues him into it, or rigs it themselves, gets him out in three minutes instead of twenty.

**Then:**

| Step | What it costs |
|---|---|
| Maud stabilises him | **One healer's kit, consumed, in front of the party.** There are two in Greybank |
| She says what that means | *"That's one. The other's mine and I'll want it. New one's three days if my garden holds and I'm not called away."* **WP02 §11.3, spoken aloud** |
| The leg | **This is the decision.** Out in under ten minutes: he keeps it. Out after twenty-five: he does not |
| Either way | **Dov Marrek is off the militia roll.** Prompt turnout at Defense 2 goes from 11 to 10 for the rest of the campaign |

**If a PC has healing magic**, it works normally — WP01 §22 does not nerf anything — and it is the first time Greybank has seen such a thing in living memory. **Greybank Reputation +1**, and Yorrin Fell will want to talk to them, because he has wanted an actual priest for thirty years.

**If a PC has none**, nothing is lost. The derrick and ten minutes do the same job.

> **Decision 12 is load-bearing here.** Dov does not die on a die roll. Whether he keeps the leg is decided by what the party does in the first ten minutes, and if they are not there, it is decided by twenty-five minutes of village logistics. **Both outcomes are fair and neither is a punishment.**

## 7.5 What Durrow does

He is Wary from the first meeting and this makes him worse before it makes him better.

| The party… | Durrow… |
|---|---|
| Helps get Dov out, competently | **Wary → Neutral.** He notices competence and nothing else |
| Helps and then asks about the opening | Neutral, and **he does not answer.** Long silence he does not fill |
| Goes to the opening while he is dealing with Dov | **Wary → Hostile fast.** He will physically stand in the way once, and he is a strong man with fourteen men behind him |
| Asks him a direct question at the face, later, alone | **Secret S2.** *"I went in ten feet. With a lantern. Came out and boarded it tighter and told nobody. The air moved."* |
| Does nothing at all | He deals with it. He was always going to |

**By the Afternoon watch, Durrow has to report it to the Reeve**, because fifteen feet of face is gone and the shipment will be short. **That is the moot argument of session 2**, and it arrives early.

## 7.6 The aperture, from the Afternoon of Day 2

**It is open, it is unguarded, and it is a quarter-watch from the Green.**

- Durrow posts nobody. He has fourteen men, one of them is hurt, and none of them will go near the lowest bench.
- **No villager will enter the site, ever, for any inducement** (WP02, WP03 §15, WP04 §11). The party will not find a guide and should be told so plainly if they ask.
- **The party can walk in.** Nothing stops them and nothing invites them.

> **Do not have anyone suggest they go in.** Design doc §22: the opening must be *available and attractive*, not assigned. Ordga Brune's *"no chisel makes that cut"* is the strongest pull in the village and it is a statement about masonry, not a quest.

|---|---|
| **At the fold** | §8.2. A fair fight, or a conversation, or a night spent watching three goblins take a hen and leave |
| **Underground at the Pale** | They come up to it. **The fold lost a hen, a coil of rope, and a hurdle, and Bessa's dogs were out all night** |
| **In the village** | Nissa Crake hears something and cannot get anyone to care, again. They find out at Dawn |
| **On the far bank** | They find out at Dawn, and they were two watches away and across a river |

## 8.2 If the party is there

**Three Red-Tusk Scouts** (WP07 §6.2). Deep dark, no moon worth the name, and the fold is unlit.

**Their objective is food, and a count.** They come over the wall where the stones are already loose, take a hen from the byre and a coil of rope from the hurdle stack, and **look at how many people come running.** That is the whole of the stage-1 probe.

| They will | They will not |
|---|---|
| Withdraw the moment they are seen in force | Kill a human if they can avoid it |
| Shoot if shot at | Enter the cott |
| **Drop everything and run if a way out is open** | Take a child |
| Use Nimble Escape and split up | **Stay** |

**This is a genuinely fair fight for five level 3 PCs and it is not why it is here.** WP04 §6.2 is binding: *probes are designed to be survivable by both sides.* A DM who kills a PC with this has broken canon.

## 8.3 The four outcomes, and what each costs

| Outcome | State change |
|---|---|
| **Let them take the hen and go** | **Red-Tusk Rep −1 → 0.** Nothing else. They report that the fold is watched now, and Ghesh notes it |
| **Drive them off without killing** | Nothing. **They will be back**, and next time they come at the farms instead |
| **Talk to them** | Two of the three speak no Common. The third speaks about six words of it and one of them is *"hungry."* **Red-Tusk Rep +1**, and Orbal hears about it |
| **Kill one or more** | **Red-Tusk Rep −2. War +1** in WP04 §8.3's pressure count. WP07 §4.5: **the survivor reports, and Ghesh counts** |
| **Capture one** | **Compact +1**, and the party has the campaign's fastest route to Orbal in a store room with a bar on the outside of the door (WP07 §4.6) |

> **None of these is the wrong answer and the DM must not signal one.** The party has been given no information about who these are. Killing three goblins raiding a sheepfold at night is an entirely reasonable act, and the campaign will charge them for it in session 3 without ever telling them they did wrong.

## 8.4 If the party never goes to the fold at all

**Also fine, and it must cost something specific rather than nothing.**

At Dawn on Day 3 — which is after the session ends — Bessa reports again. Nobody has come. **She stops reporting.** From session 2 she deals with it herself, at night, alone, and WP03's death consequences become live.

# 9. Illefarn Stage I, in One Visit

**WP06 §17.1's promise: Stage I is enterable and completable in one visit, at level 3, and it costs a watch.** WP07 §13.1 fixes that there is **no creature in it.**

## 9.1 The cost

| | |
|---|---|
| **Travel** | ¼ watch each way from the Green, up the haul road |
| **Underground** | **One watch** to see S1–S4 properly, including a full look at the Watch-Glass |
| **Total** | **1½ watches.** The Afternoon of Day 2, and they come up in the Evening |
| **What it costs them** | **The Evening watch in the village.** The moot argument about the collapse happens without them, and the fold is probed that night |

**Say the cost before they commit**, in ordinary words: *"That's most of the afternoon and into the evening. You'll not be in the village for whatever's said about the quarry."*

## 9.2 What is down there

WP06 §7 is the sector detail. **This is what a level 3 party actually experiences in one hour of table time.**

| Sector | What they find | Danger |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — The Aperture and Ramp** | Forty feet of cut ramp at a shallow grade. **Durrow's lantern-soot on the wall at the ten-foot mark, and his bootprints turning round.** The ramp is clean because something swept it for an age and stopped | **None.** The ramp cannot be closed from inside. **A forty-foot fall exists if somebody is careless in the dark** |
| **S2 — The Muster Floor** | Eighty feet by forty. Stone benches, pegs cut above them, **fifteen sets of pegs, all empty.** **One warden's kit under a bench**, complete, with a **resonance key** in it. Cut names run the far wall | None. **Level 2 signalling: the roster's last third is cut in a different, hurried hand** |
| **S3 — The Anchor Room** | A drum of jointless stone, floor to ceiling, with a worn recess at chest height. **It is the same stone as the tower** — and a party that has looked at G13 will say so out loud | None, **unless forced** — §9.4 |
| **S4 — The Watch Chamber** | Locked. A stone table and a basin of dark, still liquid. **The Watch-Glass** | None. **Level 3 signalling, and it is the most important object in the campaign** |

## 9.3 Getting into S4 — the first permanent choice

**Two routes, and one of them is irreversible** (WP06 §5.1):

| Route | Cost |
|---|---|
| **The resonance key from the warden's kit in S2** | Free. It is twenty feet away and under a bench |
| **Prise the warped shutter** | **S4 → Breached, permanently.** The Watch Chamber can never be secured again, and anything in the site can reach the Watch-Glass |

**A party that searches S2 before trying S4 finds the key.** A party in a hurry breaks a two-thousand-year-old door in session 1 and will not learn what that cost until session 3.

> **Do not warn them.** Design doc §29's obligation is telegraphing danger, not preventing bad choices. The kit is visible to anyone who searches the room, and searching a room is ¼ watch.

## 9.4 The anchor, if they force it

**S3's recess warms under the hand before anyone swings.** That is the warning, it is tactile, and it precedes the blow.

If they force it anyway: **2d6 force damage to every creature in the room, and Illefarn Instability 1 → 2** (WP07 §13.1, confirmed canonical).

**At Instability 2** the two waystones outside the village — W4 on Stonecrop Rise and W5 at Wend's Barrow — become **warm to the touch and audible at night** (WP06 §12). Nobody in Greybank will notice for a tenday. **The party did that, in session 1, in about four seconds.**

## 9.5 The Watch-Glass

**A basin of dark, still liquid on a stone table. It has never stopped working.**

Looking into it shows **six rings of light arranged in an arc**, three of them dark, and **the dark ones face north-west.**

That is all. There is no text, no voice, and no explanation. **It is a level 3 party being handed the shape of the entire campaign, for free, in session 1** — and almost every group will write it down and not understand it until session 3.

| What it is | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Portable.** It comes out of the table | The party can carry it |
| **It shows where the ward line runs** | Which is the Sentinel's first solution (WP07 §11.5), handed over before the Sentinel exists as a problem |
| **It moves no clock and nobody notices** | WP06 §14 — the only system in the site with no faction consequence |
| **Malk will pay more for it than for anything else here** | **Selling it blinds the party permanently.** WP13 owns the price |

> **Give them the image and refuse to explain it.** Six rings. Three dark. Facing north-west. If a player draws it on a napkin, that napkin is the campaign.

## 9.6 What happens above ground while they are down there

**The watch they spend underground is a watch they spend underground.**

| If they go down in the Afternoon of Day 2 | They miss |
|---|---|
| Durrow reporting the collapse to Ondra Vell | The first shape of the expansion argument |
| Maud walking back down the haul road | The chance to ask her about the apothecary while she is talking |
| The Evening at the Sundered Cart, if they linger | Half the rumour cards |
| **The Night watch** | **The fold is probed** (§8) |

**None of this is a punishment and none of it is retroactive.** It is a watch, spent.

## 9.7 What they must be told before they can be blamed

By the time a party could plausibly be holding a hammer near a waystone, the campaign owes them **all three of these**, and session 1 delivers them:

1. **Yorrin Fell**: that stone is older than the village and holds the corner up (§5.3).
2. **The Watch-Glass**: six rings, three dark (§9.5).
3. **The tower, the shrine and the burial-ground wall** are visibly the same stone as the Anchor Room, and anyone who looks can see it.

**After that, what they do with a hammer is theirs.**

## 11.3 Serina Malk

# 14. Edge Cases and Adjudication Guidance

## 14.1 The party splits

Greybank is 500 yards end to end and **anywhere in the ring is under 30 rounds from anywhere else** (WP02 §4.5). A party split inside the village is not really split — run it in parallel and let them talk.

**The split that matters is the Pale.** The quarry is **70 rounds — seven minutes — from the Green**, and help from the quarry never arrives during anything happening in the village, and vice versa. A party split between the two on Day 2 Morning has one half at the collapse and one half hearing about it nine minutes later.

**A party split across the river** is split for at least a watch. The skiff seats four.

## 14.2 The party wipes

**Extremely unlikely in session 1 and WP08 contains no encounter that should do it.** The scout probe is fair, the collapse is not an enemy, and Stage I has no creature.

If it somehow happens: WP01 §21.7 and design doc §33 — new characters, a setback, some skipped time. **WP02's empty cott H22 exists for this**, and WP03 permits a replacement drawn from the roster.

## 14.3 The party ignores all three problems

**Legitimate.** Run WP04 §12.2's baseline: **Greybank Reaction → 2**, and everything else where it started.

The session is then about Greybank itself — Rosal's room, Halvard by the fire, Enna costing the ring, the moot argument about the collapse. **That is a good session** and the players will have met a village they now care about, which is worth more to sessions 2–4 than a solved problem.

**Do not escalate to force engagement.** Do not have Bessa come back a third time. Do not have someone die to make a point.

## 14.4 A PC is at the aperture when the bench collapses

**Possible if they went down on Day 1 and stayed** (§12.3).

- **Inside S1 or deeper: they are safe.** The collapse is at the face, on the outside. What they experience is a very large noise and dust down the ramp.
- **On the lowest bench, at the boards: 4d6 bludgeoning, DC 15 Dexterity save for half**, and they are the ones under the stone instead of Dov Marrek. Dov, in that case, is fine.
- **The collapse still opens the aperture.** It does not seal them in — WP06 §7 is explicit that the ramp cannot be closed from inside and Stage I is not a trap.

## 14.5 The party tries to buy or take the blasting powder

**Six charges, at the quarry, and Kael Durrow is the only responsible keeper** (WP02 §11.1, §11.2).

| They | He |
|---|---|
| Ask, at Wary | **No.** Not a negotiation |
| Ask, at Neutral or better, with a reason | Asks what for, and does not like the answer, and **still probably no** |
| Take it | **Serious crime under WP01 §7.1**, and the crew knows exactly how many there were |

**After §7, the powder store is intact and the man who watches it is distracted.** WP07 §17.7 owns what happens if they use any.

## 14.6 The party wants to buy the shrine potion

**It is not for sale.** It is the village's emergency reserve, in Yorrin Fell's strongbox, and obtaining it requires **the Reeve's consent or theft** (WP02 §11.1).

**On Day 2, after the collapse, there is a live argument about whether Dov Marrek is an emergency.** Yorrin thinks maybe. Ondra thinks the harvest is six weeks out and there may be worse coming. **Let the party into that argument** — it is the best scene in session 1 and nobody has to fight anybody.

**If the party gives Dov a potion of their own:** Yorrin Fell's attitude +1, Ondra Vell's attitude +1, **Greybank Reputation +1**, and Maud says *"mm"*, which from her is a great deal.

## 14.7 A player asks what the Watch-Glass means

**Do not tell them.** Describe it again, precisely, and let them theorise.

Six rings of light in an arc. Three dark. **The dark ones face north-west.** It is a basin of dark liquid on a stone table in a room nobody has entered for two thousand years, and it still works.

**If they ask an NPC:** nobody in Greybank knows. **Ordga Brune will look at it for a long time and say the stone is the same.** That is the most any villager can give them.

## 14.8 The party kills a villager

In session 1 this would be extraordinary, and WP01 §7 and §8 handle it without WP08's help.

**Two things WP08 adds:**

- **Nissa Crake is awake at Night and Deep Night** (WP02 §16.1). She is Greybank's only reliable night witness and she has been trying to get people to listen to her for a tenday.
- **The river is the disposal route** and clever players will find it. WP02 §16.2: the Marrow is opaque and unwatched, **evidence quality None.** Adjudicate WP01 §7.4 against that table and not against a general sense of how careful they were.

## 14.9 The party wants to leave Greybank entirely

**Let them.** The map is eighteen zones and WP05 owns all of it.

- **Nothing in session 1 requires them to be in Greybank**, and WP04 §12.2 runs perfectly well without them.
- The three deadlines in §12.1 fire anyway. **They come back to a village where the face has come down, a man is maimed, and the fold has lost a hen.**
- **Do not relocate any of it to wherever they went.** Design doc §22 and WP07 §17.3 both forbid it.

## 14.10 The players want to start a project on Day 2

**Enthusiastic, and worth encouraging, and slower than they think.**

| Project | The real blocker in session 1 |
|---|---|
| **Palisade** (40 gp, 8 Timber, 6 days) | **There is no timber** (R5), and six days is four sessions of game time |
| **Militia training** (4 days, a trainer) | **A PC can be the trainer.** This is the fastest Defense in the campaign and it still takes four days |
| **Apothecary** (110 gp, 5 days) | The village holds **260 gp total.** The party probably cannot afford it in session 1 either |
| **Shoring the lowest bench** (2 days, 4 Timber) | Timber again |

> **Say the number of days out loud every time.** WP02 §17.2's principle is that six days is the real price, and session 1 is the session where the players learn that the campaign's clock is not generous.

## 14.11 A PC destroys a waystone in session 1

**It is available, it is a hammer, and three of the six are inside the village.**

- **That span cannot be re-anchored, ever** (WP06 §13).
- **Destroying a second one puts the good ending permanently out of reach.**
- **§9.7 lists the three things the campaign owes them first**, and session 1 delivers all three. **After that, do not stop them and do not warn them again.**
- **The village reaction is immediate and disproportionate**: the tower base, the shrine foundation and the burial-ground wall are load-bearing masonry and one of them is a shrine. **Greybank Rep −1, Unity −1, and Yorrin Fell will not forgive it.**

# 15. Final Canonical WP08 Rules

The compressed rule set. Later packages cite this section, and it is readable alone.

## The starting state

**WP02 §3.3's block, verbatim, at Day 1 Afternoon**, with all five clocks at **1** and reputation at **Greybank 0 / Hollow 0 / Red-Tusk −1 / Daggerford 0**. §3.3 is the page to copy into the dashboard.

## The three problems

**The fold (Z4), the Pale (Z2), the far bank (Z5).** One door each into the Red-Tusk, Illefarn and Laughing Hollow threads. **None is mandatory and each has a written decay through session 4** (§6). Daggerford enters through **Malk at the taproom** and is an offer, not a problem.

## The three deadlines

**Day 2 Morning: the bench collapses. Day 2 Night: the fold is probed. Day 2 Dawn: Bessa brings the ewe in.** All three fire whether or not the party is present. **Their presence changes the scene, never the event.**

## The quarry incident

**A quarry cut into a two-thousand-year-old wall until the wall failed. Nothing woke up.** Dov Marrek is pinned; **out in under ten minutes he keeps the leg, after twenty-five he does not**; he is off the militia roll either way, taking prompt turnout at Defense 2 from **11 to 10** permanently. **One healer's kit is consumed in front of the party, and there are two.**

## The bell lesson

**Twenty-five minutes is the fastest Greybank can put a healer in front of an injured man 700 yards away.** The bell does not reach the quarry, the fold, the bunkhouse, the charcoal camp or the outlying farms, and session 1 demonstrates it rather than stating it.

## The aperture

**Opened by the collapse, fifteen feet wide, unguarded from the Afternoon of Day 2.** Nobody suggests the party go in. **No villager will enter it, ever.** Entering and looking changes no clock; **opening it advances the Laughing Hollow to stage 2** (WP04 §5.3's own trigger).

## Stage I

**One and a half watches, no creature, and the Watch-Glass for free.** The S2 warden's kit holds the key to S4; **prising the shutter instead breaches S4 permanently.** Forcing the S3 anchor is **2d6 to the room and Instability +1.**

## The waystone obligation

**Yorrin Fell says the old stone is older than the village and holds the corner up, and is untroubled by it.** Halvard Vell agrees from memory; **Ordga Brune says the opposite and wants it looked at.** With those three, plus the Watch-Glass, the campaign has discharged its obligation and **a hammer is then the party's business** (WP06 §18.3).

## Red-Tusk staging

**At stage 1 the scouts take what will not be missed** — a hen, rope, a hurdle. **They do not take livestock until stage 2.** The dragged sheep are the Moorgaunt. **From session 2 both take animals, and that is when WP07 §12.1's diagnosis becomes hard.**

## Levelling
