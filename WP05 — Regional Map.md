# The Broken March — WP05: Regional Map

**Status:** Canonical campaign geography document
**Consumes:** `Campaign design doc.md`, `WP01 — Campaign State Model.md`, `WP02 — Greybank Village.md`, `WP03 — NPC Roster.md`, `WP04 — Factions.md`
**Feeds:** WP06, WP07, WP08–WP12, WP13, WP14, WP15
**Scope:** Eighteen zones of playable region — terrain, landmarks, hazards, faction territory, crossings, travel times in watches, secret locations, foraging, and how the ground itself changes as the clocks advance

WP02 ended four hundred yards outside Greybank with route labels and nothing behind them. WP05 is what is behind them: eighteen zones a party can walk into, each with a reason to go and a reason not to, priced in **watches** so that choosing one route over another costs something the DM can name. It answers the two questions earlier packages deliberately deferred — **how do carts cross when the bridge is gone** (WP01 §14.1) and **is there anywhere for sixty-one goblins to go** (WP04 §8.3) — and it fixes where the wardens walk and where the Red-Tusk sleep. It does not decide what is inside any ruin, and it writes no encounters.

---

# 1. Purpose and Boundaries

## 1.1 What WP05 owns

- **Eighteen zones**, Z1–Z18, with terrain, landmarks, character, and what is worth going there for.
- **Travel times in watches** for every leg, and the terrain rules that produce them.
- **The crossings of the Marrow**, and therefore **the bridge detour WP01 §14.1 defers to this package**.
- Faction territory: where the Laughing Hollow operates, where the Red-Tusk camp, what the Daggerford road actually is.
- **The Flight route** — whether a viable elsewhere exists for sixty-one goblins, which WP04 §8.3 scores at +1.
- Hazards **named and staged** on WP01 §12's ladder — not statted.
- Secret and discoverable locations, and exactly what reveals each.
- **How zones change as clocks advance.**
- Hunting and foraging, and their honest relationship to Food.
- DM and player map specifications.

## 1.2 What WP05 defers

## 1.3 Design decisions taken

Flagged for confirmation.

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Zones, not hexes.** Eighteen named places with stated travel times between them. | Design doc §27 permits either. Named zones are recallable at the table; hex coordinates are not, and this campaign wants players saying *"the Hollowway"* by session 3. |
| 2 | **A viable elsewhere for the Red-Tusk exists — Coldwell Vale — but the route runs through elven country.** | WP04 §8.3 scores Flight +1 if such a place exists. Making it real but geographically expensive keeps the branch live without making it cheap, and it creates the one situation where the Hollow and the Red-Tusk interact — exactly as WP04 §9.2 describes it. |
| 3 | **The bridge detour is Bythe Ford, two watches downstream — passable by cart, impassable in spate.** | WP01 §14.1 needs a number. A ford that fails in bad weather makes the bridge's loss variable rather than merely inconvenient. |
| 4 | **The uplands north of Greybank are campaign-original, not the High Moor.** | Design doc §3.1 cites the High Moor as justification for the region's character. The actual High Moor is far to the south-east. Tagged **[ADAPTED]** — same character, different ground. |
| 5 | **Illefarn waystones run across the whole region**, and three are already inside Greybank. | WP02 and WP03 established that the tower base, the shrine foundation and the burial-ground wall are the same jointless stone. Making that a **regional chain** turns a piece of scenery into a map the players can read, and hands WP06 a delivery system it did not have. |
| 6 | **Fenrow Holding is three households and no services.** | The campaign is deliberately small-scope (design doc §3.2). A second real settlement would split focus. Fenrow exists to be where evacuees go and where the ford is watched from. |
| 7 | **Getting lost is rare and the map is not hidden.** | Design doc §2.1: harsh, not arbitrary. The danger here is *what is in a zone*, never *whether you can find it*. |

# 3. The Region

## 3.1 Scale and shape

| | |
|---|---|
| **Extent** | Roughly **30 miles east–west, 25 north–south**, centred on Greybank |
| **Zones** | 18, of which the party can plausibly reach 12 in a four-session campaign |
| **Off-map anchor** | **Daggerford, ~40 miles south-east.** 2 days by cart, 2 days on foot, 1 hard day mounted |
| **The spine** | The **Marrow**, running west to east-south-east, past Greybank, to the Delimbiyr |
| **The road** | The Daggerford road: south-east from the bridge, through the Cut, gone from the map in 8 miles |

The region has three characters, and a party learns them in this order:

1. **The valley** — the Marrow's floor. Farmed, walked, safe by daylight. Zones Z1–Z6.
2. **The rise** — north and north-west. Stone, scrub, cairns, and the quarry. Zones Z2, Z7, Z11.
3. **The far country** — beyond a half-day in any direction. Zones Z12–Z18. **Nobody in Greybank goes there**, and four of the eighteen zones have never been visited by anyone now living in the village.

## 3.2 Lore placement

**[LORE]** Daggerford is a fortified settlement on the Delimbiyr, south of Waterdeep, and has long served as a base for expeditions into nearby wilderness. **[LORE]** The region carries remnants of ancient Illefarn, and later elven and dwarven activity. **[LORE]** Laughing Hollow and the forested country near Daggerford support elven and fey presence. **[LORE]** The High Moor and comparable wildernesses hold goblinoids, trolls, monsters, ruins, and isolated settlements.

**[ADAPTED]** The uplands north of Greybank — **Stonecrop Rise, Hagsfoot Moor, Kettlerock** — are campaign-original ground with the character design doc §3.1 attributes to the High Moor. **They are not the High Moor**, which lies far to the south-east. A DM should not import High Moor specifics here.

**[CAMPAIGN]** Everything named in §6 except Daggerford, the Delimbiyr and Laughing Hollow itself: the Marrow, Greybank, and all eighteen zone names.

**[CAMPAIGN]** The Hollowmarch is the outer edge of elven-watched country, not Laughing Hollow proper. The wardens of WP04 §5 come *from* the Hollow and *operate* in the Hollowmarch. **Laughing Hollow itself is off this map** and the party does not go there.

# 4. Travel

## 4.1 The time model

WP01 §10 fixes the watch at four hours and rates nearby regional travel at ¼–1 watch. This is the rate table that produces those numbers.

| Terrain | Miles per watch, on foot | Zones |
|---|---:|---|
| **Road** — the Daggerford road only | 10 | Z8 |
| **Track** — riverside, haul road, the Hollowway | 8 | Z2, Z5, Z9, Z12 |
| **Open** — fields, grazing, meadow | 7 | Z3, Z4, Z6 |
| **Rough** — upland, scrub, broken stone | 4 | Z7, Z10, Z11, Z16, Z17 |
| **Woodland or marsh** | 3 | Z6 interior, Z14, Z15, Z18 |

**Round every journey to the nearest ¼ watch.** WP01 §10.2: *"Do not repeatedly calculate ten-minute increments."*

## 4.3 Terrain rules

Four, and no more:

1. **A cart goes where a cart goes.** Road, track, and open ground only. Every ✗ in the matrix is a place stone, timber and trade goods cannot reach — which is what makes Bythe Ford matter.
2. **Mounted travel saves roughly a third**, and nothing on rough ground. **Greybank has two horses** (WP02 §11.1), hire only, at Rep ≥ +1. A party that secures them turns a four-day Daggerford round trip into two.
3. **Night travel doubles the time** in every terrain except road, and **triples it** in woodland and marsh. Zone hazards that are staged at night (§8) apply at their stated stage regardless.
4. **Weather.** Rain makes rough ground count as woodland. **Spate closes Bythe Ford** (§5.3) and raises the shoal. WP08–WP12 should use spate deliberately, not randomly.

## 4.4 Getting lost

**Rare, and the map is not hidden.** Design doc §2.1: the campaign is harsh, not arbitrary. The danger in a zone is what is in it, never whether it can be found.

| Situation | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Travelling to a zone the party has visited | **No check.** They know the way |
| Travelling to a zone described by a villager (§9.4) | **No check.** Add ¼ watch |
| Travelling to an unvisited zone with a landmark — Z7, Z10, Z14, Z2 | **No check.** All four are visible from a distance |
| Travelling to an unvisited zone without a landmark — Z11, Z16, Z17, Z18 | One survey check on arrival at the neighbouring zone. **Failure costs a watch, not the destination** |
| Travelling in fog, at night, or in spate | Add one watch. **No check** |

> **Never lose a party for a session.** A failed check costs a watch, and a watch is expensive enough — WP01 §10.3 advances threats and deadlines on it.

# 5. The Crossings

**Four ways across the Marrow, and a boat.** This section answers WP01 §14.1, which defers detour time to WP05.

## 5.1 The four crossings

| # | Crossing | Where | Carts? | Available |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Marrow Bridge** | Z1 Greybank | **Yes** | Always, while intact |
| 2 | **The shoal** | Z1, below the mill | No | **Late summer low water only.** Closed by the mill sluice, closed by spate, closed by autumn rain |
| 3 | **Anvil Gorge span** | Z10, 1½ watches west | **No — foot only, single file** | Always, and unpleasant always |
| 4 | **Bythe Ford** | Z12, 2 watches east | **Yes** | **Except in spate** |
| — | *The skiff* | Z1 wharf, WP02 G8 | No. **Four people** | While the skiff exists |

## 5.2 The bridge detour — the WP01 §14.1 answer

**For carts, stone, timber and trade goods, the detour is Bythe Ford.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Detour** | Greybank → Z12 Bythe Ford → cross → back along the south bank |
| **Added time, each way** | **+2 watches by cart** (1½ out, ½ back up the far bank) |
| **Added time, round trip** | **+4 watches — most of a working day** |
| **Fails when** | **Spate.** Two days of hard rain upstream, and the ford is impassable for 1–2 days |
| **Alternative for people only** | The skiff (4 at a time), or Anvil Gorge (+3 watches, single file, no cargo) |

**What each bridge state costs, in travel:**

| Bridge state | People | Carts and cargo | Trade effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intact** | — | — | — |
| **Damaged** | — | Unload, carry across, reload: **+½ watch per cart** | Trade cannot exceed 4 (WP01 §14.1) |
| **Disabled** | Rope and plank, +¼ watch | **Bythe Ford: +4 watches round trip** | Trade −1 |
| **Destroyed** | Skiff, 4 at a time, or Anvil Gorge | **Bythe Ford only: +4 watches round trip.** In spate, **nothing crosses** | Trade −1 immediately |
| **Destroyed, and in spate** | Skiff only | **Nothing.** Greybank is cut off from the south bank entirely | Replenishment stops (WP01 §5.10) |

> **The far bank is where Greybank's charcoal and timber come from** (WP02 G20, and Ib Sallow lives there). A destroyed bridge in spate does not merely slow trade — **it halves the smithy's output and stalls every §15 timber project**, and it strands one named NPC on the wrong side.

## 5.3 Spate

Two days of hard rain in the uplands, arriving a day later at Greybank. **Duration 1–2 days.**

| Effect | |
|---|---|
| Bythe Ford | **Impassable** |
| The shoal | **Impassable** |
| Anvil Gorge | Passable, and genuinely dangerous — the span is wet stone over 30 feet |
| The skiff | Passable by someone competent; **Denk Ottersby will refuse** to carry passengers |
| Z6 Reedwater | Floods. Becomes marsh throughout |
| Rough ground | Counts as woodland for travel (§4.3) |

**Telegraph it.** Rain in the north for two days, then the Marrow rising at the bridge piers. Corvin Ash reads the river better than anyone (WP03 §5.9) and will say so a day in advance.

## Z1 — Greybank · 0 · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**WP02 owns everything inside the boundary stones.** Listed here only so the matrix has an origin, and to record the three **Illefarn waystones** standing in the village: the tower's base course, the shrine's foundation, and the burial ground's north wall (§10).

---

## Z2 — The Pale · ¼ · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Haul road climbing to a stepped limestone cut. Benches, spoil heaps, a derrick, a bunkhouse.

**Why go** Stone Loads. The crew. **The aperture.**

**What's there** WP02 G14–G15 own the workings. WP05 adds the **surrounds**: the spoil heaps make broken ground for a quarter-mile downslope, and the haul-road cut (WP02 chokepoint 5) is the only quick route up.

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign.** Whatever the boards are holding. Nothing has come out.

**Faction** Kael Durrow's ground. Six Daggerford men sleep here, outside Greybank's ring.

**Changes when** Quarry expansion approved → the cut reaches the aperture. Hollow reaches **5** → wardens on the haul road, **quarry stops**. Instability ≥3 → WP06's business.

**Known by** Everyone. Kael Durrow best, and he has been inside (WP03 Secret S2).

---

## Z3 — The Home Fields · ¼ · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Flat arable north and east of the ring, hedged in places, no cover worth the name.

**Why go** Nothing, ordinarily. It is the ground an attack crosses.

**What's there** Three outlying farmsteads — H32 Orrick, H33 Hale, H34 Sallow-kin (WP02 §8.2). **All outside the ring.**

**Hazard** — **none at start.**

**Changes when** Red-Tusk **W4** → raids here. Farms → *Threatened*, then *Partially abandoned*, **Food −1 each** (WP02 §14.9). Greybank Reaction **4** → Orrick pulls the families inside the ring and the fields go unworked.

**Known by** Tam Orrick.

---

## Z4 — The Fold and West Grazing · ¼–½ · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Drystone folds and byres, then poorer grazing running west for two miles, then scrub.

**Why go** The flock. **And the far grazing is where a goblin encampment would go** — WP02 §15.8's site: outside the ring, upwind, out of sight of the Street.

**What's there** WP02 G18. Bessa Ruddock and two dogs. **260 yards and 26 rounds from the Green** — help does not arrive.

**Hazard** — **Stage 2, Incident.** WP02 designates this the campaign's first Level-2 threat location, and WP07 owns what is taking livestock. **Red-Tusk scouts from Z16 reach here in a night.**

**Faction** The proposed Red-Tusk site at Compact **C4**.

**Changes when** Red-Tusk **W4** → raids. Red-Tusk **C4** → negotiation over this exact ground, and **the farms bloc lives nearest it** (WP04 §9.5).

**Known by** Bessa Ruddock, and she has been saying so.

---

## Z5 — The Coppice and Charcoal Ground · ½, far bank · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Managed coppice on the south bank, two burning-clamps, a turf shelter, a stack yard.

**Why go** **Timber Loads and charcoal** — 1 Timber Load per day with four workers (WP02 §14). Without charcoal, the smithy halves.

**What's there** WP02 G20. Ib Sallow, alone, every night, **out of earshot of the bell**.

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign.** Something uses the coppice at night. Ib talks about it and is not listened to.

**Faction** **The wardens' first contact point.** WP04 §5.3 stage 2 puts a mark on a tree here.

**Changes when** Bridge lost → **Ib is on the wrong side** and timber stops. Hollow **2** → the mark appears.

**Known by** Ib Sallow, completely. Vadd Brune, the route only.

---

## Z6 — Reedwater · 1 · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** The Marrow spreads across low meadows east of Greybank. Reed beds, cattle-cropped grass, standing water in patches. **Floods in spate.**

**Why go** **Fishing and fowling** — the region's best foraging (§12). The riverside track to Bythe Ford runs through it.

**What's there** Old drainage ditches nobody maintains. Two derelict eel-traps. The track east.

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign.** Things come out of the reeds. WP07 owns what.

**Changes when** Spate → impassable marsh throughout, and the track to the ford is closed **on top of** the ford itself closing.

**Known by** Denk Ottersby.

---

## Z7 — Stonecrop Rise · 1 · **[CAMPAIGN, with ADAPTED Illefarn work]**

**Terrain** The upland shoulder above the quarry. Thin soil, limestone breaking through, wind-cropped thorn.

**Why go** **It overlooks everything.** From the Rise a party sees Greybank, the Pale, the Home Fields, both road approaches, and — in clear weather — smoke from Kettlerock.

**What's there** A scatter of low cairns, genuinely old and genuinely graves. **And among them, Waystone 4** (§10) — which is not a cairn, and which anyone who has looked at Greybank's tower will recognise.

**Hazard** — **none at start.** No water and no cover.

**Changes when** Red-Tusk **W5** → watched. Instability ≥3 → the waystone changes, and WP06 owns how.

**Known by** Kael Durrow, vaguely — quarrymen walk up here. **Nobody has connected the waystone to the tower.**

---

## Z8 — The Cut · 1 · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Where the Daggerford road climbs south-east out of the valley through a defile — 200 yards long, banks 20 feet, room for one cart.

**Why go** It is the road. Everything from Daggerford comes through here.

**What's there** A boundary stone, a drover's shelter, and the last place from which Greybank is visible.

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign.** It is the obvious ambush site in the region and every carter knows it.

**Faction** **Red-Tusk W5 closes this**, and closing it isolates Greybank from Daggerford entirely. Threll prices protection for it rather than providing it (WP04 §9.2).

**Changes when** Red-Tusk **W5** → watched, then closed. **Trade −1.** Daggerford clock stalls or reverses.

**Known by** Gedd Plank, intimately. He will describe it unprompted if asked about the road.

---

## Z9 — The Hollowway · 1, far bank · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** A sunken lane running west along the south bank, cut eight feet below the fields by centuries of use, roofed over with hazel.

**Why go** It is the fast, unseen route west — **and it is the route the wardens use.**

**What's there** Nothing built. The lane simply runs, and it runs toward the Hollowmarch.

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign.** Something has been using it that is not a warden and not a villager.

**Faction** **Laughing Hollow.** This is how nine elves reach Greybank's far bank without ever being seen.

**Changes when** Hollow **4** → wardens use it openly. Hollow **5** → it is watched, and a party on it is stopped.

**Known by** **Ib Sallow.** Nobody else. It does not appear on the player map (§14).

---

## Z10 — Anvil Gorge · 1½ · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Seven miles upstream the Marrow narrows into a limestone cleft thirty feet deep and twelve wide. The banks are rough going both sides.

**Why go** **The span** — a collapsed natural arch, four feet wide at its narrowest, wet with spray. **A foot crossing that works when nothing else does.**

**What's there** The span. Spray-slick stone. A drop into fast water in a slot the party could not climb out of.

**Hazard** — **Stage 2, Incident.** The crossing itself. WP07 need not stat it; falling is sufficient.

**Changes when** Spate → the span is still there and is genuinely dangerous. Bridge destroyed → **this becomes strategically important**, and the party will discover it is not a solution for cargo.

**Known by** **Corvin Ash** — he reads the river (WP03 §5.9). Ib Sallow knows of it. It is not on the player map.

---

## Z11 — Hagsfoot Moor · 2 · **[ADAPTED]**

**Terrain** Open upland scrub and peat north of Stonecrop Rise. No trees, no water, no shelter, and visibility for miles in every direction — **including of you.**

**Why go** It is the only route north to Kettlerock and the Old Delving.

**What's there** Nothing. That is the point. **Two watches of ground on which the party can be seen from the moment they enter it.**

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign.** Red-Tusk scouts cross it nightly. Whatever else is up here, WP07 owns.

**Faction** **Red-Tusk scouting ground.** They will know the party is coming before the party is halfway.

**Changes when** Red-Tusk **W4+** → crossing this is a running fight. Red-Tusk **C5** → they guide the party across it, which is what WP04 §10 means by *"guides through the moor edge."*

**Known by** **Nobody in Greybank has been past here.** Villagers describe it as "the moor" and stop.

---

## Z12 — Bythe Ford · 2 · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Twelve miles downstream the Marrow broadens over a gravel bed. Knee-deep in normal water, forty yards across.

**Why go** **★ It is the bridge detour.** The only cart crossing of the Marrow other than Greybank's bridge.

**What's there** The ford, a ruined tollhouse from when someone tried to charge for it, and a marker post cut with the water heights of bad years.

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign** normally. **Stage 3** in spate — the marker post is design doc §6's *explicit warning*, and a party that fords anyway has been told.

**Changes when** **Spate → impassable, 1–2 days.** Red-Tusk **W5** → watched, because interdicting the ford completes Greybank's isolation.

**Known by** Gedd Plank and Denk Ottersby. **Every carter in the region.**

## Z13 — Fenrow Holding · 2½ · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** Three farmsteads and a shared barn on the south bank half a mile beyond the ford. Nineteen people.

**Why go** **A roof that is not Greybank's**, and the only other people in the region who watch the ford.

**What's there** No store, no smith, no shrine, **no services of any kind.** They buy in Greybank and they know it.

> **Fenrow is deliberately thin** (§1.3). It is not a second Greybank and must not become one. It exists to be somewhere evacuees go, somewhere the party can sleep when Greybank is hostile, and a set of eyes on the ford.

**Hazard** — **none at start.**

**Changes when** Greybank Rep **−2 or −3** → **Fenrow will still take the party in**, for a while, because news travels at the speed of the ford. Greybank Reaction **6 evacuation** → Fenrow is where the first families go, and nineteen people cannot feed a hundred and forty-three.

**Known by** Everyone. Nobody thinks about it.

## Z14 — Wend's Barrow · 2 · **[CAMPAIGN, with ADAPTED Illefarn work]**

**Terrain** A long low mound in birch scrub, a mile south of the Daggerford road.

**Why go** Local belief says a chieftain is buried here and that digging is unlucky.

**What's there** **It is not a barrow.** Under the turf is **Waystone 5** (§10) — the same jointless stone as Greybank's tower, standing upright, and the mound is spoil heaped against it by people who did not build it.

**Hazard** — **Stage 1, Sign.** Nothing is buried here, and something about that is wrong.

**Changes when** Instability ≥3 → WP06 owns what a waystone does when the site wakes.

**Known by** Everyone knows the story. **Nobody has dug**, which is why the story survives.

## Z15 — The Hollowmarch · 3 · **[CAMPAIGN — outer edge of LORE elven country]**

**Terrain** Old woodland, thickening westward, with clearings that are too regular. Three watches from Greybank and a different world.

**Why go** **To find the elves** — and there is no other reason. Nothing here is for sale.

**What's there** **Waystone 6.** Warden sign, for those who can read it. Paths that go where they were meant to go.

**Hazard** — **Stage 2, Incident** for anyone unwelcome. WP07 owns the wardens; **WP04 §5.2 forbids them fighting fair.**

**Changes when** Hollow **5** → wards across the approaches, and **Hollow Rep decides whether the party passes at all** (WP01 §4.3).

**Known by** **Nobody in Greybank.** Halvard Vell knows there were elves once and cannot tell you which day it is.

## Z16 — Kettlerock · 3 · **[CAMPAIGN]**

**Terrain** A bowl of broken ground north-west — shattered limestone, thorn, and a spring that does not fail. Defensible, miserable, and the best available.

**Why go** **The Red-Tusk are here.** Sixty-one of them.

**What's there** WP04 §6.1's band: 22 fighters, 39 who cannot fight, 11 wounded. Ghesh, Orbal, and Orbal's knotted cords.

**Hazard** — **Stage 2, Incident** at Red-Tusk Rep −1. **Stage 4, Response** on the War branch.

**Faction** **Red-Tusk.** Scouts from here reach Z4 in a night, which is precisely what WP04 §6.1 required of this placement.

## Z17 — The Old Delving · 4 · **[CAMPAIGN surface / WP06 interior]**

**Terrain** A worked cleft in the uplands beyond Kettlerock. Cut stone at the entrance, spoil that is centuries old, and ground that is wrong underfoot in a way the party will notice before they can say why.

**Why go** **This is where the Red-Tusk lived, and what they ran from is here.**

**What's there** **WP05 fixes the place and stops at the threshold.** The approach: a cut ramp, a lintel of the same jointless stone, and a doorway the goblins piled shut from the outside and did not stay to watch.

**Hazard** — **Stage 3, Escalation**, and the only Stage 3 on the map at campaign start. Design doc §6's Level 3 obligation is met by the physical evidence: a settlement abandoned in a hurry, piled shut from outside.

**Faction** Red-Tusk, formerly. **The Laughing Hollow watches this and will intervene if the party opens it** — Hollow +1 minimum.

**Changes when** Instability rises. WP06 owns all of it.

**Known by** **Ghesh and Orbal**, and their accounts differ. **Both are testimony, not truth** (WP04 §16).

## Z18 — Coldwell Vale · 5, via Z15 only · **[CAMPAIGN]**

> **And the only route runs through Z15, the Hollowmarch.** Sixty-one goblins migrating through elven-watched country is exactly the encounter WP04 §9.2 describes: *"the Hollow lets them pass and watches carefully — they are leaving; what are they leaving because of?"*

**Changes when** Red-Tusk **F5** → they are moving through Z15 toward it. **F6** → they are there.

**Known by** **Orbal.** It is in the cords. That is why the Flight branch exists at all.

# 7. Faction Territory

## 7.1 Who is where

| Faction | Home | Operates in | Reaches Greybank in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greybank** | Z1 | Z2–Z6, and Z8 and Z12 by cart. **Nothing else** | — |
| **Laughing Hollow** | Off-map, via Z15 | Z15, Z9, Z5, Z14, and the Pale when they choose | **1–2 watches**, using the Hollowway |
| **Red-Tusk** | Z16 | Z11, Z7, Z4, Z17. Scouts reach Z4 nightly | **3 watches** for the band; **one night** for scouts |
| **Daggerford** | Off-map | Z8 and the road. **Malk goes wherever the artifacts are** | 2 days, always, without exception |

> **The map's central asymmetry:** Greybank knows six zones. The Red-Tusk know nine. The wardens know all eighteen. **Nobody in the village has been past Hagsfoot Moor**, and four zones — Z15, Z16, Z17, Z18 — have never been visited by anyone now living in Greybank.

## 7.2 The Laughing Hollow's approach

WP04 §5.1 assumed the wardens were 1–2 watches from the far bank. They are, and this is how:

**Z15 Hollowmarch → Z9 the Hollowway → Z5 the coppice → the far bank.** Two watches, entirely under cover, and it does not cross a single field a villager works.

This is why **nobody in Greybank has seen an elf in a generation** despite the wardens being there the whole time — and why **Ib Sallow** (Z5, alone, every night) is the villager most likely to meet one first, exactly as WP04 §5.3 states.

## 7.3 The Red-Tusk's reach

**Z16 Kettlerock → Z11 Hagsfoot → Z7 Stonecrop Rise → Z4 the fold.** Three watches for the band with its thirty-nine non-combatants; **one night for two or three scouts**, which is what WP04 §6.3's stage 1 describes.

**W5 interdiction — the WP04 handoff answer.** On the War branch at stage 5, the Red-Tusk close **two** routes and only two:

| Closed | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Z8 The Cut** | The Daggerford road. **Trade −1.** Threll prices protection rather than providing it |
| **Z12 Bythe Ford** | The detour. **If the bridge is also down, Greybank has no cart route at all** |

They do **not** close Z15 or Z9 — they will not go into elven country (WP04 §11). **The far bank stays open**, which means Ib Sallow's timber and charcoal keep coming and the party keeps a route west. That gap is deliberate and it is the seam a clever party will find.

## 7.4 The Flight route

**Z16 Kettlerock → Z11 Hagsfoot → west along the uplands → Z15 the Hollowmarch → Z18 Coldwell Vale.**

Roughly **four days** for sixty-one goblins moving at the pace of thirty-nine who cannot fight.

**It goes through elven country, and there is no other way.** WP04 §9.2 already fixed what happens: the Hollow lets them pass and watches carefully. WP05 adds that the passage takes **two days inside Z15**, during which the wardens have every opportunity to learn what the Red-Tusk are running from — which is the one thing the elves actively want to know.

> **This is the Flight branch's real cost and its real consequence.** The goblins get away. The Laughing Hollow gets two days of interviews. And Greybank gets a gap where its buffer used to be.

# 10. The Illefarn Waystones

**[ADAPTED — Illefarn masonry · CAMPAIGN placement]**

Six stones, all of the same jointless pale material, all obviously not local work. **Three of them stand inside Greybank**, where the village has built around them for ninety years without remarking on it.

| # | Where | Condition | Noticed by villagers? |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Z1 — the tower's lower 22 feet (WP02 G13) | Standing, built upon | **No.** "The old tower" |
| **W2** | Z1 — the shrine's foundation course (WP02 G9) | Standing, built upon | Yorrin Fell knows it is not villager work and is untroubled |
| **W3** | Z1 — the burial ground's north wall (WP02 G19) | Standing | **No** |
| **W4** | Z7 Stonecrop Rise | Standing among genuine cairns | Taken for a cairn |
| **W5** | Z14 Wend's Barrow | Standing, **buried under heaped spoil** | Taken for a grave |
| **W6** | Z15 The Hollowmarch | Standing, clear, and tended | **The wardens keep it clear** |

**What is observable, and therefore what WP05 owns:**

- All six are **the same stone**, cut the same way, with **no joints** — and Ordga Brune has already said out loud, twice, that no chisel she knows produces it (WP03 §5.3).
- Plotted on a map, they **form an arc** across the region, open to the north-west.
- **The two entrances sit at the arc's focus and its end**: the quarry aperture (Z2) and the Old Delving (Z17). Their lintels are the same stone. **They are not waystones — they are doors, and the stones stand around them.**
- W6 is the only one anybody maintains.

**What WP05 does not own:** what they are for, what they do, whether they still work, what happens to them as Instability rises, and whether the arc is a boundary, a ward, a route, or a warning. **All of it is WP06's.**

> **Why this matters to WP05.** It turns a regional map into something a party can *read*. Four zones and three village landmarks become one legible pattern, discoverable with no magic and no check — just a party that walks, looks, and asks a dwarf smith why the stone is wrong. Design doc §2.2 asks that difficult problems have discoverable structure; this is the region's.

# 12. Hunting, Foraging and Food

**WP01 §3.1 is explicit: emergency hunting success *prevents a decline* rather than producing permanent surplus.** WP05 does not soften this.

## 12.1 What foraging can and cannot do

| Can | Cannot |
|---|---|
| Feed the party in the field, indefinitely, in the right zones | **Raise village Food. Ever** |
| **Prevent** a scheduled Food decline, once, with a real effort | Replace the harvest, the mill, or the granary |
| Supply a specific need — herbs for Maud, fish for a household | Substitute for Trade |

## 12.2 By zone

| Zone | Foraging | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Z6 Reedwater** | **Best in the region.** Fish, eels, fowl | Denk Ottersby's ground. Floods in spate |
| Z5 Coppice | Good — game, nuts, and **Maud's herbs** | Ib Sallow knows every stand |
| Z4 West Grazing | Poor. It is grazed | |
| Z3 Home Fields | Nothing not already someone's | **Taking from the fields is theft** (WP01 §7) |
| Z7, Z11 Uplands | Thin. Hare, and little else. **No water on Hagsfoot** | |
| Z9 Hollowway | Adequate | |
| Z14, Z15 Woodland | Good, and Z15 is **not yours to forage** — the wardens notice |
| Z16, Z17 | **The Red-Tusk have already stripped it.** This is why their food fails | The clearest physical evidence of §6's Z16 entry |

> **Z16's stripped ground is a Level 1 signal** (design doc §6). A party that scouts Kettlerock and notices there is nothing left to eat for two miles has learned why the Strategic Clock advances on its own, and has been handed the Compact branch's whole argument without anyone explaining it.

## 12.3 The one Food ruling

A party may **prevent one scheduled Food decline** by spending **a full watch hunting or fishing in Z5 or Z6** with a plausible means, and delivering it to the village. This works **once per decline**, not repeatedly, and it does not stack.

Everything else about Food remains WP01 §3.1's.

# 15. Worked Examples

---

## 15.1 Example A — The bridge is gone and the stone must move

**Situation.** Session 3. The bridge is **DESTROYED** (WP02 Example C). Trade is 2. The quarry has four Stone Loads on the bench and Kael Durrow wants them in Daggerford.

### A1. The options, priced

| Route | Time | Works? |
|---|---|---|
| The bridge | — | Gone |
| **Bythe Ford** | **+4 watches round trip** (§5.2) | **Yes** |
| Anvil Gorge | 1½ watches | **No.** Foot only, single file, no cargo |
| The skiff | — | **No.** Four people, no stone |

**Bythe Ford is the only answer**, and Gedd Plank will say so unprompted the moment the bridge goes down (§9, discovery 6).

### A2. Then it rains

Two days of hard rain in the uplands. Corvin Ash reads the river and says so a day ahead (§5.3).

> **Bythe Ford: impassable. The shoal: closed. The bridge: gone.**
>
> **Greybank has no cart route to anywhere, for 1–2 days.**

### A3. What that actually costs

| System | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Trade | Already 2. **No further automatic fall** | WP01 §16 — no cascade |
| Replenishment | **Stops.** Nothing scheduled arrives | WP01 §5.10 |
| The far bank | Cut off. **Ib Sallow is on the wrong side** | §5.2 |
| Charcoal | Stops arriving. **Smithy output halves** | WP02 G20 |
| Timber | Stops. **Every §15 timber project stalls** | WP02 §14 |
| Z6 Reedwater | Flooded — the best foraging in the region is gone too | §12.2 |

**Nothing rolled.** A bridge the party let fall, plus weather that was telegraphed a day in advance, and the village's whole logistical position collapses for two days.

### A4. What the party can do

Repair the bridge to **Disabled** — 8 Timber, 10 workers, 4 days, 50 gp (WP02 §14.1) — **except the timber is on the far bank.** They need the skiff, four at a time, to fetch their own building material.

That is the shape of a good problem: **every solution needs a crossing, and the crossings are the problem.**

---

## 15.2 Example B — Scouting Kettlerock first

**Situation.** Session 2. The party knows goblins are taking livestock at the fold. They decide to find them before deciding what to do.

### B1. The journey

**Z1 → Z2 → Z7 → Z11 → Z16.** ¼ + ¾ + 1 + 1 = **3 watches**, and §4.4 says no check — they can follow the haul road up and the ground north from there.

**Z11 Hagsfoot costs them.** Two watches of open upland with no cover: **the Red-Tusk scouts see them coming** (§6, Z11). By the time they reach the rim of Kettlerock, they are expected.

### B2. What they see from the rim

Not a warband. **Sixty-one goblins**: 22 who can fight, **39 who cannot**, 11 wounded and lying where they were put down. A spring. Thorn. And for two miles in every direction, **ground stripped of anything edible** (§12.2).

### B3. What that changes

| Before scouting | After scouting |
|---|---|
| Goblins are taking livestock | **Goblins are starving, and there are children** |
| Kill them or drive them off | The Compact branch's entire argument, delivered without a word |
| The envoy will look like a trick | **The envoy will look like exactly what it is** |

**No mechanical change. No reputation shift. No clock movement.** The party simply knows something true, and per WP04 §8.3 they now have every reason to reach Orbal by name — which is worth **+1** in the branch count, and which they would never have thought to do.

### B4. And the cost

Three watches out, three back, and they were seen. **Six watches is a day and a half.** In that time the fold is raided again — Ghesh does not stop while Orbal talks — and Bessa Ruddock loses more of the flock: **Food −1**.

> **The information was worth it and it was not free.** That is design doc §16's prioritization working exactly as intended, and §9's discovery 7 doing what it was placed to do.

## 15.3 Example C — Three roads to the Hollowmarch

**Situation.** Session 3. The party has learned Nethra exists and wants to find her. Z15 is **3 watches** away by the matrix. But there are three ways, and the matrix number is only one of them.

| Route | Time | Cost | Risk |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **The Hollowway** — Z5 → Z9 → Z15 | **3 watches** | Requires **knowing it exists** — Ib Sallow, or the far bank walked | **The wardens' own road.** At Hollow ≤3 they are seen and let through. At Hollow 5 they are stopped at the border |
| **The road, then south** — Z8 → Z14 → west through woodland | **5 watches** | Two extra watches. **Passes Wend's Barrow**, which is discovery 5 | Safe, slow, and the party arrives visibly from the wrong direction |
| **Straight across the fields and the far bank** | **4 watches** | Needs a crossing: skiff, or the bridge | Woodland travel at 3 mi/watch. **Nobody knows the way** — §4.4's one survey check |

**Choosing the Hollowway costs nothing and requires having talked to Ib Sallow** — a man everyone in Greybank finds exhausting. **Choosing the road costs two watches and hands them a waystone.** Choosing to push straight across costs a check and arrives without either.

> **Three routes, three genuinely different prices.** This is WP05's judging criterion, and every leg in §4.2 should be able to produce a table like this.

## 15.4 Example D — The chain

**Situation.** Somewhere in session 3, a player says *"wait — that's the same stone as the tower."*

### D1. How they got there

Any two of these, and the pattern is visible:

- **Ordga Brune** has already said the quarry's old stone is cut in a way no chisel produces (WP03 §5.3). She will say it again to anyone who asks about metal.
- The tower's lower 22 feet, the shrine's foundation, and the burial ground's north wall are **all in Greybank** and all the same (WP02).
- **Stonecrop Rise** is one watch away and its waystone sits among cairns that are obviously different.
- **Wend's Barrow** is not a barrow.

### D2. What they can work out with no magic and no check

Six stones. An **arc**, open to the north-west. And **at its focus and its end, the two doors** — the quarry aperture and the Old Delving, sharing the same lintel stone.

The Red-Tusk fled from one of them. Greybank has been quarrying toward the other for two years.

### D3. What the DM says

**Confirm the pattern. Refuse the meaning.**

The stones are real, the arc is real, the doors are real. **What the arc is *for* is WP06's**, and no NPC in the region will tell them — Ordga does not know, Yorrin is untroubled, Halvard remembers a different century, and **Vaerelin Ossuine knows exactly and will not say below stage 6** (WP04 §5.5).

> The players have solved a genuine regional puzzle using nothing but walking and looking, and been handed a larger question. That is the correct reward.

# 16. Edge Cases and Adjudication Guidance

## 16.1 The party leaves the region

Legitimate. Daggerford is 2 days each way and the campaign has a road. **Four days round trip** is the cost, and per WP01 §10.3 every deadline, investigation and clock advances across it.

**Do not build Daggerford.** It stays off-map. What happens there is reported, purchased, or hired — never played out at length.

## 16.2 The party maps everything in session 1

They cannot. Eighteen zones at the §4.2 times is more than thirty watches of travel, and the campaign is four sessions. **Let them try** — a party that spends session 1 walking has learned the region and lost the fold, and both halves of that are real.

## 16.3 The party travels at night constantly

§4.3: double time, triple in woodland and marsh. Hazards apply at their stated stage regardless. **Night travel is not forbidden and is rarely worth it** — the arithmetic discourages it without a rule.

The exception is **Hagsfoot** (Z11): crossing at night halves the chance of being seen by Red-Tusk scouts and doubles the crossing to four watches. That is a real trade and the party should be allowed to find it.

## 16.4 Weather

**Spate is a DM tool, not a random table.** Use it when the campaign needs the crossings to matter — typically once, in session 3, when the party has come to rely on the ford. Telegraph it a day ahead through Corvin Ash. **Do not roll for it.** WP01 §17: roll only where uncertainty is meaningful.

## 16.5 The party tries to move cargo through Anvil Gorge

It does not work, and they should be allowed to discover why: four feet of wet stone over a thirty-foot drop, single file. **A person crosses. A Stone Load does not.**

Say this before they attempt it — the physical facts are visible on arrival, and design doc §29 requires the world not lie about basic reality.

## 16.6 The party tries to hold the Cut

**Two hundred yards, banks twenty feet, room for one cart.** It is the best defensive ground in the region and holding it is a genuinely good idea.

It is also **eight miles from Greybank** — one watch — which means holding the Cut means not being in the village. WP07 owns the fight; WP05 notes that whoever holds Z8 is not defending Z1.

## 16.7 Magical travel

If the party acquires a way to move faster, **let it work.** It converts their scarcest resource — watches — into their most abundant, and that is a legitimate reward.

Two things it does not solve: **carts still cannot cross the Marrow** without a bridge or a ford, and **the wardens are still three watches ahead of everyone** in their own country.

## 16.8 A zone the party never visits

Per design doc §22, unused material stays unused. **Four zones are expected to go unvisited in most campaigns** — Z13, Z14, Z18, and usually Z10. They are on the map so the region has edges, not so the party will tour them.

## 16.9 The party asks a villager to guide them

| Who | Will go to | Will not |
|---|---|---|
| **Ib Sallow** | Z5, Z9, and reluctantly Z15's edge | Anywhere north |
| **Gedd Plank** | Z8, Z12, Z13, Daggerford | Off the road, ever |
| **Denk Ottersby** | Z6, Z12 by water | Away from the river |
| **Bessa Ruddock** | Z4, Z3 | Past the grazing |
| **Corvin Ash** | The riverbank, as far as Z10 | Overnight |
| **Anybody** | — | **The Old Delving, the aperture, or past Hagsfoot** (WP03 §14.8) |

## 16.10 The players want to see the map

Give them the §14 player map in session 1, unasked. Let them annotate it. **Never draw on it for them**, and never print a travel time on it — the whole apparatus of §4 exists so that watches are something the party learns to fear rather than read off a chart.

# 17. Final Canonical WP05 Rules

## The region

**Eighteen zones, Z1–Z18**, roughly 30 miles by 25, centred on Greybank. **Daggerford is ~40 miles south-east and stays off the map** — 2 days by cart or on foot, 1 hard day mounted.

## Travel

**Watches, per WP01 §10.** Road 10 miles per watch, track 8, open 7, rough 4, woodland and marsh 3. Round to the nearest ¼ watch. **Night doubles, and triples in woodland or marsh.** Mounted saves about a third and nothing on rough ground.

**Carts go on road, track and open ground only.** Every ✗ in §4.2 is somewhere stone, timber and trade cannot reach.

## Getting lost

**Rare, and the map is not hidden.** A visited zone, a described zone, or a zone with a visible landmark needs no check. Failure costs **a watch, never the destination.**

## The crossings

**Four, plus a boat:** the bridge; **the shoal** (late summer only); **Anvil Gorge** (foot, single file, no cargo); **Bythe Ford** (cart — **the detour**, +4 watches round trip, **impassable in spate**); and the skiff, four at a time.

**This answers WP01 §14.1.** A bridge destroyed *and* a spate leaves Greybank with **no cart route at all** for 1–2 days, strands Ib Sallow on the far bank, halves the smithy, and stalls every timber project.

## Faction ground

**Laughing Hollow:** Z15, reaching the far bank in 1–2 watches by the Hollowway (Z9) — under cover the whole way, which is why nobody has seen an elf in a generation.
**Red-Tusk:** Z16 Kettlerock, 61 goblins; scouts reach the fold in a night; the band is 3 watches out.
**W5 interdiction closes exactly two routes — Z8 the Cut and Z12 the ford.** They will not enter elven country, so **the far bank stays open.**
**Daggerford:** the road, and two days, always.

## The Flight route

**A viable elsewhere exists — Coldwell Vale (Z18) — and WP04 §8.3's +1 applies.** The only route runs through **Z15, elven country**, taking four days with two of them inside the Hollowmarch. The goblins get away; the wardens get two days to learn what they ran from.

## The waystones

**Six**, all the same jointless stone, **three of them standing inside Greybank** unremarked. Plotted, they form an **arc open to the north-west**, with the two doors — the quarry aperture and the Old Delving — at its focus and its end. **Discoverable by walking, looking, and asking Ordga Brune. WP06 owns what it means.**

## Hazards

Named and staged on WP01 §12's ladder, never statted. **Z17 the Old Delving is the only Stage 3 on the map at start.** Two hazards need no monster: **Anvil Gorge, and Bythe Ford in spate.**

## Foraging

**Cannot raise village Food, ever** (WP01 §3.1). It can feed the party, and it can **prevent one scheduled decline**, once, at the cost of a full watch in Z5 or Z6. **Kettlerock's stripped ground is the physical evidence for why the Red-Tusk clock advances on its own.**

## Fenrow

**Three households, nineteen people, no services.** Not a second Greybank and must never become one.

## Deferred, unchanged

Inside the aperture and the Old Delving → **WP06**. Encounters and stat blocks → **WP07**. Inside the boundary stones → **WP02**. Faction behaviour → **WP04**. Daggerford itself → **nobody. It stays off the map.**

# WP05 Completion Criterion

**The judging test:** does choosing route A over route B cost or risk something concrete? §15.3 puts three roads to the same destination side by side with three different prices; every leg in §4.2 should support the same treatment.

WP05 is complete when later packages can answer questions like these without inventing geography:

- The bridge is destroyed. How does a cart reach Daggerford, how much longer does it take, and when does that stop working?
- How long to walk to the goblin camp, and will they know you are coming?
- Where do the elves come from, and why has nobody in Greybank seen one?
- Is there anywhere for sixty-one goblins to go, and what do they have to cross to get there?
- Which two routes does a Red-Tusk war footing close, and which one stays open?
- What does a party see from the rim of Kettlerock that changes what the encounter is?
- Where are the Illefarn waystones, and what can a party work out from them without magic?
- What happens to the region when it rains for two days?
- Which villager can describe which zone, and where does every one of them refuse to go?
- Where would Greybank evacuate to, and what is waiting there?
- How far is the Old Delving, and what does the approach look like before you go in?

# Handoffs

## To WP06 — Illefarn Dungeon

**Fixed by WP05, and yours to fill:**

- **Two entrances.** The quarry aperture (Z2, ¼ watch from Greybank, boarded, boards loose) and **the Old Delving** (Z17, **4 watches**, in the uplands beyond Kettlerock, **piled shut from the outside** by goblins who did not stay to watch). Their lintels are the same stone.
- **Six waystones** (§10), forming an arc open to the north-west with the two doors at its focus and its end. **WP05 owns that they exist, that they are identical, and that the pattern is discoverable. You own what any of it means** — including what happens to them as Instability rises, which §11 flags at Instability 2 and 3+ without saying what.
- **What emptied the Old Delving.** WP04 gave you Ghesh's and Orbal's accounts as testimony; WP05 gives you the physical evidence at the threshold. The truth is entirely yours.
- **What fills the gap if the Red-Tusk take Flight** and Kettlerock empties in session 4.

**Bound by:** Z17 is **Stage 3 — Escalation** at campaign start and is the only Stage 3 on the map. The approach must satisfy design doc §6's Level 3 obligation before a party goes in, and the physical evidence at the threshold already does.

## To WP07 — Encounters

**Owed:** the hazards named in §8 — every one has a zone, a stage and a reason to be there, and none has a stat block. Specifically: whatever takes livestock at **Z4** (the campaign's first Level-2 warning), whatever uses **Z5** at night, whatever is in **Z6**'s reeds, and the wardens in **Z15**.

**Bound by:** WP04 §5.2 — **the Hollow does not fight fair and does not fight pitched battles.** Red-Tusk probes before W4 are survivable by both sides. **Two hazards need no creature at all**: Anvil Gorge and Bythe Ford in spate.

**Useful ground:** the Cut (Z8) is the region's best defensive terrain and is **one watch from Greybank** — holding it means not being in the village. Hagsfoot (Z11) is two watches of no cover in either direction.

## To WP08–WP12 — Sessions

- **Session 1** should establish Z2, Z4 and Z5 — the three zones the party will care about — and print the Cut on the player map by having Gedd Plank talk about the road.
- **The fold (Z4) is the shared door** into both the Red-Tusk thread and WP02's first Level-2 warning.
- **Spate belongs in session 3**, once, telegraphed a day ahead through Corvin Ash, when the party has come to rely on Bythe Ford. **Do not roll for it.**
- **§15.2 is the model for rewarding scouting.** A party that looks at Kettlerock before deciding gets the Compact argument for free and pays a day and a half for it.
- WP11's finale should account for **which zones the party actually mapped.** A party that never went west has no Hollowmarch ending available.

## To WP13 — Items

**Where things are found:** the two entrances (WP06's business), and **the waystones**, which are not lootable and should not become so. **The Laughing Hollow will take artifacts back from anywhere at stage 4+** — including from a party camped in Z15, where they are three watches from help and inside the wardens' own country.

## To WP14 — Handouts

**Owed:** the **player exploration map** to §14's specification — starting nearly blank west of the river, with no travel times printed. **Bythe Ford's marker post**, cut with the water heights of bad years, as a physical prop and an explicit warning. **Orbal's cords** already owed by WP04 — they contain Coldwell Vale.

## To WP15 — Visual Assets

**Owed:** the DM regional map to §13 and the player map to §14. Note DM requirement 4 — **the waystones must be a single distinct symbol** so the arc reads at a glance on the DM's copy and appears nowhere on the player's.
