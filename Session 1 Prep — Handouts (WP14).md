# 1. The Rumour Cards

**Twelve. Print the front text only — everything else is on your run-sheet.** Deal them face up as they come out of conversation. **Ignored cards stay on the table across sessions.**

Suggested stock: business-card size, or index cards cut in half. Number them small in a corner so you can find them.

# 2. Bessa Ruddock's Tally Slate

**The handout that carries the campaign's first real puzzle, and it looks like housekeeping.**

**Physical:** a piece of dark slate or grey card, chalk or white pencil, in a shepherd's hand — cramped, practical, no punctuation. She has used the same slate for years, so **wipe-marks and ghosts of older counts should be visible.**

**Give it to them when Bessa brings the ewe in at Dawn on Day 2.** She hands it over without ceremony because it is the fastest way to answer the question.

```
        EWES  47  ..  46  ..  45 .. 44  .. 43

   moon turnd      47
   nxt day         47
   3rd             47
   4th        -1   46      over the wall  drag
   5th             46
   6th             46
   7th             46
   8th        -1   45      same  drag  west
   9th             45
   10th            45      DOGS WULD NOT GO OUT
   11th            45      DOGS AGAIN.  hen gone.  rope gone
   12th       -1   44      same
   13th            44
   14th            44
   15th            44
   16th       -1   43      same.  hurdle moved  put bak wrong

                          43 counted twice
```

**What it says, to a player who reads dates:** **one, then three quiet nights, then one.** Four losses, all on the same rhythm.

**What it also says, and almost nobody notices in session 1:** the **hen, the rope and the hurdle** are on nights 11 and 16 — **and one of those is a night with no ewe lost.** Two different things are visiting this fold.

**Slightly misleading, on purpose:** the dogs entry sits next to a loss, which reads as one cause. It is not.

# 3. The Crossing Book — Denk Ottersby's page

**Given in the first ten minutes of play**, when he writes the party in. Let them see the page.

**Physical:** a ruled page, several hands over the years, ink of different ages. Cheap paper, water-marked at one corner.

```
   ...
   waggon  D.ford  2 whl   Plank            4 cp
   waggon  D.ford  4 whl   Plank            8 cp
   cart    up-vale 2 whl   Orrick           4 cp
   waggon  D.ford  4 whl   Plank            8 cp
   -------------------------------------------------
   [ three months of this page are much thinner
     than the rest. Fewer entries, and one long
     stretch with nothing at all across a season
     when the cart ran every six days. ]
   -------------------------------------------------
   waggon  D.ford  4 whl   Plank            8 cp
   cart    D.ford  2 whl   -----            4 cp
   waggon  D.ford  4 whl   Plank            8 cp
   ...
   ----  THIS DAY  ----
   ____________________   on foot   5 persons   no charge
```

**True:** three months ago Denk was paid four silver **not to write down a wagon.** He does not know whose it was and did not look. He has thought about it every day since.

**If asked about it:** he understates everything. *"Bit of a quiet season."* He is a poor liar and he is not trying very hard.

> **This resolves into nothing** and that is deliberate. A village should contain things that do not pay off.

# 4. The Watch-Glass Image

**Given in Stage I, S4, if the party looks into the basin.**

**Physical:** a single card or half-sheet. **No text of any kind.** Do not title it. Do not label the compass. Hand it over and say nothing.

**The image:**

- **Six rings of soft light**, arranged in an **arc that opens toward the upper-left of the card** — not a circle, an incomplete curve.
- **Three of the six are dark** — visible as rings, but unlit. **The three dark ones are the three at the open end of the arc**, on the upper-left side.
- Two small marks sit apart from the ring line: one at the **focus of the arc**, one at its **far end**. Nothing indicates what they are.
- No labels, no north arrow, no scale. **The card should be slightly beautiful and completely uninformative.**

**Say only what it looks like.** If a player asks what it means, describe it again, precisely, and let them theorise.

**If they show it to a villager:** nobody in Greybank knows. **Ordga Brune will look at it for a long time and say the stone is the same.** That is the most any villager can give them.

> **A player who draws this on a napkin has drawn the campaign.** Three of the six rings are inside Greybank, and the dark ones face north-west.
